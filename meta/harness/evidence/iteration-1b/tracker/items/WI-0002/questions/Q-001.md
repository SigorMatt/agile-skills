## Context

The idea says an expense is "paid by someone and shared by some or all". "Shared by" fixes *who*
is on the hook but not *in what proportion*. Reading it the narrow way, everyone named splits the
cost equally. Reading it the broad way, the tool also has to accept "Alice's half of the bill was
40 and Bob's was 12" — which is a different record type, a different command shape, and a
different set of validity rules (do the parts have to sum to the total?).

I cannot resolve this from anything written down: it is a fact about how your group actually
splits things, and it decides what data WI-0002 stores. Getting it wrong in the narrow direction
means the tool cannot express the cases you care about; getting it wrong in the broad direction
means every expense entry carries syntax nobody uses.

## Question

When an expense is shared, is it always split equally among the people named — or must the tool
also accept unequal shares, where each sharer's portion is stated explicitly?

## Options considered

- **A — Equal split only.** An expense names an amount, a payer, and a set of sharers; each
  sharer owes an equal fraction. Consequence: simplest record and simplest command line. Covers
  meals, taxis and groceries, which is most of what a friend group shares. Cannot express "I only
  had the starter".
- **B — Equal by default, explicit amounts when given.** Sharers may be named plainly (equal
  split) or with an amount each. If amounts are given, they must sum to the total or the entry is
  rejected. Consequence: covers both, at the cost of a validity rule and a more complicated
  command surface. Perhaps 40% more work on WI-0002 and a new failure mode to specify.
- **C — Weights or percentages.** Sharers carry a weight ("Alice 2, Bob 1"). Consequence:
  expresses "Alice ate twice as much" without arithmetic, but percentages that do not divide
  evenly reintroduce the rounding problem in a worse form, and it is the least familiar of the
  three.
- **Recommendation:** **A** for a first version. It matches the plain reading of what you said,
  and B is a strictly additive change later — an expense recorded under A stays valid under B.
  Pick B now only if unequal splits are common enough in your group that a tool without them
  would not get used.

## Answer

[human] Usually equal, but sometimes someone only had part of it and wants to put in a different amount — so it needs to handle both.

That settles the requirement — option A, equal-split-only, is out — but not the shape. The
question's option B required that *every* sharer carry an explicit amount as soon as any of them
did. Read against what the human described, one person who "only had part of it" putting in a
different amount, B would make the common case worse than A: to record "Bob only had a starter, 5",
the user would have to compute and type everyone else's share, which is the arithmetic the tool
exists to do. It would also contradict "usually equal", since stating one amount would close off
the equal path.

**Decision: the mixed form**, recorded as `ADR-0002`. Any subset of sharers may carry an explicit
amount; those sharers owe exactly that; whatever is left of the total is divided equally among the
sharers that carry none. Stating nothing for anybody is the plain equal split and costs no extra
syntax.

The requirement is the human's; the shape is the architect's, and `ADR-0002` says which is which.

`ADR-0002` also fixes what makes an entry invalid, because the mixed form admits two failures the
equal-only form did not: stated amounts that overshoot the total, and stated amounts that undershoot
it with nobody left to absorb the remainder. Both are refused per `ADR-0001` — stated message on
standard error, non-zero exit, nothing recorded. Two cases are accepted deliberately and are named
in the ADR: the payer need not be a sharer, and a sharer whose remainder works out to zero owes
zero rather than triggering an error.

The concrete syntax is not decided here. `refine` pins it on this item.
