## Context

`ADR-0001` settled that the tool is a set of one-shot subcommands — `python3 -m expenses
<subcommand> [arguments]` — and deliberately left the *names* of the subcommands to the item that
introduces each one, to be pinned during refinement. This is that moment for the two this item
introduces: one that adds a person, one that lists the group.

Every acceptance criterion on this item has to name a command a person can actually type, so the
names have to be fixed before the item can be Ready. They also set the pattern the rest of the epic
follows — WI-0002 will introduce an equivalent pair for expenses, WI-0004 for payments, and
WI-0003 the command that prints who owes whom — so choosing here is choosing four times.

There is nothing in the record to derive them from: no existing code, no convention in this
repository, and you have not said how you would want to type them. It is cheap to change before the
criteria are written and slightly annoying afterwards, which is why it is being asked now.

## Question

What should the two subcommands on this item be called — the one that adds a person, and the one
that lists who is in the group?

## Options considered

- **A — `add-person <name>` and `people`.** A verb-noun for the thing that writes, a plain noun for
  the thing that reads. The rest of the epic would follow: `add-expense`, `expenses`, `add-payment`,
  `payments`, `who-owes-whom`. Consequence: reads as English at the prompt and stays unambiguous as
  the other commands arrive. Slightly more typing than C.
- **B — `person add <name>` and `person list`.** Noun first, then the operation, the way
  `docker container ls` and `git remote add` work. Consequence: groups well and scales if people
  ever gain more operations — but renaming and removing a person are out of scope for this whole
  epic, so there are exactly two operations to group, and the grouping costs a word on every line.
- **C — `add <name>` and `list`.** The shortest to type. Consequence: ambiguous the moment expenses
  and payments exist — `add` would have to mean "add a person" forever, or grow a qualifier, which
  is option A with extra steps.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is the shortest form that is still unambiguous once `add-expense` and
  `who-owes-whom` exist, and it needs no explanation to somebody who has not used the tool before.
  Pick B if you have a preference for the noun-first style; the work is identical either way, and
  it is only worth a round trip because changing it later means editing the acceptance criteria on
  four items rather than one.

## Answer

The human declined to choose and delegated it, as they did on `Q-001`:

> [human] Whatever you think is best, you know this better than I do.

That is a delegation, not a silence, so `answer-questions` decided it rather than escalating
again — `spec/question.md` §4 does not admit a vague answer as a condition for escalation, and
this is the second time they have handed this particular subject over.

**Decision: option A**, recorded as `ADR-0006`. A command that records a fact is `add-<noun>`; a
command that lists facts is the bare plural noun. For this item that is `add-person <name>` and
`people`; for the epic it fixes `add-expense` / `expenses`, `add-payment` / `payments`, and
`who-owes-whom`.

Option B (`person add`, `person list`) was rejected because noun-first grouping earns its keep
when a noun has many operations, and here each has exactly two — renaming and removing a person
are out of scope for the whole epic, and expenses and payments cannot be edited or deleted. It
would cost a word on every line to group pairs. Option C (`add`, `list`) was rejected because it
is ambiguous the moment expenses exist, and resolving that ambiguity later arrives at option A by
a worse route.

`ADR-0001` expected these names to be pinned per item by `refine`. `ADR-0006` settles the whole
vocabulary in one place instead, and says why: the question itself observed that choosing here is
choosing four times, and re-deriving the pattern on three later items is how an `add-expense`
ends up next to a `payment add`. What remains `refine`'s on each item is that command's
*arguments* — the flags for the amount, the payer and the sharers — which `ADR-0005` constrains
but does not fix.
