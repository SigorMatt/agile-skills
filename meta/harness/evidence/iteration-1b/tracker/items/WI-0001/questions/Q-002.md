## Context

AC4 on this item says that adding a person who is already in the group "produces a stated outcome
rather than a silent duplicate". That is not yet decidable by someone with a terminal, because two
things are missing: what makes two names *the same person*, and what the tool actually does when it
happens.

Both matter beyond this item. Every later item names people — an expense names a payer and sharers,
a payment names two people, and the balances print their names — so if "Sam" and "sam" can both be
in the group, the group will eventually have two Sams and the balances will be quietly wrong for
both. That is the kind of error nobody notices until they are settling up.

I cannot settle it from anything on disk. `docs/product/vision.md` (v3) says only that everyone
except the person running the tool "is a name in that person's records"; ADR-0001 fixes how a
refusal is reported but not what counts as one. It is a fact about how your group refers to each
other, which is exactly what the record does not contain.

## Question

When someone types a name that is already in the group — allowing for a difference in
capitalisation — should the tool treat it as the same person, and what should it do about it?

## Options considered

- **A — Exact match only, and refuse.** `Sam` and `sam` are two different people. Adding a name
  that already exists character-for-character is refused: a message on standard error, a non-zero
  exit, nothing added. Consequence: simplest rule, and the one that most easily produces two Sams
  by accident. Every later balance involving either of them would be half right.
- **B — Match ignoring case and surrounding spaces, and refuse.** `sam` is Sam. The spelling first
  entered is the one kept and shown everywhere. A second attempt is refused with a message naming
  the spelling that already exists ("Sam is already in the group"). Consequence: a typo is caught at
  the moment it is made, when it costs nothing. Adding somebody genuinely called `sam` alongside
  `Sam` becomes impossible without a distinguishing name.
- **C — Match ignoring case, and succeed quietly.** As B, but adding someone already there exits
  `0` and says they are already in the group; the roster is unchanged. Consequence: re-running a
  list of `add-person` commands is harmless, which is pleasant if you ever script the setup. The
  cost is that a typo'd re-add looks exactly like a successful one.
- **Recommendation: B.** In a friend group, typing `sam` when `Sam` exists is a typo, not a new
  person, and the failure mode of A — two people who are one person — corrupts the arithmetic the
  tool exists to do, silently and permanently. B tells you at the one moment when fixing it is free.
  Say C instead if you expect to re-run a batch of adds and would rather they be harmless; the
  difference between B and C is one line of behaviour and nothing else in the epic depends on it.

  Accented letters are treated as distinct under all three options — `José` and `Jose` are two
  people — unless you say otherwise. Folding accents together is a rule with surprising edges and I
  would rather not adopt it silently.

## Answer

The human chose **option B**, in their own words:

> [human] Go with B. If someone types "sam" and Sam's already in the group, just tell them and
> don't add a duplicate — I don't want two half-right versions of the same person messing up the
> totals.

Two names denote the same person when their **identity keys** are equal: surrounding whitespace
stripped, internal runs of whitespace collapsed to one space, the result case-folded. The spelling
first entered is the one recorded and shown. Adding a name whose key is already present is refused
— a message on standard error naming the spelling that exists, a non-zero exit status, nothing
added, per `ADR-0001`. Accented letters are not folded: `José` and `Jose` are two people, which is
what option B said and what they chose.

Two parts of this go beyond the literal answer, and are the architect's, not theirs. Both are in
`ADR-0005` and are marked as derivations there:

- **Collapsing internal whitespace**, so that `Sam  Okafor` is `Sam Okafor`. Option B named case
  and surrounding spaces only. It is included because a doubled internal space is the one variant
  that no printed output would ever reveal, and the human's stated objection is to two half-right
  versions of one person — which is exactly what it would produce.
- **Applying the same rule wherever a person is named** — payer, sharer, either party to a payment
  — not only in `add-person`. A tool that refuses to add `sam` because `Sam` exists, and then
  refuses `sam` as a payer because he does not, would be incoherent.

Recorded as `ADR-0005`, together with `Q-004`, because they are one identity rule with two halves.
