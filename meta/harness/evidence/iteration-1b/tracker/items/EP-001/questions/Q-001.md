## Context

The stated idea is: "add people, add expenses paid by someone and shared by some or all, and at
any point show who owes whom." Every verb in it is about *recording what was spent* and *deriving
the consequence*. Nothing in it says what happens when somebody actually hands over the money.

This matters for the shape of the whole epic, not just one item. If settling up is in scope,
there is a fourth kind of fact the tool has to store (a payment from one person to another), the
balance calculation has to net it off, and there is at least one more work item. If it is not,
"who owes whom" is a pure function of the expense list and never changes except by adding
expenses — a materially smaller product, and one where the group has to remember out-of-band what
they have already settled.

It is filed as non-blocking for a mechanical reason, not because it can be worked around: an
epic at status `open` is terminal in this pipeline, so there is no legal transition that
suspends it, and `blocking: true` would leave the workspace failing validation with no way to
fix it. In substance this question blocks everything — nothing sensible can be refined until it
is answered — and every work item under the epic is suspended in its own right, so no engineering
can proceed regardless. The orchestrator also stops on any open question addressed to the human,
whether or not it is marked blocking.

I have not guessed, because guessing wrong in either direction is expensive: building settlement
that is not wanted is wasted work, and omitting it means the tool tells the group they owe money
they already paid, which is worse than not having the tool.

## Question

Should the tool be able to record that a debt has been paid — for example "Alice paid Bob 20" —
so that it stops reporting that debt; or is the tool only a record of expenses, with settling up
handled entirely outside it?

## Options considered

- **A — Expenses only.** The tool records people and expenses; "who owes whom" is always the
  full running total since the group started. Consequence: smallest thing that works, three work
  items, nothing to get wrong. But the numbers grow forever and the group must track outside the
  tool what they have already squared up; realistically they would end up not using it after the
  first settle-up.
- **B — Expenses plus settlements.** A payment between two people is recordable and is netted
  off the balances. Consequence: one more work item, one more record type, and "who owes whom"
  becomes "who owes whom, as of everything recorded". The tool stays useful past the first
  settle-up. Costs perhaps a third more work across the epic.
- **C — Expenses plus a "reset to zero" action.** Instead of individual payments, an action that
  declares everybody settled and starts the balances again. Consequence: cheaper than B and
  covers the common case of the group squaring up all at once, but cannot express one person
  paying another in between.
- **Recommendation:** **B**, unless you want the smallest possible first version. B is what makes
  the tool still correct three months in, and A's failure mode — confidently reporting debts that
  were already paid — is the one thing that would make the group stop trusting it. If the answer
  is B, this epic gains a fourth work item; if A or C, the epic is unchanged apart from the
  out-of-scope list.

## Answer

[human] Yes — being able to mark that someone's paid up matters. Otherwise the numbers just keep racking up forever and stop meaning anything.

Read against the options above this is **option B — expenses plus settlements**, and explicitly
not option C: "mark that someone's paid up" is about a payment being recorded, and the reason
given ("the numbers just keep racking up forever") is the failure mode that a reset action would
only postpone until the next partial payment. `answer-questions` propagated it as B and did not
choose between B and C on the human's behalf; C is ruled out by the wording, and the difference is
recorded in WI-0004's `## Out of scope` so a later reader can see the alternative was considered
and why it was dropped.

Consequently the epic gains a fourth work item, as this question's context said it would. WI-0004
depends on WI-0003 rather than replacing it: WI-0003 still delivers a correct answer over expenses
alone, and WI-0004 adds a second kind of record that the same calculation nets off. That ordering
keeps both items separately demonstrable, which is the property `intake` used to split the epic in
the first place.
