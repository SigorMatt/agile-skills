## Context

`WI-0003`, round 1, question 2 of 3: which envelopes get a row. Question 1 was about what the
numbers mean; this one is about how many lines you are looking at.

At `EP-001/Q-003` you said *"One row per envelope"*. Read strictly that is every envelope you
have, and for most of them it is the right answer — an envelope with 50.00 carried in, nothing
added and nothing spent still tells you something, and the carrying-over is the thing you said
you would least want us to get wrong.

The case it does not settle is an envelope that had nothing to do with the month at all. If you
summarise August in November, and you created a `car insurance` envelope in October, does August
gain a row reading 0.00, 0.00, 0.00? That means last August's report is not the same document in
November that it was in September — it grows a line every time you make an envelope. And if you
run this for a while, the current month's report grows a row for every envelope you have ever
made, including the ones you stopped using.

Nobody has said either way, and it is not something we should decide for you: it is what you see
every time you run the command, and it is the difference between a report that is a snapshot of a
month and one that is a list of all your envelopes with a month's figures against them.

The last question this round is about whether income can carry a date the way a spend can.

## Question

Should the summary show a row for every envelope you have, or only the envelopes that had
something happen in that month — and where does that leave an envelope with money sitting in it
that you did not touch?

## Options considered

- **A — Every envelope, always.** A row each, zeros where nothing happened. Consequence: the
  report is a complete picture of where your money is at the end of that month, and the third
  column is meaningful on every line. The cost is that it grows as you accumulate envelopes, and
  an old month's report gains rows for envelopes that did not exist at the time.
- **B — Only envelopes with money in or money out that month.** Consequence: the report is about
  the month, it is short, and it is stable — August's summary looks the same whenever you run it.
  The cost is that an envelope holding 200.00 that you did not touch vanishes from the report
  entirely, which is a strange way to treat the money you are most successfully not spending.
- **C — Every envelope that existed by the end of that month.** Rows of zeros for untouched ones,
  no rows for envelopes made later. Consequence: the complete picture of A, and August's report
  does not change in November. The cost is that "existed by then" is a rule you cannot see from
  the output, so a missing row looks like a bug rather than a rule.
- **D — Every envelope with either activity in the month or money in it at the end.**
  Consequence: nothing with money in it is ever hidden, and a long-dead empty envelope stops
  appearing. The cost is the most complicated rule of the four to explain to yourself in six
  months.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **C**. It keeps the whole of *"one row per envelope"* for the
  month you are looking at, and it is the only option where a past month's summary is a fixed
  document — which matters because you said you look at a month *"a couple of days after it has
  ended"* and compare it against payslips and statements, and those do not change afterwards
  either. This is our reading, not yours: if you would rather the report were always just the
  envelopes you have now, A is simpler and we will build it.

## Answer

[human] C. August's report should say the same thing in November as it did in September — that's
the whole reason I check it against a statement. Envelopes I didn't touch still get their row;
that's where the rolled-over money is sitting.
