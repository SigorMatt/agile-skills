## Context

`WI-0003`, round 1, question 3 of 3 — the last of them for now. Whether income can carry a date
the way a spend can.

This is something we found by reading your answers against what has actually been built, and it
is not a question anyone has put to you before.

At `WI-0002/Q-001` you said a spend is dated today unless you say otherwise, and you gave the
reason yourself: *"a Saturday shop landing in the wrong month would make the monthly summary
wrong."* That is exactly right, and it is being built.

Income does not work that way. `envel add groceries 400` is finished and shipped, and it stamps
the money with the moment you typed the command. There is no way to say when it actually arrived.
So the first column of this summary — *"what went in that month"* — is keyed to when you sat down
at the terminal, not to when you were paid.

Your own argument applies to it word for word. If your salary lands on the 31st of August and you
enter it on the 2nd of September, August shows no income and September shows two salaries — the
same failure you asked us to avoid for spends, in the column next to it. And it is the column most
likely to be affected, because you told us at `EP-001/Q-004` that calendar months matter to you
precisely because *"calendar months are what my payslips and statements use."*

We are asking rather than fixing it because there are two honest answers and they cost very
different things: one changes a command that is already finished, and one accepts the limitation
and says so. Neither is ours to pick.

That is all three questions for this round. The others are `WI-0003/Q-001` and `WI-0003/Q-002`.

## Question

Should you be able to say when income arrived, the way you can for a spend — and if so, is that
worth changing a command that is already built and working?

## Options considered

- **A — Yes, and date income the same way as a spend.** `envel add` grows the same optional date
  as `envel spend`, and the summary's first column is keyed to when the money arrived.
  Consequence: both columns mean the same kind of thing, your report matches your payslips, and
  you learn one rule instead of two. The cost is new work on a finished command, and it would be
  its own item — this summary would wait for it or ship with the limitation in the meantime.
- **B — No. Income is dated when you type it, and that is good enough.** Consequence: nothing to
  change and nothing more to remember; if you enter your salary the day it lands, which is the
  normal case, the figures are right. The cost is that the one time you are late, that month's
  income figure is wrong and there is no way to correct it — and unlike a spend, you would not
  necessarily notice.
- **C — Yes, but not now.** Record the limitation against this summary, ship it, and raise
  dating income as its own piece of work you can schedule when you have used the tool for a
  month or two. Consequence: you find out from real use whether it actually bites before anyone
  spends time on it. The cost is that any income you enter late between now and then is filed in
  the wrong month permanently, because correcting a recorded entry is only planned for spends
  (`WI-0005`).
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **A**, as its own item, before this summary is built. The
  reason is your own sentence — a shop landing in the wrong month makes the summary wrong, and a
  payslip landing in the wrong month makes it wrong in the same way and by a larger amount. But
  this is a judgement about your habits, not about the code: if you always enter income the day
  it arrives, B is free and A is work for nothing. That is why it is a question rather than a
  decision.

## Answer

[human] B. I put income in when it arrives — that's the one thing I'm never late on, because
it's the bit I enjoy. I'm not paying for rework on a command that already works for a case I
don't have; if it turns out to bite me after a couple of months, I'll tell you then.
