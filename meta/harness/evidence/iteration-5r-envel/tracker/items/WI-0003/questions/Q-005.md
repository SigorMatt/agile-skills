## Context

`WI-0003`, round 2, question 2 of 2 — the last of them for now. A month that has not happened
yet.

This is a question we found by reading your last answer against a decision we had already taken,
and it turns out our decision no longer works. It is worth saying that plainly rather than
quietly changing it.

Round 1 decided — without asking you, and recorded as ours — that a month in the future would be
*"summarised like any other, and is simply empty"*. The reasoning was that a future month
contains no entries, so it would fall into the same case as a month in which nothing was
recorded. That was true when round 1 wrote it, and your answer to `WI-0003/Q-002` made it false.

You chose there that every envelope existing by the end of the month summarised gets a row, with
zeros where nothing happened and its carried-over balance under what is left. Apply that to
`2027-03`: every envelope you have today existed by the end of March 2027, so the report is not
empty at all. It prints **every one of your envelopes**, zeros in the in, spent and moved
columns, and today's balance under what is left. It looks exactly like a real report. Nothing on
the page tells you the month has not happened.

That matters here more than it would in most tools, because of something you have already said
twice. At `WI-0002/Q-005` you refused a spend dated in the future — *"Same reasoning as the
overspend: stop me rather than take something I didn't mean"* — and at `WI-0002/Q-004` you turned
down a short date form because *"a spend in the wrong month is exactly the thing I'm trying to
get away from"*. Typing `2027-08` when you meant `2026-08` is the same slip, and what you would
get back is a page of plausible figures under a heading you would have to read carefully to
catch.

The countervailing point is real too, and it is why we are asking rather than copying your spend
answer across. A summary records nothing and changes nothing: the worst a wrong month can do
here is waste the ten seconds it takes you to notice, where a wrongly dated spend sits in your
file until something goes wrong. Refusing may be protecting you from something that does not
need protecting against — and there is at least one honest use for it, which is asking what your
balances would be if you spent nothing more this month.

Nobody has said either way, and our own earlier answer is no longer available.

## Question

If you ask for a summary of a month that has not happened yet — `envel summary 2027-03` — should
the tool refuse it, or print it?

## Options considered

- **A — Refuse it.** A message saying that month has not happened yet, nothing printed, the same
  treatment a future date already gets when you record a spend. Consequence: the mistyped year
  stops at the point you make it, and the tool behaves the same way about future dates in both
  places you can write one. The cost is that there is no way to ask "what will I have left if I
  stop spending now", and a month that is *partly* in the future — the current one — has to be
  allowed, so the rule is "later than this month" rather than "later than today".
- **B — Print it like any other month.** Every envelope, zeros in the three activity columns,
  today's balances under what is left. Consequence: one rule for all months and nothing special
  to remember; the "what if I stop spending" reading works. The cost is the one above: a page
  that looks like a report for a month that does not exist, and a mistyped year that looks like
  a very quiet month.
- **C — Print it, with a line saying the month has not happened yet.** Consequence: you get the
  figures and you cannot mistake what you are looking at. The cost is a line of output in a
  report you have twice asked to stay small, and it is the only place in the tool that warns
  rather than either doing the thing or refusing it.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **A**. You have told us twice that you would rather be
  stopped than handed something you did not mean, and the tool already refuses a future date on
  the other command that takes one — so A is the answer that makes `envel` behave one way about
  future dates rather than two. This is our reading of what you said about spends applied to
  something you have not been asked about: a summary is read-only and you may well think that
  makes it not worth stopping for, in which case B is simpler than what we have today and we will
  build it.

## Answer

[human] A — refuse it, and say why. A page of figures with the wrong year at the top is exactly
the sort of thing I'd read straight past, and I'd rather be stopped. The "what if I stop spending
now" idea is a nice-to-have, not this round — don't build it in.
