## Context

`WI-0003`, round 1, question 1 of 3: what a move between envelopes does to the summary's
columns. Three questions in all, and they are one conversation about what the three numbers in
each row actually mean.

When you told us what the summary should show, at `EP-001/Q-003`, you chose *"One row per
envelope showing what went in that month, what was spent, and what is left."* You gave that
answer before moving money between envelopes existed as a thing the tool would do — you asked
for that a few minutes later, at `EP-001/Q-005`, and it is now `WI-0004`. So nobody has ever
said what a move should look like in these columns, and there is no reading of your answer that
settles it.

It matters because you told us moving money is not an occasional thing: *"I will need it the
moment an envelope runs short, which is the same week I start using this."* If you move 30.00
from `fun` to `groceries` in a month, the row for `groceries` either says 30.00 went in that
month or it does not, and the two versions of the report tell you different stories about
where your money went. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions.

The other two questions this round are about which envelopes get a row at all, and about
whether income can be dated the way a spend can.

## Question

When you move money from one envelope to another, should that show in the summary as money
going into the receiving envelope and money out of the giving one, or should moves be left out
of those two columns entirely?

## Options considered

- **A — A move counts, like anything else.** The receiving envelope shows it under money in, the
  giving envelope shows it under money spent. Consequence: the columns add up to the change in
  every balance without exception, and the report never has a number you cannot account for. The
  cost is that "what was spent" now includes money that did not leave your budget at all — a
  month where you shuffled 200.00 around looks like a month where you spent 200.00 more than you
  did.
- **B — Moves are excluded from both columns.** Money in means income, money spent means real
  spending, and a move changes only the third column, the amount left. Consequence: the first
  two columns mean exactly what their names say, and a month of shuffling does not inflate your
  spending. The cost is that the three figures in a row no longer reconcile on their own — an
  envelope can end the month with less in it than "in minus spent" would predict, and the
  difference is invisible.
- **C — Excluded from those two columns, and shown as a fourth figure.** Money in, money spent,
  moved in or out, and what is left. Consequence: both of the above, with nothing hidden. The
  cost is a fourth number on every row in a report you asked to be small, and it is there even in
  the months when you moved nothing.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **B**, with the reconciliation gap written into the item as a
  known consequence rather than hidden. Your reason for wanting this report was to see *"what
  came in and what went out"*, and a transfer is neither; you also said the report should be
  small and that the thing you are getting away from is a spreadsheet that shows you everything.
  We hold this one loosely — C is the only answer where the numbers on the page are complete, and
  if you would rather have the fourth column, say so.

## Answer

[human] C. "Spent" has to mean money that left the house, so moves don't belong in it — but I
won't use a report whose rows don't add up, I've spent years chasing that in the spreadsheet. A
column of zeros costs me nothing; a number I can't account for costs me an evening.
