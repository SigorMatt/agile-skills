## Context

This is one of six questions filed on `EP-001` in the first round, at intake. They are one
ask, not six: every one of them is about the idea you stated, and answering them together is
what lets the work start.

How we read your idea: you want a command-line tool, written in Python, with no services of any
kind, for envelope budgeting — you name envelopes, put income into them, record each spend
against the envelope it came out of, and see what is left in each. You also want a simple
summary of a month. Whatever you record must still be there the next time you run the tool.
That reading is what `EP-001`, `WI-0001`, `WI-0002` and `WI-0003` were written from; if it is
wrong anywhere, Q-001 is the place to say so.

This is question 5 of 6. Your idea names four verbs — set up, put in, spend, look — and none of
them is a correction. In practice people mistype an amount, or find that the grocery envelope is
empty and the fun envelope is not. We have written both of those as out of scope in `WI-0001`
and `WI-0002` and pointed them here rather than deciding on your behalf, because adding them
is real work and adding them later costs more than adding them now. Q-002's answer interacts
with this: if an overspend is refused, moving money between envelopes is the only way to record
what actually happened.

## Question

Should this first version also let you fix mistakes and move money between envelopes, or is it
only the four things you named?

## Options considered

- **A — Only the four you named.** Set up, put in, spend, look. Consequence: the smallest tool
  that does what you asked, delivered soonest; a mistyped amount is corrected by recording a
  compensating entry, which works but leaves both entries in the month's summary.
- **B — Add moving money between envelopes.** One more command, taking from one envelope and
  putting into another. Consequence: envelope budgeting the way it is usually practised, and the
  natural companion to a refused overspend; roughly one more work item.
- **C — Add both moving money and correcting or deleting a recorded entry.** Consequence: you
  can make the record match reality whatever you do; the cost is the largest, and deletion
  raises its own question about whether a deleted spend should still show in a past summary.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be B — a correction can be done by recording the opposite entry
  and lived with, whereas the absence of moving money is felt every time an envelope runs short.
  We would open it as its own item rather than folding it into `WI-0002`.

## Answer

[human] C — both, please. Moving money between envelopes is normal practice and I will need it the moment an envelope runs short, which is the same week I start using this. And I mistype things in the spreadsheet most weeks, so being able to correct a spend I already recorded matters to me more than keeping a perfect history of my own typos — if I fix an entry, a past summary should just show the corrected figure.
