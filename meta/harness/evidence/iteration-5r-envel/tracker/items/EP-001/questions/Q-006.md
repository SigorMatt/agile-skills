## Context

This is one of six questions filed on `EP-001` in the first round, at intake. They are one
ask, not six: every one of them is about the idea you stated, and answering them together is
what lets the work start.

How we read your idea: you want a command-line tool, written in Python, with no services of any
kind, for envelope budgeting — you name envelopes, put income into them, record each spend
against the envelope it came out of, and see what is left in each. You also want a simple
summary of a month. Whatever you record must still be there the next time you run the tool.
That reading is what `EP-001`, `WI-0001`, `WI-0002` and `WI-0003` were written from; if it is
wrong anywhere, Q-001 is the place to say so.

This is question 6 of 6 — the last one in this round, and the smallest. You will type this
tool's name more often than anything else about it, and nobody but you should choose it. Nothing
downstream can pin the acceptance criteria to an exact command until it is settled, which is why
it is being asked now rather than left to whoever writes the first line of code.

The rest of the output wording — what the tool says when it creates an envelope, how amounts are
printed — is not being asked here. Unless you say otherwise in Q-001, we will decide that during
refinement and show you the result.

## Question

What should the command be called?

## Options considered

- **A — `envel`.** Short, five letters, unlikely to collide with anything already installed.
  Consequence: quick to type, and slightly cryptic to read in your own shell history.
- **B — `envelope`.** Says exactly what it is. Consequence: obvious, and eight characters
  every time; also a fairly generic word to occupy on a machine.
- **C — Something of your own.** Consequence: whatever you want it to be; we only need it to be
  a single word a shell will accept.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be A, on the grounds that you will type it several times a day —
  but this is genuinely a matter of taste and C is a perfectly good answer.

## Answer

[human] `envel`. Short is what matters if I'm typing it several times a day.
