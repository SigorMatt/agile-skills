## Context

This is one of six questions filed on `EP-001` in the first round, at intake. They are one
ask, not six: every one of them is about the idea you stated, and answering them together is
what lets the work start.

How we read your idea: you want a command-line tool, written in Python, with no services of any
kind, for envelope budgeting — you name envelopes, put income into them, record each spend
against the envelope it came out of, and see what is left in each. You also want a simple
summary of a month. Whatever you record must still be there the next time you run the tool.
That reading is what `EP-001`, `WI-0001`, `WI-0002` and `WI-0003` were written from; if it is
wrong anywhere, Q-001 is the place to say so.

This is question 3 of 6. You asked for "a simple monthly summary I can look at", and that is the
one part of the idea we cannot turn into something checkable without you: we can write a summary
that is simple and still not the one you had in mind. We have written a first guess into
`WI-0003` AC2 — each envelope, with money in and money spent that month — and flagged it as
ours rather than yours.

## Question

What does the monthly summary need to show you for it to be worth looking at?

## Options considered

- **A — Per envelope: money in, money spent, amount left.** Three numbers a row, one row per
  envelope. Consequence: it answers "where did it go" per envelope and is easy to read at ten
  envelopes. It does not tell you anything about the month as a whole.
- **B — A — plus a total line** for the whole month: total in, total spent, total left.
  Consequence: you get both the per-envelope breakdown and the one-line answer to "did I
  overspend this month".
- **C — B — plus the individual spends** listed under each envelope. Consequence: the summary
  becomes the record of the month and you never need another command to see detail; the cost is
  that it stops being short, which is the thing you asked for.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be B — it is the shortest thing that answers both questions a
  month-end look usually asks — but "simple" is your word, and you are the one who has to find
  it worth looking at.

## Answer

[human] A. One row per envelope showing what went in that month, what was spent, and what is left. That is the thing I actually want to see. I don't need a total line for the month and I definitely don't want every individual spend listed — that is the spreadsheet I am trying to get away from.
