## Context

ask, not six: every one of them is about the idea you stated, and answering them together is
what lets the work start.

How we read your idea: you want a command-line tool, written in Python, with no services of any
kind, for envelope budgeting — you name envelopes, put income into them, record each spend
against the envelope it came out of, and see what is left in each. You also want a simple
summary of a month. Whatever you record must still be there the next time you run the tool.
That reading is what `EP-001`, `WI-0001`, `WI-0002` and `WI-0003` were written from; if it is
wrong anywhere, Q-001 is the place to say so.

This is question 4 of 6, and it is the other half of the summary. Separate from *what* the
summary shows (Q-003) is *which* month it covers and how the tool knows. Two different sensible
answers put a spend on the 31st into two different months, so somebody has to decide, and
guessing it would quietly build a rule you never chose. It decides `WI-0003` AC5.

## Question

Which month should the summary cover by default, and should you be able to ask for a different
one?

## Options considered

- **A — The current calendar month, and you can name another** (for example by giving a year and
  month). Consequence: the boundary is the calendar's, so it lines up with statements and
  payslips, and last month stays reachable when you look on the 2nd.
- **B — The current calendar month only.** Consequence: the simplest possible command, nothing
  to remember, and no way to look back at a month once it is over — which makes a summary you
  can only see while the month is still running.
- **C — The last 30 days, ending today.** Consequence: always a full period of data whenever you
  run it, and it never lines up with anything else you will compare it against; "March" stops
  being a thing the tool can show you.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be A, because a month-end look usually happens after the month
  has ended. It costs one optional argument.

## Answer

[human] A. Calendar months, the one I'm in by default, and I can ask for an earlier one — I usually look at a month a couple of days after it has ended. Calendar months are what my payslips and statements use, so anything else would be useless to me.
