## Question

How is a stale or wrong citation **inside** an append-only `## Corrections` row repaired — by
appending a further row that names the earlier row and what was wrong with it, or is the earlier row
left standing as the history it is, with the error visible and recorded only here?

## Options considered

- **A — append a further correction row to each ADR, naming the row it corrects.** This is the
  journal's own rule for an append-only record — *"a wrong entry is corrected by a later entry that
  says what was wrong"* (`spec/journal-and-history.md`) — applied to the one other append-only
  structure in the workspace. Consequence: a reader of `ADR-0006` or `ADR-0011` sees the error and
  its repair together, and no evidence is destroyed. Cost: it needs a `kind` for a row whose subject
  is another row, and `doc-header.md` §4b's alphabet is `provenance` and `erratum` — `erratum` fits
  F4 (*a clause that was false against the code*) and F3 is arguably `provenance`. It also grows a
  section whose entries are getting further from the decision they are attached to.
- **B — leave both rows standing, and record the errors here only.** Consequence: nothing is written
  that the section's own rule did not anticipate, and the `## Findings` section of
  `tracker/items/EP-001/artifacts/review.md` is the record. Cost: the two sentences stay false in
  `docs/`, where a reader is far likelier to meet them than in a closed epic's review; and DE6 asks
  whether *every claim in `docs/`* is true, so this option answers it with "no, and we know".
- **C — repair F4 by appending a row and leave F3, on the ground that they are different.** F4 is a
  claim about what a line contains, which is falsifiable at a glance and was wrong when written; F3
  is a citation that was right and drifted, which is the ordinary shape and which no repair can
  stop recurring inside a section that may not be edited. Consequence: the repair goes where it
  buys most. Cost: two treatments for one class, which is the thing `Q-007`'s recommendation is
  trying to avoid.
- **D — treat a `## Corrections` row's citation as exempt from DE6**, because the row is a dated
  historical statement rather than a standing claim about the product. Consequence: the question
  stops recurring and the audit's scope gets a stated boundary. Cost: that boundary is
  `spec/doc-header.md` §4a's to draw, not this engagement's, and F4 shows the exemption would cover
  a sentence that is simply wrong rather than merely dated.
- **Recommendation:** **A**, held loosely, and it is the team's preference rather than a finding.
  The append-only rule exists to keep evidence, not to freeze errors, and appending is the move
  every other append-only record here already uses. D is the interesting one and is worth raising
  at `retro` whichever way this goes: whether an audit of *claims about the product* should be
  reading rows whose subject is the record's own history is a real question, and this engagement is
  the first to have hit it.

## Answer

**A — append a further `## Corrections` row whose subject is the earlier row, and leave the earlier
row untouched.** The record was silent on this, so it is a new decision and it is recorded as
`ADR-0013` [src: ADR-0013] rather than settled inside this question file.

The decision in short, and the ADR carries the reasoning: the earlier row is never edited — not its
citation, not its wording, not its stamp; the new row carries `kind: erratum`, which is the kind for
a clause that was false against the code and whose quoting requirement identifies the target here
instead of preserving something deleted, since nothing is deleted; and the new row says **which**
row it corrects, by that row's `when` stamp, and states what is true with a citation that resolves.

It is the journal's own rule — *"a wrong entry is corrected by a later entry that says what was
wrong"* — applied to the other append-only structure in this workspace. `provenance` was considered
and is the wrong word: nothing in the earlier row gains a citation.

**B was rejected** because it leaves two false sentences in `docs/`, where a reader is far likelier
to meet them than in a closed epic's review, and DE6 asks exactly that question of `docs/`.
**C**, exempting `## Corrections` rows from the claims audit, is the interesting one and is not mine
to take: the scope of that audit is the toolkit's claims spec, not this project's, and `ADR-0011`'s
row shows the exemption would have covered a sentence that is simply wrong rather than merely dated.
It stays a toolkit finding for `retro`. **D** would supersede two ADRs whose decisions are correct.

Both rows are repaired in this execution under the rule just decided, so the question does not leave
its own finding outstanding.
