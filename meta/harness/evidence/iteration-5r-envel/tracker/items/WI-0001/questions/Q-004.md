## Context

`WI-0001`, round 1, question 4 of 4 — the last of this round, and the one about the names
themselves.

Every other command in this tool finds an envelope by the name you typed, so how names are
matched decides whether `groceries` and `Groceries` are one envelope or two. You would find
out which the hard way, by ending up with two envelopes and half the money in each. We would
otherwise just pick, but the answer shows up in your own listing every day, so it is yours.

## Question

Should envelope names be matched exactly as typed, or should differences of capitalisation be
treated as the same envelope — and is there anything a name should not be allowed to contain?

## Options considered

- **A — Case-insensitive matching, stored as first typed.** `Groceries` finds the envelope you
  created as `groceries`, and the listing shows it the way you first wrote it. Any characters
  are allowed except a leading or trailing space, and the empty name. Consequence: you cannot
  accidentally end up with two envelopes that look the same; the cost is that you cannot
  deliberately keep `Fun` and `fun` apart.
- **B — Exact matching.** `Groceries` and `groceries` are two different envelopes.
  Consequence: what you type is what you get, always; the cost is the split-envelope mistake
  above, which is quiet and annoying to undo.
- **C — Case-insensitive, and restricted to letters, digits, spaces, hyphens and underscores.**
  Consequence: A, plus names that are safe to type in a shell without quoting; the cost is that
  a name with an ampersand or an emoji in it is refused.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be A. The split-envelope mistake is the one that actually costs
  you something, and refusing characters you might want is a restriction with no benefit to you.

## Answer

[human] A. "Groceries" and "groceries" are the same envelope — the same money split across two of them is exactly the mess I want to avoid. Show it back the way I first typed it, and don't go restricting what characters I can use; my names are things like "groceries", "car" and "eating out".
