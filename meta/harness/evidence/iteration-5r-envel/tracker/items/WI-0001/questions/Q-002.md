## Context

`WI-0001`, round 1, question 2 of 4: how amounts are written and shown.

`WI-0001` AC2 and AC3 talk about "an amount" without saying what one looks like. Somebody with
a terminal cannot decide whether the tool is behaving correctly until they know whether
`12.5` is accepted, what it becomes, and how it comes back out of the listing. It also decides
whether a third of a pound rounds, and rounding is the sort of thing that quietly makes the
figures stop adding up.

## Question

How should amounts be written when I type them, and how should they be shown back to me?

## Options considered

- **A — Amounts to two decimal places, shown to two decimal places.** `12.5` is read as
  12.50 and printed as `12.50`; more than two decimals is refused. Consequence: it matches how
  money is normally written and the arithmetic stays exact; the cost is you may not enter a
  fraction of a penny.
- **B — Whole units only.** `12` is accepted, `12.50` is refused. Consequence: the simplest
  possible figures and nothing to round; the cost is you cannot record a real shop receipt.
- **C — Any number of decimals, shown as entered.** Consequence: nothing is refused; the cost is
  that totals in the summary can show figures like `33.333333`, and two entries that look
  equal may not be.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be A — it is what a budget is usually kept in, and it is the
  only one of the three in which a month's figures always add up exactly.

## Answer

[human] A. One currency, cents exactly, two decimal places both ways — `12.5` meaning 12.50 is fine. I do not want anything rounded off; the figures have to add up. How you keep it under the hood is yours to decide.
