## Context

`WI-0005`, round 2, question 1 of 3. Three questions in all, and they are one conversation about
what is left after your round-1 answers. Two of them are about income, which is the thing you
brought into this item last time.

You said corrections should reach an income *"in the same way as a spend"* [src: WI-0005/Q-005],
and we took that as settled. Writing the criteria for it, we found that *in the same way* cannot
be done without contradicting something else you decided, so we are putting both of your
sentences in front of you rather than choosing between them.

A spend carries four things: an envelope, an amount, a **date it happened**, and an optional
description. You can correct all four — that was your answer at `WI-0005/Q-002`.

An income carries three: an envelope, an amount, and the moment it was typed. It has **no date
you can set**, and that is your decision, not an omission of ours. At `WI-0003/Q-003` we asked
whether income could be dated the way a spend can, and you said:

> [human] B. I put income in when it arrives — that's the one thing I'm never late on, because
> it's the bit I enjoy. I'm not paying for rework on a command that already works for a case I
> don't have; if it turns out to bite me after a couple of months, I'll tell you then.

And at `WI-0005/Q-005` you said:

> [human] B — spends and income. I turned down the negative income on the understanding that
> proper corrections were coming, so corrections had better reach an income; a payslip typed with
> the wrong number of noughts is worse than a mistyped shop. Not moves — that stays as I decided
> it.

Both are reasonable and they do not sit together. If a correction can change an income's date,
then income has a date you can set after all, reached by a different command — which is the
rework you declined, arriving by the back door. If it cannot, then *"in the same way as a spend"*
is not quite true, and you should know which part is missing.

Nothing here reopens `envel add` itself: adding income still takes no date, whichever way you
answer. The question is only what `envel fix` may change on one.

A related fact you may want when choosing: which month an income counts in is decided by the
moment it was typed, and `WI-0003`'s monthly summary uses that. So an income typed on the 2nd of
September for August's payslip is already in September's summary, and no correction can move it
unless you answer C below.

The other two questions this round are whether an income can be **removed** as well as corrected,
and whether the reference you read off the listing is numbered within an envelope or across the
whole file.

## Question

When you correct an income, which of its parts should `envel fix` be able to change: the amount
only, the amount and which envelope it went into, or those and the date it counts in — which
would mean income gains a date it does not have today?

## Options considered

- **A — The amount only.** `envel fix 12 --amount 1800.00`. Consequence: it fixes the mistake you
  named — *"a payslip typed with the wrong number of noughts"* — and it changes nothing about what
  an income is. `WI-0003/Q-003` stands untouched. The cost is that a payslip typed against the
  wrong envelope cannot be put right by correcting; you would have to use `envel move` to shift
  the money, which leaves the income recorded in the wrong place even though the balances end up
  right.
- **B — The amount and the envelope.** `envel fix 12 --amount 1800.00 --envelope salary`.
  Consequence: both mistakes you can make while typing `envel add` are fixable, and income still
  has no date, so `WI-0003/Q-003` stands. The cost is that changing an envelope moves money
  between two of them and can be refused for lack of funds in the one it leaves, exactly as
  correcting a spend's envelope can.
- **C — The amount, the envelope, and the date it counts in.** This gives income a date you can
  set, reached through `envel fix` rather than through `envel add`. Consequence: an income is
  corrected in exactly the same way as a spend, with no exception to remember, and a payslip that
  landed in the wrong month can be moved to the right one. The cost is that it reverses
  `WI-0003/Q-003` in substance — you declined that rework — and it is more work than A or B, on
  `envel add`, on the summary, and on the listing.
- **D — The amount and the envelope now; the date only if it bites you.** As B, with the date
  question left on the board as its own item, which is the arrangement your own `WI-0003/Q-003`
  answer proposed: *"if it turns out to bite me after a couple of months, I'll tell you then."*
  Consequence: nothing is decided against you and the gap is visible rather than forgotten. The
  cost is an item on the board that may never be built, and the epic cannot finish while a child
  item is undecided, so it would have to be closed rather than parked.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **B**. It is the reading of *"in the same way as a spend"*
  that takes everything you can type into `envel add` and makes it correctable, without giving
  income a field you told us you did not want. We are not recommending **C** because you declined
  precisely that rework in your own words and nothing since has changed the case for it — but if
  a payslip in the wrong month is a mistake you actually make, **C** is the honest answer and it
  is yours to give.

## Answer

[human] B — the amount and the envelope. What I meant by "the same way as a spend" was that
anything I can type wrong when I put it in, I can put right; income has two of those and a spend
has four, and that is the whole of the difference. I am not giving income a date by the back door
after turning it down two rounds ago, and I am not leaving a dated-income item sitting on the
board either — that is D and it is a nice-to-have, not this round. If a payslip in the wrong month
ever actually costs me something I will come and tell you, as I said before.
