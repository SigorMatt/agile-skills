## Context

`WI-0005`, round 1, question 3 of 5. One conversation about correcting a spend; this one is
about a spend that should not be there at all.

Correcting assumes the spend happened and you got a detail wrong. The other mistake is
different in kind: you recorded a spend twice, or you recorded one against the wrong tool
entirely and there is no figure to fix — there is an entry that should not exist. Nothing in
the record says whether you want to be able to remove one.

Two of your answers pull in opposite directions here, and neither settles it:

- at `WI-0001/Q-001` you said *"Nothing should ever be able to wipe money out of an envelope by
  accident"*, which is a reason to be careful about anything that deletes;
- at `EP-001/Q-005` you were explicit that you would rather fix an entry than keep a perfect
  history of your own typos, which is a reason not to make you live with a duplicate for ever.

You cannot get to the same place by correcting. The tool as delivered refuses a spend of zero
when you record one, and we would not want a correction to be the one back door through that
rule — that is the shape of thing you refused outright at `WI-0001/Q-003`, where you turned down
a negative income because *"there is no need for a back door"*.

## Question

Should you also be able to remove a recorded spend altogether, as well as change one?

## Options considered

- **A — No. Corrections only.** Consequence: nothing in the tool can ever make a spend
  disappear, which is the strongest reading of *"nothing should ever be able to wipe money out of
  an envelope by accident"* — except that here it is the reverse, money reappearing. The cost is
  that a spend you recorded twice stays recorded twice, your envelope balance is permanently
  wrong by that amount, and the only workaround is putting income in to cover it, which would
  then be wrong too.
- **B — Yes, as a separate command, and it says what it removed.**
  `envel unspend 7` answers `removed the 12.50 spent from groceries on 2026-09-07, which now
  holds 100.00`. Consequence: a duplicate is fixable and you see exactly what went, so it is hard
  to do by accident to the wrong entry. The cost is one more command to build and one more thing
  that can be typed wrongly.
- **C — Yes, but only by correcting the amount to zero.** `envel fix 7 0`. Consequence: nothing
  new to build beyond the correction command itself. The cost is that the spend is still there
  as a zero, it still shows up wherever spends are listed, and it makes zero a special value in
  a tool where you refused zero everywhere else.
- **D — Yes, and it asks you to confirm before it does it.** As B, but it prints the spend and
  waits for `y`. Consequence: the strongest protection against removing the wrong entry. The
  cost is that it is the only command in this tool that stops and asks you something, and you
  have twice chosen being refused outright over being prompted.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **B**. A duplicate spend is a mistake you will make, and it
  is the one mistake correcting cannot repair; and B keeps zero refused everywhere, which is the
  rule you set. We prefer it over D because nothing else in this tool asks you a question, and
  your stated preference at `WI-0002/Q-005` was *"stop me rather than take something I didn't
  mean"* — a refusal, not a prompt. If you would rather this round stayed small, **A** is a
  legitimate answer and the duplicate case can come back as its own item later.

## Answer

[human] B — yes, and have it tell me what it took out. Recording the same shop twice is the one
mistake I cannot correct my way out of, and I don't want a zero sitting in there pretending to be
a spend. Don't make it stop and ask me to confirm; just say plainly what went and what the
envelope holds now.
