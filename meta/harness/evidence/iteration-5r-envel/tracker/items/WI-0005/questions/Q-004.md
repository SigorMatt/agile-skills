## Context

`WI-0005`, round 1, question 4 of 5. One conversation about correcting a spend; this one is
about the correction that does not fit.

At `EP-001/Q-002` you settled what happens when you record a spend bigger than the envelope
holds: *"A — refuse it and tell me the envelope only has so much left. I don't want to see a
negative envelope; if the money has to come from somewhere else then I want to move it there
myself and record the spend after."* That is built and it works at the moment of recording.

It says nothing about the moment of correcting, and the case is not hypothetical. Suppose
`groceries` holds 30.00 and you find a spend you typed as 12.50 that was really 140.00. Recording
it fresh would be refused. Correcting it to 140.00 would take the envelope to −97.50 — a
negative envelope, which is the one thing you said you did not want to see.

It is not obviously the same question as the original one, which is why we are asking rather
than reading your answer across. When you record a spend, refusing you costs nothing: the money
has not moved and you can go and move some first. When you correct one, the money *already went*
— the 140.00 left the house — and refusing you means the tool is holding a figure you know to be
wrong because it cannot represent the truth.

## Question

When a correction would take an envelope below zero, should the tool refuse it, or record it and
let the envelope go negative?

## Options considered

- **A — Refuse it, the same as recording one.** `groceries holds 30.00, which is less than
  140.00. Nothing has been changed; move money into it first if you want to spend that.`
  Consequence: you never see a negative envelope, and the rule is the same one everywhere in the
  tool, so there is nothing extra to remember. The cost is that the tool is refusing to record
  something that has already happened, and you have to move money first and then come back and
  retype the correction.
- **B — Allow it, and let the envelope show negative.** Consequence: the figures always say what
  actually happened, and a negative number is a loud signal that you owe that envelope some
  money. The cost is that it is exactly the thing you said you did not want to see, and once one
  envelope can be negative every total in the tool has to cope with it.
- **C — Refuse it, and tell you what would fix it.** As A, but the message also says how much is
  missing: `groceries holds 30.00 and the corrected spend is 140.00, so it is 110.00 short. Move
  110.00 into groceries and run this again.` Consequence: you are still never shown a negative
  envelope, and the refusal tells you the number you need rather than making you work it out.
  The cost is one more sentence of output and nothing else.
- **D — Allow it, but only when you say so.** Refuse by default; `envel fix 7 140.00 --anyway`
  goes through and the envelope goes negative. Consequence: the truth can always be recorded and
  you can never do it by accident. The cost is a flag that exists for a rare case, and a
  negative envelope is still possible, so everything that reads a balance still has to cope.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **C**. It keeps the single rule you set — no negative
  envelopes, ever — and it removes the only real cost of A, which is having to work out how short
  you are. We have put B and D in front of you anyway because your reason for refusing at
  `EP-001/Q-002` was about a spend that had not happened yet, and this is a spend that has; if
  you would rather the tool recorded the truth and showed you a negative number until you fix it,
  that is a different and defensible answer and we will build it.

## Answer

[human] C. No negative envelopes anywhere in this tool, and that includes here — refuse it and
tell me how much short I am, so I can move the money across and type the correction again. Doing
it in two steps is fine; seeing a minus number is not.
