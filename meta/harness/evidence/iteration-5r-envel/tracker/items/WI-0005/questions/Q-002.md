## Context

`WI-0005`, round 1, question 2 of 5. One conversation about correcting a spend; this one is about
*what* you can change once you have pointed at one.

A spend as the tool records it today has four things on it, and you chose three of them
yourself:

- the **envelope** it came out of;
- the **amount** — cents exactly, two decimal places [src: WI-0001/Q-002];
- the **date** it happened — today unless you say otherwise, because *"a Saturday shop landing
  in the wrong month would make the monthly summary wrong"* [src: WI-0002/Q-001];
- an optional **description**, *"on the ones I might query later"* [src: WI-0002/Q-002].

Your words at `EP-001/Q-005` were about a mistyped figure — *"I mistype things in the
spreadsheet most weeks"* — and the example you gave was an amount. But three of these four are
things you type, and two of them change which month a spend lands in or which envelope it came
out of, which is more consequential than the amount. We are not going to assume which ones you
meant, because building the wrong subset here is a whole extra round of work later.

This matters for a reason beyond typing: the date is the field that decides which monthly
summary a spend appears in [src: EP-001/Q-004], so a wrong date is exactly the mistake your
*"a past summary should just show the corrected figure"* rule was written for — and it is not an
amount.

## Question

When you correct a spend, which of its four parts do you want to be able to change: the amount
only, or the amount, the date and the description, or all four including which envelope it came
out of?

## Options considered

- **A — The amount only.** Consequence: the smallest thing to build and the smallest command to
  type, and it is exactly the example you gave. The cost is that a spend typed against the wrong
  envelope, or dated wrongly so that it fell in the wrong month, can only be fixed if a spend can
  also be removed and retyped — which is question 3 of this round, and if the answer there is
  also no, those two mistakes are permanent.
- **B — The amount, the date and the description; not the envelope.** Consequence: covers every
  mistake you can make while typing the line except getting the envelope wrong, including the
  wrong-month one. The cost is that moving a spend from `groceries` to `eating out` stays
  impossible, and that is a mistake we would expect to be common with eight or ten envelopes.
- **C — All four, including which envelope it came out of.** Consequence: any part of a spend
  you got wrong can be put right. The cost is that changing the envelope moves money between two
  envelopes' balances, which looks a lot like `envel move` and could be confused with it — and it
  can be refused for lack of funds in the new envelope, where the other three cannot.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **C**, weakly. The reason is that the four fields are one
  line you typed, and a tool that lets you fix three quarters of a line invites the question
  "why not that one". Against it: you drew a clear line yourself at `WI-0004/Q-001` between a
  spend you can fix and a move you cannot, and changing the envelope on a spend is the one
  correction that is also a move. If that distinction matters more to you than completeness, **B**
  is the honest answer and it is less to build.

## Answer

[human] C — all four. If I typed the line wrong I want to fix the line, and with ten envelopes,
putting the Saturday shop against "eating out" instead of "groceries" is a mistake I will
certainly make. That is still fixing a spend, not fixing a move: a move stays unfixable, exactly
as I decided before.

## Cross-answer check

Checked against: WI-0004/Q-001; WI-0002/Q-001; WI-0002/Q-002; WI-0001/Q-002; EP-001/Q-005.

- `WI-0004/Q-001` — **compatible**, and this is the pair the question was filed to watch. *"I can
  fix a spend afterwards and I can't fix a move"* is the sentence `WI-0004` was designed around, and
  option C is the one that puts it under pressure: changing a spend's envelope moves money between
  two envelopes without being an `envel move`. The stakeholder saw that and drew the line
  themselves — *"That is still fixing a spend, not fixing a move: a move stays unfixable, exactly as
  I decided before"* — so the older answer stands unedited and this one says where its boundary is.
  That is the reconciliation ADR-0008 §3 asks for, given by the person who wrote both sentences.
- `WI-0002/Q-001` — **compatible**. A spend is dated *"today unless I say otherwise"* because a
  Saturday shop in the wrong month makes the summary wrong; correcting the date is the repair for
  exactly that mistake. How a date is given when a spend is first recorded is untouched.
- `WI-0002/Q-002` — **compatible**. The description is optional at `envel spend` and stays so; this
  adds that it can be changed afterwards.
- `WI-0001/Q-002` — **compatible**. Cents exactly, two decimals; a corrected amount is read and
  shown the same way a first-typed one is.
- `EP-001/Q-005` — **compatible**. It gave an amount as the example and did not draw a boundary;
  this reply says the example was an example.

### Filed with the question

Filed with the question, naming the prior answers this reply will have to coexist with:

- `WI-0002/Q-001` — *"Today unless I say otherwise"*, and the reason: a shop landing in the
  wrong month makes the summary wrong. Options B and C let you repair that mistake; option A
  does not. Nothing here changes how a date is given when a spend is first recorded.
- `WI-0002/Q-002` — the description is optional. Under B and C, correcting a spend would let you
  add a description that was not there, or change one; whether you can take one off again is part
  of the same answer and we will write it into the criteria whichever way you answer, rather than
  filing a sixth question about it.
- `WI-0001/Q-002` — cents exactly, two decimals, nothing rounded. A corrected amount is read and
  shown the same way a first-typed one is under every option.
- `WI-0004/Q-001` — *"I can fix a spend afterwards and I can't fix a move."* Option C is the one
  place that sentence comes under pressure: changing a spend's envelope moves money between two
  envelopes without being an `envel move`. It does not contradict your answer — a move is still
  unfixable — but if C is what you want, you should want it knowing that.
- `EP-001/Q-005` — the answer that asked for corrections. It named a spend and gave an amount as
  the example; it did not say whether the other three fields were included, which is why this is
  a question rather than an assumption.
