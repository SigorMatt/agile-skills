## Context

`WI-0005`, round 2, question 2 of 3. One conversation about what your round-1 answers left open;
this one is the second of the two about income.

At `WI-0005/Q-003` you asked to be able to remove an entry outright, and gave the reason:

> [human] B — yes, and have it tell me what it took out. Recording the same shop twice is the one
> mistake I cannot correct my way out of, and I don't want a zero sitting in there pretending to
> be a spend.

That question was about a **spend**, and your reply names a shop. At `WI-0005/Q-005`, answered in
the same round, you extended **correcting** to income — but nothing was said about removing one,
by us or by you.

The gap is real rather than tidy-minded: a payslip typed twice is the same mistake as a shop typed
twice, and it has the same property you identified — correcting cannot repair it, because the
entry should not be there at all. Correcting the duplicate to zero would leave *"a zero sitting in
there pretending to be"* an income, which is the thing you refused, and it would need the zero
that `envel add` refuses [src: WI-0001/Q-003].

We are not reading your answer across on your behalf. Removing money from an envelope is the one
direction where a mistake costs you a figure you might not notice, and you have been careful about
that before — *"Nothing should ever be able to wipe money out of an envelope by accident"*
[src: WI-0001/Q-001]. Removing a spend puts money **back**; removing an income takes money
**out**, and one of those is the direction that sentence was about.

There is a practical consequence either way. If an income can be removed, then removing one can
leave an envelope short of what has already been spent from it — the below-zero case you settled
at `WI-0005/Q-004`, arriving from a new direction. We would apply your answer there unchanged:
refuse it, and say how much short it would leave you. That part we will not ask you about again.

## Question

Should `envel remove` be able to remove an income you have already put into an envelope, as well
as a spend?

## Options considered

- **A — Spends only.** Consequence: nothing this tool does can reduce an envelope's balance
  except a spend you recorded on purpose, which is the strongest reading of *"nothing should ever
  be able to wipe money out of an envelope by accident"*. The cost is that a payslip typed twice
  stays typed twice: every envelope it went into is permanently too high, and the only way back is
  a spend you invent to cancel it, which then appears in a monthly summary as money you never
  spent.
- **B — Spends and income, the same command and the same output.** `envel remove 12` says what it
  took out and what the envelope holds now, whichever kind of entry it was. Consequence: a
  duplicate of either kind is fixable, and there is one rule to remember rather than two. The cost
  is that a mistyped reference can now take money out of an envelope, where today it could only
  put money back — so the report the command prints is the thing standing between you and a wrong
  removal.
- **C — Spends and income, but income needs you to say so.** `envel remove 12 --income`, refused
  without the flag. Consequence: the removal that takes money out cannot happen by a
  mistyped reference alone. The cost is a flag that exists to slow you down on a command you asked
  not to be slowed down on — *"Don't make it stop and ask me to confirm"* — and a second way of
  typing the same command.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **B**. Your reason at `Q-003` was about a duplicate entry
  rather than about spends in particular, and the fix for a duplicated payslip is the same fix.
  We prefer it over **C** because you have twice chosen a plain refusal over anything that makes
  you confirm, and the safeguard you did ask for — the command saying what it took out — is
  already in AC11. If you would rather this tool never reduced an envelope except through a spend,
  **A** is a coherent answer and we will record it as your decision rather than as a gap.

## Answer

[human] B — spends and income, one command. You are right that my reason was the duplicate, not
the shop: a payslip entered twice is money I do not have sitting in an envelope, and inventing a
fake spend to cancel it would be worse than the mistake. What I said about not wiping money out by
accident was about an envelope disappearing under me, not about me removing an entry I asked it to
remove — the command telling me what it took out is enough. No flag.
