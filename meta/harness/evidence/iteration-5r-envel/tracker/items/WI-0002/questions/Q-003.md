## Context

`WI-0002`, round 2, question 1 of 3: how you tell the tool the date and the description. Three
questions in all, and they are one conversation about the line you will actually type to record
a spend.

You have already settled *what* a spend carries. At `WI-0002/Q-001` you said the date is today
unless you say otherwise, and at `WI-0002/Q-002` you said a description is optional —
*"I'll put one on the ones I might query later and skip it on the weekly shop."* What nobody has
said is how you write either of them on the command line, and that is the last thing standing
between this item and being buildable.

It matters because the two are both optional and both come after the amount. If they were plain
words in a row, `envel spend groceries 12.50 lunch` and `envel spend groceries 12.50 2026-09-07`
would look the same to the tool and it would have to guess which you meant from the shape of
what you typed. So the tool needs some way to tell them apart, and that way is something you
will type — or deliberately not type — several times a day.

The first three commands (`envel new`, `envel add`, `envel list`) take plain words only, with no
`--` options at all; that was our decision, not yours, and it was made when no command had
anything optional to carry. This is the first one that does.

Two things this question is **not** asking, so you do not have to hold them in your head:
what a date looks like when you write one is question 2 of 3, and whether the tool accepts a
date in the future is question 3 of 3.

## Question

When you record a spend with a date, or with a description, or with both, how would you rather
type them: as named options (`--on`, `--note`), or as extra plain words in a fixed order, or
some mixture?

## Options considered

- **A — Named options for both.** `envel spend groceries 12.50 --on 2026-09-07 --note "lunch"`,
  and `envel spend groceries 12.50` when you want neither. Consequence: you can give either one
  without the other, in any order, and you never have to remember which slot is which. The cost
  is more typing on the occasions you do use them — eight characters before the date, and a word
  like `--note` before the description.
- **B — Extra plain words in a fixed order: date first, then description.**
  `envel spend groceries 12.50 2026-09-07 "lunch"`. Consequence: the shortest thing to type. The
  cost is that a description on its own is awkward — you would have to type today's date in full
  just to get to the description slot — and a mistyped date silently becomes a description.
- **C — A mixture: the description as a plain word, the date as a named option.**
  `envel spend groceries 12.50 "lunch"` and `envel spend groceries 12.50 --on 2026-09-07`, or
  both together. Consequence: the thing you said you would use more often — a description, on
  *"the ones I might query later"* — is the cheap one to type, and the date, which you said is
  the exception rather than the rule, carries the longer spelling. The cost is that the two
  optional things are spelled differently, which is one more thing to remember.
- **D — Something else.** If you already type a tool that does this in a way you like, name it
  and we will match it.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **C**. Your two answers in round 1 point the same way: the
  date is the exception (*"today unless I say otherwise"*) and the description is the one you
  reach for on purpose (*"the ones I might query later"*), so making the description the cheap
  positional and the date the explicit option puts the typing where you said the usage is. We
  hold this loosely — it is a preference about your keystrokes, not about your money, and A is
  the more conventional answer.

## Answer

[human] C. The description is the bit I'll actually type, so make that the cheap one, and put
the longer spelling on the date since I'll hardly ever give one. I don't want to be typing
`--note` on the weekly shop.
