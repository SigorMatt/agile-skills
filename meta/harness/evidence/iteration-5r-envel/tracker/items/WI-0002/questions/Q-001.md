## Context

`WI-0002`, round 1, question 1 of 2: when a spend happened. Two questions in all, and they are
one conversation about recording spending.

You asked for a summary of a month (`WI-0003`), and that only works if every spend has a date
on it. Nobody has said where that date comes from. If it is always the moment you run the
command, then a Saturday's shopping typed in on Monday lands in the wrong week — and if the
month has turned over in between, in the wrong month's summary.

## Question

When I record a spend, should the date always be the moment I run the command, or should I be
able to say when it actually happened?

## Options considered

- **A — Always today.** The tool stamps the spend with the current date and there is nothing to
  type. Consequence: nothing to get wrong and nothing to remember; the cost is that anything you
  enter late is filed on the day you entered it, and `WI-0003`'s monthly summary inherits that
  error with no way to correct it.
- **B — Today by default, and you can give a date.** `--date 2026-09-07`, or whatever it ends
  up being called, and without it the spend is today's. Consequence: typing as you go stays as
  quick as A, and catching up on a week of receipts is still accurate; the cost is one more
  thing the tool has to accept and check.
- **C — The date is always required.** Consequence: the record is never accidentally wrong; the
  cost is that the common case — recording something you just bought — becomes more typing every
  single time.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be B. Catching up on receipts a few days later is the normal
  way this kind of tool gets used, and the cost of allowing it is small.

## Answer

[human] B. Today unless I say otherwise. I will not always sit down with the receipts on the day, and a Saturday shop landing in the wrong month would make the monthly summary wrong.
