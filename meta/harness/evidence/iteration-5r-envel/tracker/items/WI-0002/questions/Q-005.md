## Context

`WI-0002`, round 2, question 3 of 3 — the last of them for now. A date in the future.

Questions 1 and 2 were about how you write a date. This one is about a date the tool can read
perfectly well and might still refuse: you type `2027-09-07` when you meant `2026-09-07`, or you
genuinely want to record a direct debit that leaves your account next Tuesday.

Those two are the same keystrokes and opposite intentions, which is why this is yours rather
than ours. Refusing a future date catches the typo; allowing it lets you record something you
know is coming. Nobody has said which you want, and `WI-0002` `## Notes` has carried the point
since round 1 with the note that guessing it would manufacture a requirement.

What makes it worth a question rather than a shrug is that your money moves either way. At
`EP-001/Q-002` you chose to have an overspend **refused** rather than shown as a negative
envelope — *"I don't want to see a negative envelope"* — so a spend dated next year still comes
out of the envelope today and can push it to the point of refusing the next real spend. And
whichever way this goes, there is no correcting it yet: correcting a recorded spend is `WI-0005`
and is not built.

This is the last question of this round. Questions 1 and 2 are `WI-0002/Q-003` and
`WI-0002/Q-004`.

## Question

If you give a spend a date that is in the future, should the tool refuse it, record it, or
record it and say something?

## Options considered

- **A — Refuse it.** A message saying the date is in the future, nothing recorded, and the
  envelope unchanged. Consequence: a mistyped year is caught the moment you type it, which is
  the only moment it is cheap to catch, since there is no way to correct a recorded spend until
  `WI-0005` is built. The cost is that you cannot record something you know is coming, and you
  would have to remember to enter it on the day.
- **B — Record it like any other spend.** Consequence: you can enter a direct debit or a
  standing order the moment you know about it, and the envelope reflects money that is as good as
  gone. The cost is that a typo in the year is accepted in silence, the money leaves the envelope
  now, and `WI-0003`'s summary of this month will not show it because it belongs to a month that
  has not happened.
- **C — Record it, and print a line saying the date is in the future.** Consequence: both of the
  above — the deliberate case works, and the typo is visible at the moment you make it. The cost
  is a line of output you will learn to skim past, which is what usually happens to warnings.
- **D — Refuse it unless you say you meant it**, with something like `--even-though-future`.
  Consequence: the typo is caught and the deliberate case is still possible. The cost is another
  option on a command we are otherwise trying to keep short, for a case you may never have.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **A**, on the strength of what you have already chosen
  elsewhere: you asked for an overspend to be refused rather than shown, which says you would
  rather the tool stop you than record something you did not mean. If recording future spends
  turns out to be something you actually want, it is a small change later and nothing already
  recorded has to move. That is our reading of your preference, though, not something you have
  said — if you do pay by direct debit and want them in as they come up, say so and we will
  build B or C.

## Answer

[human] A — refuse it. I record money that has gone, not money that is going to go; a direct
debit gets entered the day it leaves. Same reasoning as the overspend: stop me rather than take
something I didn't mean.
