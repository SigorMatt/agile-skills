## Context

`WI-0006`, round 1, question 4 of 4, and the last of them — that is all we need from you on this
item for now. **If you closed the item at question 1, ignore this one too.**

This is the question we think matters most if you do want the command, because it is the one
where your own answers point hardest in one direction and the item as drafted points the other
way.

The item is titled *"Look up the **spends** recorded against an envelope"*, and its `## Out of
scope` currently rules out listing income and moves. We wrote that, not you.

But an envelope's balance is not made of spends. It is income in, spends out, and moves in and
out — you asked for moves at `EP-001/Q-005` and they are built. So a listing of `groceries` that
shows only spends is a list of some of the things that changed its balance, and the lines will
not add up to the number `envel list` shows you.

That is the exact thing you objected to at `WI-0003/Q-001`, deciding how moves appear in the
monthly summary: *"I won't use a report whose rows don't add up, I've spent years chasing that in
the spreadsheet. A column of zeros costs me nothing; a number I can't account for costs me an
evening."* You chose, there, to have moves visible even where the figure was zero, rather than
have a report you could not reconcile.

We are not going to apply that sentence to this command on your behalf. It was an answer about
the monthly summary, and this is a different report with a different job — a list of spends you
are hunting through is not obviously a thing that should balance. But it is close enough that
deciding it silently would be us choosing for you.

## Question

Should this command list only the spends, or everything that moved the envelope — income and
moves as well — so that the lines account for its balance?

## Options considered

- **A — Spends only.** Consequence: the output is exactly the thing you go looking for, and
  every line is the same kind of thing, so it is easy to read. The cost is that it does not
  explain the balance: add the lines up and you will not get the number `envel list` prints, and
  by your own account that is the sort of gap that costs you an evening.
- **B — Everything that moved the envelope, each line saying what kind it is.** Income, spends
  and moves, in date order, so the lines account for the balance. Consequence: the report
  reconciles, which is the property you said you would not do without. The cost is that finding
  a spend means reading past income and moves, and the command stops being the simple thing its
  title describes.
- **C — Everything, with a running balance down the right-hand side.** As B, and each line shows
  what the envelope held after it. Consequence: the strongest form of "the rows add up" — you can
  see exactly where a balance came from. The cost is the most output per line, and a running
  balance only makes sense when the listing starts from the beginning, which conflicts with
  showing a single month.
- **D — Spends only by default, everything if you ask for it.** `envel spends groceries` for
  spends; `envel spends groceries --all` for the lot. Consequence: the common case stays simple
  and the reconciling case is available. The cost is two behaviours to learn for one command,
  and the default is the one that does not add up.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **B**, on the strength of your `WI-0003/Q-001` answer — you
  have already told us once that you would rather see a line that reconciles than a shorter
  report you cannot account for, and the reasoning you gave was not specific to the summary. We
  are recommending it while telling you plainly that it makes this item bigger than its title,
  and that if what you want is just to read back the descriptions you have been typing, **A** is
  the smaller and more honest thing and the reconciling view can be its own item later.

## Answer

[human] B — everything that moved the envelope, with each line saying which kind it is. Yes, that
rule travels: a list I cannot add up costs me an evening whichever report it is on. I don't need
a running balance down the side.
