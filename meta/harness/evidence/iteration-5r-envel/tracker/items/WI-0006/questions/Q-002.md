## Context

`WI-0006`, round 1, question 2 of 4. One conversation about a command that lists an envelope's
spends. **If you closed the item at question 1, ignore this one.**

Assuming you want it: how far back does it go? This is the question that decides whether the
command is useful or whether it turns into the thing you are trying to get away from.

You told us at `EP-001/Q-001` that today you keep *"a spreadsheet with a tab per month"*, and at
`EP-001/Q-004` that you look at a month *"a couple of days after it has ended"*. Both of those
say your unit of attention is a month. But an envelope's balance is not a monthly figure — at
`EP-001/Q-001` you were clear that *"money left in an envelope at the end of a month stays in
that envelope; rolling over is the whole point"* — so the spends that explain a balance can
stretch back over several months.

After a year of use, `groceries` might have three or four hundred spends in it. A command that
prints all of them is a page you scroll past; a command that prints one month's is a command you
sometimes have to run three times to find what you are after.

## Question

When you list an envelope's spends, which ones should it show: all of them, or the ones in a
month you name?

## Options considered

- **A — All the spends in that envelope, oldest first.** `envel spends groceries`. Consequence:
  one command, nothing to remember, and everything is there. The cost grows with use: after a
  year it is hundreds of lines and you would be reaching for `grep` or scrollback to find
  anything, which is the sort of thing you said you were leaving behind.
- **B — A month's spends, the current month by default, with a named month like the summary.**
  `envel spends groceries` for this month, `envel spends groceries 2026-08` for August.
  Consequence: it reads exactly like `envel summary` — same spelling of a month, same default —
  and the output is always short enough to take in. The cost is that finding a spend whose month
  you have forgotten means running it two or three times.
- **C — The most recent ones, a fixed number of them.** `envel spends groceries` shows the last
  twenty. Consequence: always short, and the most recent spend is the one most likely to be the
  mistake you are hunting. The cost is a number nobody chose that is wrong for somebody —
  and older spends become unreachable, which makes a month you are checking against a statement
  the one thing you cannot see.
- **D — All of them by default, with a month you can name to narrow it.** A and B together:
  `envel spends groceries` prints everything, `envel spends groceries 2026-08` narrows it.
  Consequence: nothing is unreachable and the common case is one command. The cost is that the
  default is the long output, which is the one that gets worse the longer you use the tool.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **B**. It matches how you have described using this tool —
  a month at a time, checked against a statement — and it is the only option where the output
  stays the same size in year three as in month one. It also means the two commands that take a
  month spell it the same way, which is the consistency you asked for at `WI-0003/Q-004`. If
  what you actually want is to hunt for one spend without knowing when it was, **D** is the
  honest answer and it is barely more work.

## Answer

[human] B — a month at a time, this month unless I name one, and named the same way as the
summary. I work a month at a time and I don't want a command whose output gets longer every year
I use it.
