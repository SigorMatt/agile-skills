## Context

`WI-0004`, round 1, question 1 of 1 — the only one this round, and the only thing on this item we
could not settle from what you have already told us.

`WI-0004` is the moving-money command you asked for at `EP-001/Q-005`: *"Moving money between
envelopes is normal practice and I will need it the moment an envelope runs short, which is the
same week I start using this."* Most of what it needs is already decided by things you have said.
The overspend rule from `EP-001/Q-002` says a move bigger than the source envelope holds is
refused. Your answer at `WI-0003/Q-001` says a move is kept out of the *spent* column of the
monthly summary and shown as its own fourth figure — *"'Spent' has to mean money that left the
house, so moves don't belong in it"*. The rest of it is the same shapes this tool already has.

The one thing that is genuinely open is **when** a move happened, and we cannot work it out from
what you have said, because you have answered this question twice and given opposite answers —
both with good reasons about your own habits.

For a **spend**, at `WI-0002/Q-001`, you said it can be back-dated:

> Today unless I say otherwise. I will not always sit down with the receipts on the day, and a
> Saturday shop landing in the wrong month would make the monthly summary wrong.

For **income**, at `WI-0003/Q-003`, you said it cannot:

> I put income in when it arrives — that's the one thing I'm never late on, because it's the bit
> I enjoy. I'm not paying for rework on a command that already works for a case I don't have.

A move is a third kind of event and the two answers point opposite ways, so guessing would be us
deciding which of your own habits this one resembles. It matters for the same reason the spend
one did: the summary's fourth column is *"the money moved into or out of it during that month"*
[src: WI-0003 AC2 "the money moved into or out of it during that month"], so a move's date is
what decides which month's report it lands in.

There is a specific case where it could bite. You described moving money as what you do when an
envelope runs short, and then recording the spend [src: EP-001/Q-002]. If the spend you are
covering is one you back-dated — Saturday's shop typed in on Monday — then the move you make to
cover it is being made on Monday for something that happened on Saturday. Inside one month that
changes nothing. Across a month boundary, the spend lands in one month and the move that funded
it lands in the next, and the two rows will not tell the same story.

Two things this question is **not** asking, because we have settled them and recorded them as
ours rather than putting them to you. A move will carry **no description**: nothing anywhere in
this project would ever show one back — `WI-0006` is scoped to looking up *spends* — so it would
be a note you could write and never read, and adding it later costs one optional word on the
line. And moving zero or a negative amount will be refused, on the rule you set for income at
`WI-0001/Q-003` and which `envel spend` already follows. Both are in the item's `## Notes` with
where a disagreement about them lands.

That is all of them for now.

## Question

Should a move be datable the way a spend is — `envel move groceries fun 20 --on 2026-08-28`,
today unless you say otherwise — or should it always be dated the moment you type it, the way
income is?

## Options considered

- **A — Datable, exactly like a spend.** `--on YYYY-MM-DD`, defaulting to today, refusing a date
  in the future and a date it cannot read, all identical to `envel spend`. Consequence: a move
  made on Monday to cover a Saturday spend can be put in the same month as that spend, so the two
  rows agree. It also means one rule for both of the commands that take money out of an envelope,
  which is one less thing to remember. The cost is a little more work in this item, and a second
  place where you can put a date in wrong.
- **B — Always dated when typed, like income.** No `--on`, nothing extra to type or learn.
  Consequence: the cheapest option and the smallest command, and it matches what you said about
  doing moves *"the moment an envelope runs short"* — in which case the date you type it is the
  date it happened. The cost is the month-boundary case above: a move covering a back-dated spend
  falls in the month you typed it, with no way to say otherwise, and there is also no way to
  correct it afterwards, because `WI-0005` is scoped to spends.
- **C — Always dated when typed, and revisit it if it bites.** The same build as B, with this
  recorded as a known limitation rather than as an oversight — which is what you chose for income
  at `WI-0003/Q-003`: *"if it turns out to bite me after a couple of months, I'll tell you then."*
  Consequence: identical to B today, and the difference is only in the record — anyone who later
  finds a move in the wrong month is looking at a decision you made rather than at a bug, and the
  route back is you telling us.
- **Recommendation:** ours would be **A**, and we say so knowing it is the more expensive one.
  The reason is not symmetry for its own sake: it is that a move and a spend are two halves of the
  same action in the story you told us — the envelope runs short, you move money in, you record
  the spend — and you have already told us the spend half can be back-dated because you *"will
  not always sit down with the receipts on the day"*. If the spend can be late, the move that
  covers it can be late too. This is our reading of how you described using it rather than
  something you have said: if in practice you always move money at the moment you need it and
  never afterwards, B or C is smaller and we will build it.

## Answer

[human] A — datable, same as a spend. You've put your finger on it: I move the money because of a
shop I'm typing in late, so the move is late too. The thing that settles it for me is that I can
fix a spend afterwards and I can't fix a move, so I need to be able to get it right when I type it.
