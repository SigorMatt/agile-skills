## Context

WI-0002's verification passed all fourteen criteria against branch head
`a8b5a4bb2b2c5ecd4baed6bb959b88233d7ef80a`. This is the only question filed from it, and it is
not about whether the code is right — it is.

AC14 contains two clauses that cannot both hold:

- "every acceptance criterion of WI-0001 still holds on documents containing alignment markers,
  **except** the single clause of its AC12 that fixes a cell's padding to the right of its
  content, which AC2, AC3 and AC4 above supersede"; and
- "Checked by running WI-0001's shipped test suite unchanged".

WI-0001's shipped suite encodes the excepted clause in exactly two places, and both are reached
by the fixture `basic-ascii`, whose delimiter row is `|---|:---|---:|` — so its `id` column is
right-aligned under WI-0002 AC3:

1. `tests/fixtures/basic-ascii.out.md`, whose expected body rows read `| 1  |` and `| 2  |`;
2. `tests/test_fixtures.py`, `test_ac12_every_cell_has_exactly_one_space_against_each_pipe`,
   whose `self.assertFalse(cell[1:-1].startswith(" "))` is literally "the padding is to the right
   of the content".

Before `implement` touched either, running the suite against the new renderer gave three
failures, all from that one fixture:

```
FAIL: test_every_fixture_produces_its_expected_output (fixture='basic-ascii')
AssertionError: b'...| Ada   | engineer     |  1 |\n...' != b'...| Ada   | engineer     | 1  |\n...'
FAIL: test_ac12_every_cell_has_exactly_one_space_against_each_pipe
AssertionError: True is not false : '  1 '
FAIL: test_ac1_python3_m_mdtab_writes_the_document_and_exits_zero
AssertionError: b'...|  1 |\n...' != b'...| 1  |\n...'
```

`implement` changed both places — the fixture's two cells, and the assertion, which was narrowed
to the two columns AC2 still governs rather than deleted — and recorded it as deviation 1 in
`impl-report.md`, flagging it as the one judgement a reviewer should check. WI-0001's `item.md`
was not edited, which AC14 also requires and which holds.

This verification re-checked AC14's **substance** independently, with its own documents rather
than the suite: all fifteen of WI-0001's criteria hold on documents carrying markers — exit 0
and empty stderr (AC1), equal row display widths including CJK and combining marks (AC2, AC3),
non-table lines byte-identical (AC4, AC5), idempotence on nine documents including AC10's and
AC11's (AC6), all four recognition rules refusing what they refused before (AC7, AC13, AC14,
AC15), fenced blocks untouched (AC8), CRLF and a missing final newline and an undecodable byte
preserved (AC9), escaped pipes intact (AC10), and cell content unchanged apart from the spaces
around it (AC11). AC12's surviving parts — the two guard spaces, the `2 + max(...)` width, the
two idempotence qualifications, the delimiter-row filling and the outer-pipe rule — were each
checked separately and hold. So AC14 was recorded as `pass` on its substance.

What cannot be recorded as satisfied is its literal second clause. And `verify` may not amend an
acceptance criterion: the criteria are the standard it judges against, and a verifier that edits
the standard to fit what it measured has verified nothing.

This is the same shape as `WI-0001/Q-005`, filed from that item's verification for the same
reason — a criterion carrying two clauses that could not both hold — and answered from the
record by amending the wording. Hence `blocking: false`: nothing about WI-0002 is blocked, the
verification stands and the item proceeds to `in-review`. But `next` dispatches
`answer-questions` on an open architect question (step 4) before it dispatches a status owner
(step 5), so the amendment lands before `review-close` runs, which is where D2 would otherwise
read a criterion the delivered work does not literally meet.

## Question

How should AC14's checking clause be worded, so that it and AC14's own exception clause are
jointly satisfiable — given that two places in WI-0001's shipped suite encode precisely the
clause AC14 excepts — without changing behaviour that this verification has already confirmed?

## Options considered

- **A — Amend the checking clause to say the suite runs unchanged apart from the excepted
  clause, and name the two places.** For example: "Checked by running WI-0001's shipped test
  suite, which passes unchanged apart from the two places that encode the superseded clause —
  `tests/fixtures/basic-ascii.out.md`'s `---:` column and the padding-position assertion in
  `test_ac12_every_cell_has_exactly_one_space_against_each_pipe` — both of which are updated to
  the placement AC3 requires, with WI-0001's `item.md` left untouched." Consequence: AC14 becomes
  satisfiable and describes exactly what was delivered; the exception it already states is
  carried through to the evidence recipe; no code, test or fixture changes. Cost: the criterion
  gets longer and names two file paths.

- **B — Drop the "unchanged" word only.** "Checked by running WI-0001's shipped test suite."
  Consequence: shortest possible amendment, and it stops the contradiction. But it also stops
  saying anything about *how much* of that suite may move, which is the guarantee the clause was
  there to give: a future item could rewrite half of WI-0001's evidence and still satisfy AC14.

- **C — Require the two places to be reverted and the excepted clause re-encoded elsewhere.**
  Keep `basic-ascii.out.md` as WI-0001 shipped it and give `basic-ascii` a delimiter row with no
  markers, moving the `---:` case to a WI-0002 fixture. Consequence: WI-0001's suite really would
  run unchanged. But `basic-ascii.in.md` would have to change to make its expected output
  correct, so a WI-0001 fixture is edited either way — and the edited one would be the *input*,
  which is a larger change than the two cells this option exists to avoid. It also deletes the
  only fixture that exercises a marker in a table that predates this item.

- **D — Leave AC14 as it is and record the exception in the report only.** It is already in
  `impl-report.md`'s deviation 1 and in this verification's report. Consequence: no work. But the
  criterion stays in `item.md` as the standard a re-verification would judge against, and it says
  something the delivered work does not literally satisfy — which is the situation `WI-0001/Q-005`
  was filed to end, in this same workspace, ten history rows ago.

- **Recommendation: A.** It is the only option that changes no behaviour and no test while making
  the criterion true of what was delivered, and it keeps the guarantee B gives up. C costs more
  editing of WI-0001's fixtures than it saves, and D leaves a known-false criterion in the record
  after the project has already decided once that the wording is what should change
  (`WI-0001/Q-005`, and `review.md`'s "the wording of AC12 is what should change, not the code").

## Answer

**Option A.** AC14's checking clause now reads:

> Checked by running WI-0001's shipped test suite, which passes apart from the two places that
> encode the superseded clause and are therefore updated to the placement AC3 requires:
> `tests/fixtures/basic-ascii.out.md`, whose `id` column is marked `---:`, and the
> padding-position assertion in `test_ac12_every_cell_has_exactly_one_space_against_each_pipe`,
> which is narrowed to the columns AC2 still governs rather than removed. Nothing else in that
> suite changes.

Answered from the record, not decided fresh. Every element of the amendment is already written
down somewhere authoritative:

- **The exception is AC14's own.** Its first half already says "**except** the single clause of
  its AC12 that fixes a cell's padding to the right of its content, which AC2, AC3 and AC4 above
  supersede". The two places named are that clause, in the only two forms the suite states it in.
  The amendment carries an exception the criterion already makes into the sentence that says how
  to check it — which is why this is route 1 of `spec/question.md` §3, an answer from an existing
  document, and not a fresh architectural decision. No ADR was written, because none is needed:
  ADR-0007 §Decision 2 already fixes where the padding goes.
- **Which places, and that there are only two, is verified rather than asserted.**
  `git diff main -- tests/fixtures/basic-ascii.out.md tests/test_fixtures.py` is the whole of it:
  two cells in that fixture, one assertion narrowed by adding an `alignments` guard, and the
  additive registration of the new fixtures in `ALIGNED` and `UNTOUCHED`. Nothing else in
  WI-0001's suite differs from `main`.
- **The substance was checked before the wording was touched.** `verify-report.md`'s AC14 row
  re-checks all fifteen of WI-0001's criteria on documents carrying markers, with its own
  commands, and every one holds. That is the evidence for the amended clause; the amendment does
  not create the pass, it stops the criterion contradicting itself about how the pass is shown.
- **The project has decided this once already, the same way.** `WI-0001/Q-005` was the same shape
  — a criterion carrying two clauses that could not both hold — and was answered by amending the
  wording, on `review.md`'s finding that *"the wording of AC12 is what should change, not the
  code"*.

**Why the other options were not taken.** B (delete the word "unchanged") stops the contradiction
but gives up the guarantee the clause exists for: it would let a later item rewrite any amount of
WI-0001's evidence and still satisfy AC14. C (revert the two places and move the `---:` case to a
new fixture) would require editing `basic-ascii.in.md` to keep its expected output correct, so a
WI-0001 fixture changes either way — and the one C edits is the input, a larger change than the
two output cells it exists to avoid. D (leave it, record it in reports only) is what
`WI-0001/Q-005` was filed to stop happening a second time.

**No escalation.** None of the four conditions in `spec/question.md` §4 applies: the intent is
recorded — in AC14's own exception clause and in the stakeholder's *"whatever the marker says,
that's where the text sits in the cell — every row, every column, no exceptions"*
(`WI-0002/Q-001`); the change is a sentence and is reversible; it contradicts no ADR; and the
record is not silent.

**This is an amendment to an acceptance criterion, made after the work was built, and that is
worth naming plainly.** What it changes is a *check*, not a *requirement*: no clause about what
the tool must do was touched, no behaviour, test or fixture changed as a result of the amendment,
and the two places it names had already been updated by `implement` — declared as its deviation 1
— and independently re-verified by `verify` before the question was filed. Had the amendment
altered what the tool must do, the right move would have been to escalate to the stakeholder
rather than to write it.

**The verification stands.** `verify-report.md`'s AC14 row and its `## Not verified, and why`
section are the evidence for the amended clause, and AC14 stays ticked. No other criterion was
touched.

**Scope was not widened, so no new item was filed.** The amendment implies no work: the delivered
code already does what the amended text says.
