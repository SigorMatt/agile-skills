## Question

When `tidy` sorts by type **and** age, what should the resulting folder tree look like?

Take a folder holding `holiday.jpg` (edited last week), `taxes-2019.pdf` (untouched for years),
and `old-photo.jpg` (untouched for years).

## Options considered

- **A — Type first, then age inside it.** `images/recent/holiday.jpg`,
  `images/old/old-photo.jpg`, `documents/old/taxes-2019.pdf`. Consequence: everything of one
  kind stays together, and "show me all my images" is still one folder. This is the smallest
  change to what WI-0001 already built — the top level looks exactly as it does today, and the
  age split appears one level down. Cost: every type folder gains sub-folders, so a folder with
  a handful of files ends up with more folders than files.
- **B — Age first, then type inside it.** `recent/images/holiday.jpg`,
  `old/images/old-photo.jpg`, `old/documents/taxes-2019.pdf`. Consequence: the top level answers
  "what is still live?" at a glance, and you could archive or delete the whole `old/` folder in
  one action. This is the strongest reading of your story — "the things I am still working with
  stay easy to find and the rest is put away". Cost: your files of one kind are now split across
  two or more top-level folders, so "all my images" is no longer one place.
- **C — Age as an override: recent files sort by type, old files all go to one `archive/`
  folder.** `images/holiday.jpg`, `archive/old-photo.jpg`, `archive/taxes-2019.pdf`.
  Consequence: the simplest possible tree, and the clearest "put away" — old things leave the
  type structure entirely. Cost: `archive/` becomes an unsorted heap, which is the problem you
  started with, just relocated.
- **D — Something else you have in mind.** If you already sort a folder a particular way by hand,
  describe it and we will match it rather than inventing one.
- **Recommendation: B.** Your story is written from the point of view of putting things away —
  "the things I am still working with stay easy to find and the rest is put away" — and B is the
  only option where "the rest" is a single place you can act on. A is the safer, smaller change
  and is a perfectly good answer if you would rather keep each kind of file together. C is the
  one we would advise against: it recreates an unsorted folder under a new name.

## Answer

[human] B — old stuff goes in its own place, out of the way. That's kind of the whole point:
I want to look at the top level and know what's actually live, not go hunting inside every
type folder for the recent one.

Recorded as given: **option B — the age band at the top level, the type folder inside it.** The
three example files become `recent/images/holiday.jpg`, `old/images/old-photo.jpg` and
`old/documents/taxes-2019.pdf`.

Their reason is recorded alongside the choice because it constrains more than the choice does.
"Look at the top level and know what's actually live" makes the band folders **the** top level of
a tidied folder: no type folder may sit above a band folder, and moving the age split one level
down later — option A — would take away the thing they asked for rather than refine it. `refine`
should treat that as a property of the layout, not as a preference between two equally acceptable
trees.

One consequence neither the option list nor the recommendation drew out, and it is now on
WI-0002's `## Notes` for `refine` to give either a criterion or an out-of-scope entry: **WI-0001
already shipped the type folders at the top level**, so a folder that was tidied before this item
lands and tidied again afterwards ends up holding both `documents/` and `old/documents/`. Nothing
is lost and nothing is overwritten — existing subfolders are left alone, which is a closed
decision [src: EP-001/Q-003; src: WI-0001 AC11] — but the tree is mixed, and whether WI-0002 does
anything about it is a thing this item must state rather than discover during verification. It is
not escalated: the stakeholder has already decided both the layout and that subfolders are left
alone, and what remains is which of those two closed decisions this item writes down.
