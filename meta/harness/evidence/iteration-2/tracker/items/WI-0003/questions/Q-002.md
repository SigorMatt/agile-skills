## Context

**WI-0003, round 1, question 2 of 2, and that is all of them for now.** See Q-001 for the frame:
both are about how much of the tool's sorting your own rule file controls. Q-001 is about the type
folders; this one is about the age side, and they are separate because the answers do not have to
match — you could hand over the type table completely and keep the age behaviour fixed, or the
other way round.

When you chose the age behaviour you said: "Keep it simple — two bands, recent and old, split at a
year. Anything I haven't touched in a year is old" [src: WI-0002/Q-002]. That is what the tool does
now: `recent/` for a file modified less than 365 days ago, `old/` for 365 days or more, and
`README.md` says in as many words "There are two bands and no others" [src: README.md].

WI-0002 was built to that decision and put two things on its out-of-scope list as belonging to
*this* item: "user-supplied age boundaries and user-supplied band names"
[src: tracker/items/WI-0002/item.md `## Out of scope`]. So renaming `recent`/`old` and moving the
one-year line are already promised to you here, and are not in question.

What is in question is the thing nobody has promised either way: whether you may change **how
many** bands there are. WI-0002 also excluded "more than two bands" — but that exclusion was about
what *it* would build by default, and it says nothing about what your own rule file may ask for. It
is being put to you rather than decided here because it is a decision about how much of the tool's
shape is yours, and because it changes something you can see: under one answer the top level of a
tidied folder is always exactly two directories, and under another it is whatever you wrote.

## Question

How far does your control over the age side go — may you only rename the two bands and move the
one-year line, or may your rule file decide how many bands there are at all?

## Options considered

- **A — Two bands, always; you choose their names and where the line falls.** You could write
  "`current` under 90 days, `archive` after" and get `current/` and `archive/` at the top level.
  You could not have three. Consequence: the tool's shape is fixed and every tidied folder looks
  the same at the top level, whoever's rules produced it; `README.md` keeps its "two bands and no
  others" sentence with the numbers made yours. Cost: if a year turns out to be too blunt — say
  you want this-year, last-few-years and ancient — you cannot have it without another piece of
  work.
- **B — Any number of bands, named and bounded by you.** Your file gives an ordered list: a name
  and the age at which each one ends, with the last one open. One band means no age routing in
  practice (everything under one folder); four means four. Consequence: the age side is genuinely
  yours, and the two-band default stays exactly as it is for anyone who writes nothing. Cost: more
  ways to write a rule file wrong — bands out of order, an overlapping boundary, a missing
  open-ended last band — so more error cases to define and more to explain in `README.md`. Worth
  knowing: the code already stores the bands as an ordered list of name-and-boundary pairs, chosen
  in ADR-0005 precisely so this would be cheap when it came [src: ADR-0005; src: tidy/rules.py], so
  this is a smaller step than it sounds.
- **C — B, and you may also turn age routing off entirely.** Everything in B, plus a way to say
  "no bands" and get the flat layout the tool had before age existed — `documents/`, `images/` and
  so on at the top level, no `recent/` or `old/` above them. Consequence: the fullest control, and
  it gives anyone who never wanted bands a way back. Cost: a third layout the tool must produce,
  preview and be tested against, and one more thing that can be misread from a rule file. Note it
  does **not** re-file anything you already tidied — subfolders are left alone [src: EP-001/Q-003]
  — so switching would leave you with both shapes on disk until you moved things yourself.
- **Recommendation: B.** It is what your own reasoning asks for: "anything I haven't touched in a
  year is old" is a statement about your files, and the item exists because your folder does not
  look like anybody else's. ADR-0005 already shaped the code so that a list of bands costs little
  more than two, and the two-band default is untouched for anyone who supplies nothing — so "keep
  it simple" stays true of the tool you get out of the box.

  Say **A** if you would rather every tidied folder look the same at the top level and would
  rather not think about it again, and **C** if being able to switch age routing off matters to
  you. There is no wrong answer here; A is the smallest, C is the largest.

## Answer

[human] A — keep it at two bands. Recent and old is all I need, don't want to think about more
than that.
