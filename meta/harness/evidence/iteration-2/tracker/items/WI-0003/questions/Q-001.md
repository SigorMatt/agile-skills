## Context

**WI-0003, round 1, question 1 of 2: what your rules do to the built-in ones.** (Two questions in
this round, both about how much of the tool's sorting your own rule file controls. The second is
about the age side. Answer both and WI-0003 can be made Ready — it is the last of the three items,
and you asked that none of them be left hanging [src: EP-001/Q-004].)

You can run the tool today. `python3 -m tidy <folder>` previews every move; `--apply` carries them
out. A file lands at `<band>/<type>/<name>` — `recent/` or `old/` chosen by how old it is, and one
of seven type folders chosen from its extension. The seven folders and the extensions in each are
listed in `README.md` under "Where each file goes"; a file whose extension is in none of them is
left where it is and reported on a `leave` line, never swept into a catch-all folder
[src: README.md].

This item makes those rules yours instead of ours. Your story for it says you want the tool to
sort "the way I want rather than the way its author guessed". What nobody has decided is what
happens to our seven folders when you supply your own file, and it is not a detail — it decides
whether a two-line rule file is useful or dangerous.

Concretely. Suppose our table sends `.csv` to `spreadsheets/`, and you write a file whose entire
content is "`.csv` goes to `data/`". Afterwards, where does `holiday.jpg` go?

Intake deliberately left this open and wrote it into the item as undecided
[src: tracker/items/WI-0003/item.md `## Notes`]. It is being put to you rather than decided here
because it changes what you would see: under one answer that rule file tidies your whole folder
with one line changed, and under another it leaves almost every file untouched with a `leave` line.

## Question

When you supply your own rules, do they **replace** our built-in ones entirely, or do they sit on
top of them — ours still applying wherever yours say nothing?

## Options considered

- **A — Replace.** Your file is the whole rule set. If it names only `.csv`, then `.csv` files
  move and everything else is reported `leave  holiday.jpg   [no rule for '.jpg']`. Consequence:
  completely predictable — what you see in your file is exactly what the tool does, and nothing
  invisible affects the outcome. Cost: to change one extension you must retype all seven folders
  and roughly seventy extensions, and if you forget one, files you expected to be filed are
  silently left instead. The preview would show you that before anything moved, but it is still an
  easy mistake to make repeatedly.
- **B — Layer, with your entries winning.** Ours apply, yours override any extension you name and
  add any we do not have. The one-line file above sends `.csv` to `data/` and leaves everything
  else exactly as it is today. Consequence: a small file does a small, obvious thing, which is
  what most people want most of the time. Cost: the rules actually in force are your file *plus*
  a table in `README.md`, so your file alone does not tell you the whole answer — and under this
  option there is no way to *remove* one of our mappings, only to redirect it. If you want `.md`
  files left alone rather than filed under `documents/`, B as stated cannot express that.
- **C — Layer by default, replace on request.** B's behaviour normally, with a way to say "this
  file is the whole truth" — a flag when you run it, or a line at the top of the rule file.
  Consequence: both behaviours available, and you pick per rule file. Cost: two behaviours to
  build, document and test, and one more thing to remember; it is the most work of the three and
  it is only worth it if you expect to want both.
- **Recommendation: B.** Your stated reason for wanting this is that your folder does not look
  like anybody else's — which usually means a handful of extensions we got wrong, not seven
  categories you would draw differently. B makes that a two-line file. It also fits how you have
  answered before: "keep it simple", and the safety net is that the preview shows you the real
  destinations before anything moves [src: WI-0002/Q-002; src: EP-001/Q-003].

  If you take B, `refine` will put "removing a built-in mapping" on this item's out-of-scope list
  explicitly, so that it is a known gap rather than a surprise. Say **A** if you would rather the
  file in front of you be the complete answer, and **C** if you genuinely want both.

## Answer

[human] B — sit on top, mine win. I don't want to retype your whole seven-folder list just to
move one extension somewhere else.
