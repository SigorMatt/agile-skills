## Context

WI-0003 is "import expenses from my bank's CSV export instead of me typing them in". I cannot
write acceptance criteria for it, and `refine` cannot make it Ready, because two facts are
missing and neither can be derived from anything in this workspace or from your statement of the
idea. This is not a choice between options I could reason my way through — it is a missing fact
plus a decision only you can make.

**The missing fact:** I do not know what your bank's CSV actually looks like. Banks disagree on
everything — whether there is a header row, the column names and order, comma vs semicolon,
`31/01/2026` vs `2026-01-31`, whether money out is `-42.50` or `42.50` in a separate debit
column, whether amounts carry a currency symbol or thousands separators. A parser written against
a guess will fail on your first real file.

**The decision:** a bank row knows a date, an amount and a merchant. It does not know **who
shared the expense**, and it only implicitly knows who paid — presumably you, since it is your
account. Something has to supply the missing information, and what that something is changes what
the import command looks like.

There is also a consequence for WI-0001: if every bank row carries a date, expenses probably need
a date field, which hand-entered expenses would then need too.

## Question

Please paste 3–5 real lines from your bank's CSV export, including the header row if there is one
(you can change the merchant names and amounts — I need the *shape*, not your spending). And say
which of the options below describes how an imported row should become a shared expense.

## Options considered

- **A — everything imported is paid by you and shared by everyone currently recorded.** Consequence:
  the simplest possible import, one command, no extra input. Wrong for any transaction that only
  some of the group shared, and wrong for anything personal that is in the same export — you would
  be importing your own groceries as a group expense and then having no way to remove them, since
  WI-0001 has no delete.
- **B — you name the payer and the sharers once, for the whole import.** Consequence: one command
  with a couple of flags, e.g. import this file, paid by me, shared by Ana, Ben and me. Handles a
  weekend away, where one export section is genuinely all-group. Still imports every row in the
  file, so a mixed export needs trimming first — which is the hand-editing you said you did not
  want to do.
- **C — the import asks you row by row: include this one, and who shared it?** Consequence: handles
  a mixed export honestly and needs no pre-trimming. It is interactive, so it is slower for a large
  file, harder to test, and closer to "typing them in" than the other two.
- **D — the CSV itself carries the sharing**, because you add columns to it before import.
  Consequence: precise and scriptable, and the import stays a pure file-in operation. But it means
  editing the export by hand every time, which is what WI-0003 exists to avoid.
- **Recommendation:** B, with a way of limiting which rows are taken (a date range, or a match on
  the description) so a mixed export does not have to be trimmed by hand. It is one non-interactive
  command, it is testable, and it does not assume every line of your statement is a group expense.
  I would not choose A — it is the cheapest to build and the one most likely to put wrong data into
  a store that cannot yet delete anything.
