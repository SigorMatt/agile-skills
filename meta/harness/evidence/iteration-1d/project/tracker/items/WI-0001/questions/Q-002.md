## Context

The second reason AC3 is not yet decidable: it says "an amount" and nothing else. Two things
about amounts have to be pinned down before anyone can write a test for this item, and both are
choices rather than facts I can look up.

**What you type.** Whether `12.5`, `12.50`, `€12.50` and `1,234.56` are all accepted, and what
happens to `abc`, `-5` and `0`. `verify` will be checking exactly these and cannot ask anyone.

**The remainder.** €10 shared by three people is €3.3333… Something must absorb the last cent, and
whatever it is has to be stated, because otherwise WI-0002's report will not balance and nobody
will be able to say whether that is a bug. This is the classic place a shared-expenses tool goes
quietly wrong.

The epic already assumes one currency throughout, with no currency named (`EP-001/item.md`,
`## Notes`) — that assumption is not what I am asking about here.

## Question

How should amounts be written when recording an expense, and when a shared amount does not divide
evenly among its sharers, who absorbs the leftover minor units?

## Options considered

On what is accepted:

- **A — a plain decimal number, at most two decimal places, no symbol and no thousands
  separator.** `12.5` and `12.50` both mean twelve euros fifty; `€12.50`, `12.505`, `1,234.56`,
  `0` and anything negative are refused with a message and a non-zero exit. Consequence: one
  simple rule, easy to test, and it will reject a paste from your bank export that carries a
  symbol — which WI-0003 will have to strip.
- **B — accept a leading currency symbol and thousands separators as well**, and normalise them.
  Consequence: friendlier, especially for anything copied out of a statement; a longer list of
  accepted forms, and more places for WI-0003's parser and this command to disagree.

On the remainder:

- **C — the payer absorbs it.** €10 among three: two people owe €3.33 and the payer covers €3.34.
  Consequence: the tool never invents money; the person who fronted the cash eats at most a couple
  of cents, which is what most people do in practice anyway.
- **D — spread the extra cents over the first sharers in a fixed order** (alphabetical, say), so
  someone owes €3.34 and two owe €3.33. Consequence: fairer across many expenses, and it means the
  amount a given person owes depends on their name, which is surprising when you first see it.
- **E — refuse an amount that does not divide evenly.** Consequence: no rounding rule to test, and
  a tool that refuses to record a real €10 pizza split three ways, which makes it useless.
- **Recommendation:** **A** and **C**. A is the rule that makes every error case decidable, and
  WI-0003 can normalise a bank-formatted amount into it rather than both commands having to agree
  on a loose format. C is the only option where the total recorded always equals the total spent
  and nobody has to explain why Ana owes a cent more than Ben. E is listed only to be rejected.

## Answer

[human] Format — whatever's easiest to type, I don't need symbols or commas. Who eats the odd
cent — not sure yet, go ahead anyway, we'll decide later.

**Recorded by `answer-questions`:** the first sentence selects **option A** on format — a plain
decimal, no currency symbol, no thousands separator. The second sentence is a delegation rather
than a deferral: "go ahead anyway" is permission for the architect to decide. It was therefore
**not** re-escalated — none of the four conditions in `spec/question.md` §4 applies, since intent
was stated, the choice is reversible and it contradicts nothing recorded. The architect chose
**option C**: the payer absorbs the remainder. Each non-payer sharer owes the total divided by the
number of sharers rounded down to the minor unit; the payer carries the rest, so an expense's
shares always sum to its recorded total. `ADR-0002` records the options, the reasoning and the
reversibility.

The stakeholder has not seen the remainder rule. That is recorded as `[unresolved]` in the
refinement Q&A so a later question can cite it.
