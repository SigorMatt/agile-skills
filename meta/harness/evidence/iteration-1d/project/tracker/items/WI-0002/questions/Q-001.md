## Context

WI-0002 is the item that answers your "show who owes whom". I am refining it against the
Definition of Ready, whose criterion R4 says every acceptance criterion must be decidable by
someone with a terminal and no context. Five of this item's six criteria hang on one thing
nobody has stated, and this question is that thing.

AC1 currently says the command prints "a set of debts of the form `<person> owes <person>
<amount>`". It does not say **which** set. There are two ordinary answers, both correct, and they
print different lines from the same data. `intake` recorded this as an open gap rather than
guessing it (`WI-0002/item.md`, `## Notes`, first bullet), and I am not going to guess it either,
because AC2 (the debts balance), AC4 (deterministic output), AC5 (repayments net off) and AC6
(fully repaid reads as "nobody owes anybody") all mean different things depending on your answer.

Everything else this item needed is already settled and is **not** what I am asking about: an
expense is split equally among its sharers (`WI-0001/Q-001`), the payer absorbs the leftover cent
when it does not divide evenly (`ADR-0002`), amounts print as plain two-decimal numbers
(`WI-0001` AC6), and a repayment is its own record that shrinks a debt (`ADR-0001`).

Worked example, so the difference is concrete rather than abstract. Suppose Ana, Ben and Cara are
recorded, and:

- Ana pays **30.00** for dinner, shared by Ana, Ben and Cara — so 10.00 each;
- Ben pays **12.00** for the taxi, shared by Ben and Cara — so 6.00 each.

## Question

When you run the report, do you want to see **every debt between each pair of people** (option A
below), or **the shortest list of payments that settles everybody up** (option B below)?

## Options considered

- **A — one line per pair of people.** For each pair, everything one of them owes the other is
  added up across all expenses and repayments, and a line is printed when the result is not zero.
  The example prints three lines:

  ```
  Ben owes Ana 10.00
  Cara owes Ana 10.00
  Cara owes Ben 6.00
  ```

  Consequence: every line traces back to expenses those two people actually shared, so "why do I
  owe her?" is always answerable by looking at the expense list. The cost is that it can ask for
  more payments than are strictly needed, and it can print a circle — Ana owes Ben, Ben owes
  Cara, Cara owes Ana — which reads oddly when the group could have settled with one transfer.
  Here it is three payments moving 26.00 in total.

- **B — the shortest list of payments that settles everybody.** Each person's overall position is
  worked out first — what they paid, minus what they were shared for, plus or minus what they
  have repaid — and then the tool prints the fewest transfers that bring everyone to zero. The
  example prints two lines:

  ```
  Ben owes Ana 4.00
  Cara owes Ana 16.00
  ```

  Consequence: fewest actual payments, and never a circle. Ben, who is owed 6.00 by Cara and owes
  10.00 to Ana, simply pays Ana 4.00 and is done. The cost is that a printed line need not
  correspond to any expense those two people shared: Cara's 16.00 to Ana includes the 6.00 she
  owes Ben for the taxi, and nothing in the output says so. If your group ever argues about a
  line, A is easier to defend.

- **C — one line per expense, un-netted.** "Ben owes Ana 10.00 for dinner", and so on, with
  nothing added up. Listed to be rejected: that is the expense list you already have from WI-0001
  with the arithmetic done per row, not an answer to "who owes whom", and it gets longer every
  time you record anything.

- **Recommendation: B.** Your idea says the point is to "show who owes whom", and the epic's goal
  says it is so the group can settle up — B is the version that minimises the number of times
  money actually changes hands, which is the thing settling up costs. It is also what the
  well-known tools in this space show by default. Choose **A** instead if you expect anyone in
  the group to want each line justified against a specific meal; that traceability is the one
  real thing B gives up, and it is the reason this is your call rather than mine.

  Either way I will make the report add up exactly — the printed amounts will account for every
  cent, with none invented or lost — and print in a fixed, repeatable order, and say "nobody owes
  anybody" rather than nothing when everyone is square. Those are AC2, AC4 and AC3, and they hold
  under both options.

## Answer

[human] A — I want the pairwise breakdown. If the number ever gets questioned I want it to trace
straight back to what those two people actually shared, not to some clever routing through
somebody else's taxi. Fewer transfers doesn't matter as much as nobody being able to argue about
a line.
