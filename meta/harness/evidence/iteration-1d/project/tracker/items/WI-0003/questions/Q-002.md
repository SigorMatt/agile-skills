## Context

This question supersedes the second half of `EP-001/Q-002`, per `spec/question.md` §3 rule 6.
`Q-001` on this item asks for the sample file; this one asks the decision that goes with it, and
they are filed together so you can answer both in one sitting. Neither can be derived from the
other: a sample tells me how to *read* your file, and this tells me what to *do* with each row.

The problem, stated plainly: **a bank row does not know who shared the expense.** It knows a
date, an amount and a merchant. It only implicitly knows who paid — presumably you, since it is
your account. Something has to supply the missing information, and what that something is changes
what the import command looks like and what you type when you run it.

Two things have been settled since `EP-001/Q-002` was asked, and both make the answer matter more
than it did then:

- **There is still no way to delete anything.** WI-0001 shipped with no edit and no delete: a
  wrongly recorded expense stays in the file unless you edit the JSON by hand
  [src: WI-0001]. So an import that pulls in your own groceries as a group
  expense is not a small mistake — it is one you cannot undo from the tool.
- **An expense is shared equally among whoever shared it** (`WI-0001/Q-001`, `ADR-0002`), so the
  answer only has to name *who*, never in what proportion.

## Question

How should a row in your bank export become a shared expense — who is recorded as having paid it,
and who is recorded as having shared it?

## Options considered

These are the options `EP-001/Q-002` put to you, unchanged except where the no-delete decision
above has sharpened the consequence.

- **A — everything imported is paid by you and shared by everyone currently recorded.**
  Consequence: the simplest possible import — one command, a file path, nothing else. Wrong for
  any transaction only some of the group shared, and wrong for anything personal that is in the
  same export. Given that nothing can be deleted, a single run over a full month's statement puts
  your own shopping into the group's books permanently.
- **B — you name the payer and the sharers once, for the whole import**, with a way of limiting
  which rows are taken — a date range, or a match on the description. Consequence: one
  non-interactive command with a few flags: import this file, paid by me, shared by Ana, Ben and
  me, only the rows between these dates. Handles a weekend away, which is the case where a whole
  block of the statement genuinely is one group's spending. A mixed export still needs the range
  or the match to be right, but nothing has to be edited by hand.
- **C — the import asks you row by row: include this one, and who shared it?** Consequence:
  handles a mixed export honestly and needs no filtering. It is interactive, so it is slower on a
  large file, harder to test, and closer to the typing you said you wanted less of — though it is
  still less typing than entering each expense in full.
- **D — the CSV itself carries the sharing**, because you add columns to it before importing.
  Consequence: precise and scriptable, and the import stays a pure file-in operation. But it
  means editing the export by hand every time, which is the thing this item exists to avoid.
- **Recommendation: B.** It is one command, it is testable without a person at the keyboard, and
  it does not assume every line of your statement is a group expense. I would not choose A: it is
  the cheapest to build and the one most likely to put data you cannot remove into a store that
  has no delete. If you tell me your exports are already only group spending — a separate account
  for the trip, say — then A is fine and I will take it, but say so explicitly rather than letting
  me assume it.

  Whichever you pick, tell me if you would rather answer **C** and have me build it interactively;
  it is the only one of the four that changes this item from "run a command" into "sit with the
  tool", and that is your call, not mine.

## Answer

[human] B — let me say who paid and who it's shared with when I run the import, and let me limit
it to a date range. That's basically what a trip looks like anyway.
