## Question

How should the importer learn the shape of your bank's CSV — from a sample you send me now, from
options you type when you run it, or from a fixed format you convert your export into?

## Options considered

- **A — send the sample after all.** Paste 3–5 lines with the header row into this file's
  `## Answer`; change the merchant names and amounts to anything, I need the shape and not your
  spending. Consequence: I write the column mapping, the date format, the delimiter and the
  amount convention straight into the acceptance criteria, and the importer needs no options
  beyond the payer, the sharers and the date range you already chose. It is the smallest item of
  the three and the one that works with no thought at the moment you use it. Cost: this item
  waits until the sample arrives, which has now been three turns.

- **B — fix the format, and you convert your export to it.** The importer accepts exactly one
  shape: a comma-separated file with a header row naming `date`, `description` and `amount`, the
  date written `YYYY-MM-DD`, and the amount a plain signed number like `-42.50`. If your bank
  writes something else, you open the export in a spreadsheet, rename and reorder the columns,
  and save. Consequence: buildable today and simple to test. Cost: it is manual work at *every*
  import, which is close to what you said you wanted to stop doing — "instead of me typing them
  in" — so I would rather you rejected this than accepted it quietly.

- **C — the importer takes the mapping as options, and you fill them in once from your own
  header row.** You run something like
  `import-csv --file statement.csv --date-column "Booking Date" --amount-column Amount
  --description-column "Merchant" --date-format DD/MM/YYYY --payer Ana --shared-by Ana
  --shared-by Ben`, and the tool tells you what the columns in your file are called if you get one
  wrong. The delimiter is detected from the header line, and the amount normaliser accepts a
  stated list of forms — `-42.50`, `42,50`, `(42.50)`, `42.50-`, `€42.50`, `1,234.56` — so
  currency symbols and separators are not your problem. Consequence: the sample stops being on the
  critical path entirely; nothing is guessed, because the facts I am missing become things you
  state at the moment you have the file open. Cost: the largest of the three items — five more
  options and a normaliser with its own tests — and you have to look at your header row once,
  though not paste it here.

- **D — drop the import from this epic.** Listed to be rejected: you were explicit on
  `EP-001/Q-001` that the import ships and is not optional. It is here so that the option you
  ruled out is visible rather than silently dropped.

- **Recommendation: C.** It is the only option that both requires nothing from you before work
  can start *and* keeps the promise the idea made — that you stop retyping what the bank already
  wrote down. B trades the retyping for spreadsheet work, which is the same chore wearing a hat.
  A is genuinely better than C *if* the sample is easy for you to produce, because the tool ends
  up simpler for everyone, so answer A if the file is at hand. If you are unsure, C is the safe
  one: an importer built for C can grow a `--profile` shortcut later that remembers your bank's
  mapping, and then it behaves exactly like A without ever having needed the sample.

If you pick C, one thing still comes back to you later: whether importing the same file twice
should skip the rows it already imported or add them again (AC5). I cannot put that to you yet —
it depends on what identifies a row — and I expect to decide it myself, toward skipping and
telling you how many were skipped, because WI-0001 ships with no way to delete an expense.

## Answer

**Read by answer-questions (architect) as: option A, with B rejected too.** "A name-the-columns
version" is option **C** by description, and it is refused by name. "Just wait for my file" refuses
**B** as well — under B there is no waiting to do, because the stakeholder would convert their
export into the fixed shape and import it today; saying "wait" means they intend to hand over the
export *as it is*. **D** was never in play. So the importer is written against the stakeholder's
real export, the column mapping is fixed in the acceptance criteria once the sample exists, and
the import command gains no mapping options beyond the payer, the sharers and the optional date
range `ADR-0007` already settled. This is recorded as `ADR-0010`.

**What the answer does not supply is the sample itself**, and under A nothing about the file's
shape can be written down without it — not AC1's mapping, not AC3's "a row the tool cannot turn
into an expense", not AC4's "not the expected shape at all", not AC5's duplicate detection. The
question is nonetheless genuinely answered: it asked *where the shape should come from* and the
stakeholder said, unambiguously, from their file. The consequence is a wait, and the honest way to
record a wait on something only a person can supply is `blocked`, not a fourth question.

**No fourth question will be filed for the sample.** The stakeholder has been asked three times —
`EP-001/Q-002`, `Q-001`, and this one — and has now answered the third with an instruction. Asking
again would spend their attention on something already settled. `ADR-0010` names
`tracker/items/WI-0003/artifacts/bank-sample.csv` as where the file should land so that a fresh
session finds it without being told, and instructs the next `refine` execution to move this item

`blocked` once a human has recorded the resolution — here, once the file exists.
