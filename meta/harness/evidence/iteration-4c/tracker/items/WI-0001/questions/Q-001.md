This one is not that kind of decision. The item's AC3 says that adding several cards keeps all of

with two meanings ("bank" the place, "bank" the river edge) is two cards with one front side, and
so is the same card typed twice by mistake. Whatever we choose, you meet it the first time you
type a word you have already entered.

## Question

When you add a card whose front side is exactly the same as a card you already have, what should
the tool do?

## Options considered

- **A — Add it, say nothing.** You end up with two cards, both come up for review on their own
  schedules, and the tool never gets in your way. Consequence: two meanings of one word are easy
  and cost you nothing. A card typed twice by accident is now two cards that both come back
  forever, and you would only find out during a review.
- **B — Refuse it, and say the card already exists.** Nothing is added, the tool exits with an
  error, and your existing card and its schedule are untouched. Consequence: you can never
  accidentally duplicate a card. You also cannot have two cards with the same front at all, so
  the second meaning of a word has to be typed differently — "bank (river)" — to get in.
- **C — Add it, and warn you that the front is a duplicate.** The card goes in exactly as in A,
  and the tool prints a line saying you already have a card with that front. Consequence: nothing
  is ever refused, and you find out at the moment it happens rather than during a review. The cost
  is a line of output you will sometimes see when you meant it.
- **Recommendation (ours, not yours to defer to):** **C**. It never refuses something you meant to
  add, which matters because we cannot tell a homograph from a typo and you can. If you would
  rather the tool stayed silent, say A — the warning is the only difference.

## Answer

[human] C — add it and warn me. I don't want it refusing a second meaning of a word, and a
warning is enough to catch a typo.
