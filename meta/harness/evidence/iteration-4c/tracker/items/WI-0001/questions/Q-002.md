## Context

This is **question 2 of 2** on WI-0001 in this round, and the last of them — `WI-0001/Q-001` asks
what happens when you add the same front side twice. That is everything we have to ask you on
this item.

You told us where your cards have to live: *"It needs to live in a file on my machine that
survives a reboot"* (`EP-001/Q-004`). This item currently carries a criterion (AC5) that goes
further than that sentence: it says both sides of every card and its scheduling state must be
readable **out of that file with an ordinary text tool while the tool is not running**. That
criterion is ours, not yours — it was written at intake and flagged at the time as something to
put to you rather than let stand as if you had asked for it.

We are asking rather than deciding it under *"whatever you think is best"*, for one reason: this
is the one decision in the whole project that is expensive to undo. Your cards and their schedule
accumulate in whatever the first version writes, so changing the shape of that file later means
either migrating your real study history or losing it — and losing your progress is one of the two
things you told us would make this a failure (`EP-001/Q-004`). A promise that you can always read
your own cards is a promise the format has to keep from the first day.

It is worth saying what it costs, so the answer is not free money: a format you can read is
usually a bit larger and a bit slower than one you cannot, at the sizes you are talking about
neither is noticeable, and the real cost is that we would be committing to keep it that way.

## Question

Does the file your cards live in have to be something you can open and read yourself in a text
editor, or is its format ours to choose?

## Options considered

- **A — Yes, and treat it as a document you may edit.** The file is plain text you can read, and
  editing it by hand is a supported way to change your cards. Consequence: you are never locked
  out of your own data, and the tool has to cope with whatever you leave behind — a half-finished
  edit, a missing field — which is real work and would likely be its own work item.
- **B — Yes, but the file is the tool's to write.** Plain text you can open and read at any time,
  so you can always see your cards and check nothing was lost; editing it by hand is not something
  we promise to support, and the tool may rewrite or reformat it whenever it saves. Consequence:
  the "nothing was lost" check is yours to do with `cat` rather than something you have to take
  the tool's word for. No extra work beyond choosing a readable format.
- **C — No, the format is ours.** The file exists at a documented path on your machine and
  survives a reboot, and nothing more is promised about what is in it. Consequence: we would keep
  AC5 only as far as "a file exists where the documentation says", and checking that a card
  survived means asking the tool to show you, because there is no other way to look.
- **Recommendation (ours, not yours to defer to):** **B**. It gives you the guarantee you actually
  named — that your progress is yours and is still there — without committing us to accept
  hand-edited files, which is a different piece of work. If you do want to edit by hand, say A and
  we will scope that separately rather than pretend it is free.

## Answer

[human] B. I want to be able to open it and see my cards are still there, but I'm not asking to
hand-edit it — that's a different thing.
