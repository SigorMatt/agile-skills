## Context

You said: *"A flashcard tool: add cards, review due cards daily, simple spaced repetition.
Progress persists."* We have written that up as epic EP-001 with two work items — WI-0001 (add a
card, and it survives a restart) and WI-0002 (review what is due today and reschedule it).

This is **question 1 of 4** filed on EP-001 in one round. They are one conversation, not four
separate asks: 1 is how you use the tool, 2 is what the spaced repetition actually does, 3 is
what makes a card due on a given day, and 4 asks what we have not thought to ask you.

The repository is empty — there is no existing code, language or framework to fit into — so
nothing on disk answers this. It is not reversible in any cheap sense either: the interaction
style decides how every acceptance criterion in both work items is written and checked, and
changing it later means rewriting the product rather than adjusting it. That is why we are asking
rather than assuming.

## Question

How do you want to use this tool day to day — what do you actually sit in front of when you add
a card or do your daily review?

## Options considered

- **A — A command-line tool.** You run something like `flash add` and `flash review` in a
  terminal and answer prompts. Consequence: fastest to build and the easiest to check — every
  acceptance criterion becomes a command with an output someone can read. No mouse, no browser,
  and it only works on a machine with a terminal.
- **B — A local web page.** You start the tool and it opens in your browser. Consequence: nicer
  to look at, works on a tablet on the same network, and is the closest thing to the flashcard
  apps you may have used. Costs noticeably more work, and every criterion has to be checked
  through a browser rather than by reading output.
- **C — A full-screen terminal app.** Still the terminal, but a single interactive screen you
  move through with the keyboard rather than one command at a time. Consequence: the review
  session feels more like a study session; harder to test than A, and the persistence and
  scheduling work is the same either way.
- **D — Something else you have in mind.** Say what, and we will scope it.
- **Recommendation:** A, the command-line tool — this is *our* preference, not a conclusion. It
  gets you a working review loop soonest and it makes "progress persists" trivially checkable.
  If you would actually use B or C and would not use A, say so; the scheduling and storage work
  is the same underneath and only the surface changes.

## Answer

[human] A. Command-line is fine — it's just me, once a day at a terminal, running through vocab.
