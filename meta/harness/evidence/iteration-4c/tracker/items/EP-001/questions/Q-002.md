## Question

When you finish a card in a review session, what rule should decide when that card comes back?

## Options considered

- **A — A fixed ladder of intervals (Leitner boxes).** Each card sits on a rung. Get it right,
  it moves up and comes back after a longer fixed gap — for example 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days, then
  stays at 30. Get it wrong, it drops back to rung 1 and you see it tomorrow. Consequence: you
  can predict and check exactly when any card is next due by reading its rung; there is nothing
  to tune. It treats "just barely right" and "instant" identically.
- **B — SM-2, the classic Anki-style algorithm.** Each card carries an interval and an "ease"
  number. You grade each answer on a scale (typically 0–5 or four buttons), and the next interval
  is the current one multiplied by that ease, with the ease itself nudged up or down by how you
  graded. Consequence: adapts to each card and to you, and it is what commercial flashcard apps
  do. More moving parts, floating-point state to persist, and "why is this card due in 11 days"
  is harder to answer.
- **C — Two intervals only.** Right → come back in a week. Wrong → come back tomorrow. Nothing
  else. Consequence: the smallest thing that is honestly spaced repetition; trivial to check.
  Cards you know well keep reappearing weekly for ever, which gets tedious on a large deck.
- **D — Intervals you name.** You tell us the sequence you want, and we implement exactly that.
- **Recommendation:** A, the fixed ladder — again, *our* preference and not a conclusion. It is
  what most people mean by "simple spaced repetition", the schedule is inspectable by eye, and
  moving to B later is a change to one function rather than to the shape of the stored data. If
  you have used Anki and want it to feel like Anki, say B and we will build B.

## Answer

[human] Just right or wrong — no difficulty scale. Get it right and it comes back later each
time: a day, then three days, then a week, then a month. Get it wrong and it goes back to the
start.
