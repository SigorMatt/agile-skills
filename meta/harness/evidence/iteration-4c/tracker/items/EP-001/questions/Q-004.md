## Context

This is **question 4 of 4** filed on EP-001 in one round, and the last of them — Q-001 asked how
you use the tool, Q-002 what the spaced-repetition rule does, Q-003 what makes a card due on a
given day. That is everything we have to ask you for now.

Every one of those came from our list of things *we* need to know in order to build. That list
has a blind spot the size of the product: it cannot contain the thing you would have mentioned
unprompted, because we do not know it is there. In a previous engagement of this kind the
stakeholder held two real requirements for the entire project and wrote at the end that they were
never asked about anything they would have thought to say themselves.

So this question exists to make room for that. It does not block anything — work continues
whatever you say, or if you say nothing. But whatever you write here will be routed like any
other answer: into a new work item, into an acceptance criterion, or into a note that says
plainly that we heard it and are not acting on it, and why.

To give you something to push against, here is what we have currently written down as **out of
scope** on the epic, all of it our inference rather than your words: multiple decks or tags;
more than one user; syncing between devices; editing or deleting a card after adding it;
importing from Anki or CSV; images, audio or formatting on cards; reminders or notifications;
statistics, streaks and graphs. If any of those is actually important to you, this is the place
to say so.

## Question

What else matters to you about this flashcard tool that we have not asked you about?

## Options considered

This is not a choice between options, and inventing two would defeat the purpose of asking. It
is deliberately open: anything you say is a legitimate answer, including "nothing, you have
covered it".

## Answer

[human] A card's just a front and a back, one line of text each — nothing fancy. It needs to
live in a file on my machine that survives a reboot. I want to be able to delete a card; editing
can wait. Two things would make this a failure for me: losing my progress, or a review taking
more than a couple minutes to get through. As for how it's actually built — whatever you think
is best.
