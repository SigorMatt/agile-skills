## Question

When more cards are due than you can get through in a couple of minutes, what should a review
session do?

## Options considered

- **A — Your sentence in `EP-001/Q-003` wins: show everything that is due.** A session offers
  every due card, however many there are, and a big backlog means a long session. Consequence:
  the schedule and the session agree exactly — what is due is what you see — and it is the
  simplest thing to check. After a week away you face the whole backlog in one sitting, or you
  quit part-way through; cards you did answer keep their new schedule, and the rest stay due
  tomorrow.
- **B — Your sentence in `EP-001/Q-004` wins: cap the session.** A session offers at most some
  number of cards — say the first 40 by due date, oldest first — and tells you how many are still
  waiting. Consequence: a review is always roughly the same length. The tool now shows you fewer
  cards than are due, so "exactly the cards that are due" stops being what the session does, and
  a backlog drains over several days rather than in one sitting. We would need a number from you,
  or you can let us pick one and change it later.
- **C — Show everything, but let you stop cleanly.** No cap; the session tells you at the start
  how many are due and roughly how long that is, and quitting part-way is a supported action that
  keeps every answer you gave. Consequence: both sentences are honoured in spirit — nothing is
  hidden from you, and no session traps you — but neither is honoured literally, because a full
  review of a large backlog still takes as long as it takes.
- **Recommendation:** none — this is yours to settle. Both sentences are yours, we have no basis
  for preferring one, and choosing for you is exactly the move this protocol exists to prevent.

## Answer

[human] C. Don't cap it at some arbitrary number, but let me quit partway through without
losing anything — whatever I already answered keeps its new schedule, and the rest is still due
tomorrow. I'd rather see the honest number of cards waiting than have the tool quietly decide
which ones I don't get to see today.
