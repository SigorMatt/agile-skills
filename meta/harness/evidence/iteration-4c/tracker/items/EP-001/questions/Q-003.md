## Context

This is **question 3 of 4** filed on EP-001 in one round, following Q-001 (how you use the tool)
and Q-002 (what the scheduling rule is). This one is smaller but it is the difference between an
acceptance criterion someone can decide and one they cannot.

You said "review due cards **daily**". Whatever rule Q-002 settles on, something has to say when
a card scheduled "in 3 days" actually becomes due, and whether a card you have already done today
can come up again if you start a second session this evening. WI-0002's AC2 ("exactly the cards
that are due and no others") and AC5 ("a session run again after a restart, on the same day")
both turn on it, and so does WI-0001's AC4 for a brand-new card.

We are asking rather than assuming because either answer is defensible, the difference is visible
to you every time you use the tool, and it is baked into the stored schedule from the first card
you add.

## Question

What makes a card due on a given day — and what happens if you start a second review session the
same day?

## Options considered

- **A — Calendar days, local time.** A card is scheduled for a date. It becomes due at the start
  of that date in your own timezone, and once you have answered it that day it is not offered
  again until its next scheduled date. Consequence: matches how people actually think about
  "daily review"; a second session the same evening shows only cards you had not got to. Needs a
  decision about what "your timezone" means if you ever move machines, and a card done at 23:55
  can come back at 00:05 the next night.
- **B — Elapsed hours.** A card reviewed now with an interval of 3 days is due exactly 72 hours
  from now. Consequence: precise, timezone-free, and easy to test with a fixed clock. Your
  "daily" review drifts later and later each day, and a card can be not-quite-due when you sit
  down at your usual time, which people find annoying.
- **C — Calendar days, but a day starts at a time you choose** (say 4am, so a late-night session
  still counts as the previous day). Consequence: the nicest behaviour for real use; one more
  setting to store and to explain, and one more thing for us to get wrong.
- **Recommendation:** A, calendar days in local time — *our* preference, stated after the
  options rather than before them because it should not be what you read first. It is the
  simplest thing that matches the words "review due cards daily", and if the midnight edge ever
  bothers you, C is a small change on top of it.

## Answer

[human] A card's due if its date is today or earlier. If I miss a day it's just still due —
nothing lost, nothing punished. A second session the same day should only show me whatever's
still due, not everything again. And no cap on how many come up at once — whatever's due, all
of it.
