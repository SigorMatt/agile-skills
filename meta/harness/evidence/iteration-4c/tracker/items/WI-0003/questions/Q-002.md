## Context

This is **question 2 of 2** on WI-0003 in this round, and the last of them — `WI-0003/Q-001` asks
how you name the card you want deleted. That is everything we have to ask you on this item.

The item currently excludes undo: no trash, no archive, nothing a deleted card can be recovered
from. That exclusion is ours and nobody has asked you about it, which is exactly the kind of thing
that should not stand as if you had.

We are asking because of something you told us. You named two things that would make this tool a
failure for you, and the first was *"losing my progress"* (`EP-001/Q-004`). Deleting the wrong
card is a small, self-inflicted version of that: the card goes, and so does everything the
schedule had learned about it — which rung it had reached and when it was next due. On a card you
have been getting right for a month, that is a month of work gone in a keystroke.

There is a real cost on the other side, so this is not a free choice. Every safeguard is something
you meet **every** time you delete, including the many times you meant it.

## Question

What should protect you from deleting the wrong card?

## Options considered

- **A — Nothing. Deleting deletes.** The card and its schedule are gone from the file
  immediately, and the tool says which card it removed. Consequence: the fastest thing, and there
  is nothing to learn. A mistyped delete costs you that card's history with no way back.
- **B — Ask you first.** The tool shows you the card it is about to delete — both sides, and how
  far up the ladder it had got — and removes it only if you say yes. Consequence: you see what
  you are about to lose before you lose it, and a mistyped front side simply shows you the wrong
  card and you say no. You answer a prompt every single time, including when you are deleting
  five cards in a row.
- **C — Keep it recoverable.** Deleted cards go somewhere they can be brought back from, and
  never appear in a review again. Consequence: nothing is ever really lost. It is the most work
  of the three, it needs its own commands to see and restore what was deleted, and it means your
  file keeps things you told it to throw away — so we would file it as its own item rather than
  build it inside this one.
- **Recommendation (ours, not yours to defer to):** **B**. It is the one that directly answers the
  thing you called a failure, it costs one keystroke, and it needs nothing new. If the prompt would
  annoy you more than the risk worries you, say A — you are the one who would be typing it.

## Answer

[human] B — show me the card and ask first. One keystroke is worth it to not lose a month of
progress by fat-fingering a delete.
