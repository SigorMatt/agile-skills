## Context

WI-0001, refinement round 1, question 2 of 3 — same batch as Q-001, same item.

This one is about what an expense record holds, and it is a scope question rather than a
technical one, which is why it is coming to you rather than being decided for you.

You described an expense as an amount, paid by someone, shared by some or all. Taken literally
that is all an expense would store — so `expense list` would print something like
"30.00, paid by Ana, shared by Ana, Ben, Cara" and nothing about what the 30.00 was. Two things
made refinement stop rather than assume:

First, a week later that list is hard to use. If there are four expenses of similar size, nothing
distinguishes them, and if you want to check whether you already entered the taxi you cannot.

Second, the bank CSV import you asked for (WI-0003) is going to arrive with a date column and a
description column in every row. If an expense has nowhere to put them, that information is
thrown away at the moment of import — and the description is very often the only thing that
tells you what a bank row was.

But adding fields is adding to what you asked for, and that is yours to authorise, not ours to
slip in.

## Question

Should each expense also record a short description of what it was for, and the date it happened
— or is the amount, the payer and the sharers all you want stored?

## Options considered

- **A — Amount, payer and sharers only.** Consequence: exactly what you described and nothing
  more; the least to type per expense. `expense list` shows amounts without saying what they
  were for, and the CSV import will discard each row's date and description.
- **B — Add a required description; no date.** `--description "dinner at Luigi's"`. Consequence:
  the listing becomes readable and the CSV import has somewhere to put the row's description.
  You have to type a description every time, including for the obvious ones.
- **C — Add an optional description and a date that defaults to today.** `--description` may be
  omitted; `--date` may be omitted and then means today. Consequence: nothing extra to type
  when you do not care, everything available when you do, and the CSV import can carry both
  columns through unchanged. Slightly more to build and to verify than B, and "who owes whom"
  does not use either field — they are there for reading the list, not for the arithmetic.
- **Recommendation:** C. It costs you nothing at the keyboard, it is the only option that lets the
  bank import keep what the bank told you, and dates are the thing people reach for first when
  reconciling a list against a real trip. If you would rather keep the tool as small as you
  described it, A is a perfectly coherent answer and we will build exactly that.

## Answer

The stakeholder's reply, verbatim:

> Yeah, add both — description and date. I'll want to know what a charge was for when I'm looking
> back over the list.

That is **option C**: an optional description and a date that defaults to the day the expense is
recorded. Option A (amount, payer and sharers only) is refused outright, and option B is excluded
by "add both" — B offered a required description and *no* date.

They chose C as it was written, so the description stays **optional** rather than required. Their
stated reason is served by the field existing when they want it, not by being made to type one
for every entry; and the question put the optionality in front of them as C's defining
difference from B. This is recorded rather than quietly reinterpreted, because "they said they
wanted descriptions, so make it required" is exactly the kind of drift the record exists to
catch. If they later want it compulsory, that is a new question, not a reading of this one.

Neither field takes part in any arithmetic. They are for reading the list back — and they are
what lets WI-0003's importer carry a bank row's own date and description through instead of
discarding them at the moment of import, which was the second reason the question was asked.

The command surface gains `[--description <TEXT>]` and `[--date <YYYY-MM-DD>]` on `expense add`.
What a date looks like on disk, and how `--date` is validated, are technical and stay `plan`'s.
