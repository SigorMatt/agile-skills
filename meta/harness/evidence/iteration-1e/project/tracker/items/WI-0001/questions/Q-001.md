## Context

WI-0001, refinement round 1, question 1 of 3 — the batch is about recording people and expenses,
the first item of EP-001. Three questions, asked together; nothing else is being asked about this
item for now.

This one is about the arithmetic, and it is the question everything else in the tool rests on.

Intake wrote down that you had not said how an expense divides between the people who shared it,
and deliberately did not invent an answer. Refinement agrees that it is not ours to invent,
because it decides what every number this tool will ever print actually means — including the
"who owes whom" list you asked for in EP-001/Q-002.

An equal split between the named sharers is the plain reading of "shared by some or all", and if
that is what you meant this is a one-word answer. But the reason it is worth asking is the dinner
where Ana had the steak at 22 and Ben had the soup at 8: with equal splits the only way to record
that is as two separate expenses, one shared by Ana and one shared by Ben, which works but means
typing two entries for one bill. The alternative is to let you say the amounts per person on a
single expense. Neither is obviously right, and the difference shows up in what you type every
time you use the tool.

Everything else about amounts, refinement has settled on its own and is not asking you about:
amounts are money with at most two decimal places, must be greater than zero, and however an
expense divides, the shares always add up to exactly the amount paid — no fraction of a unit
created or lost. Which person absorbs an odd remaining unit when a split does not come out even
is a technical decision and we will make it.

## Question

When an expense is shared by several people, how much does each of them owe — an equal share
each, or do you want to be able to say a different amount for different people on the same
expense?

## Options considered

- **A — Equal split between the named sharers, always.** A 30 dinner shared by three people is
  10 each. Consequence: the simplest thing to type and the simplest to check —
  `--shared-by Ana,Ben,Cara` and nothing more. An unequal bill is recorded as two or more
  expenses. This is the smallest tool that does what you described.
- **B — Equal split by default, with optional per-person amounts.** `--shared-by Ana,Ben,Cara`
  still means 10 each, but you could instead write something like
  `--shared-by Ana=22,Ben=8` when the bill was uneven. Consequence: one expense per real-world
  bill, at the cost of a second thing to learn and more to build and verify. The tool has to check
  that the per-person amounts add up to the total and refuse the entry if they do not.
- **C — Shares or weights rather than amounts.** `--shared-by Ana=2,Ben=1` meaning Ana owes twice
  what Ben does. Consequence: handles "he only had a starter" without arithmetic on your part,
  but you have to think in ratios, and rounding becomes visible in a way people argue about —
  which is the arguing you said you wanted to stop.
- **Recommendation:** A, unless uneven bills are common in your group. It matches what you
  described, it is the least to type, and B can be added later as its own piece of work without
  changing anything built for A — the equal split stays the default in B. If uneven bills happen
  often enough that recording them as two expenses would annoy you, say so and we will take B.

## Answer

The stakeholder's reply, verbatim:

> Equal split, keep it simple. If a bill's uneven we'll just enter it as separate expenses.

That is **option A**, and the second sentence confirms it was chosen with its cost understood:
they were told an uneven bill would have to be recorded as two or more expenses, and they said
so back. Option B (optional per-person amounts) and option C (weights) are both refused.

So, settled: **an expense divides equally between the people named as its sharers.** A 30 dinner
shared by three people is 10.00 each. There are no per-person amounts and no weights anywhere in
the tool, and `--shared-by` stays a bare list of names — which is the command surface refinement
had already assumed, so nothing in it changes.

Two things this does **not** settle, both deliberately left where they were:

- **The remainder.** An equal split still has to add up: 10 shared by three is 3.34/3.33/3.33, not
  3.33 three times. Which sharer absorbs the extra unit is a technical decision with no
  stakeholder stake, it was named as such in the question's own context, and it stays an open
  design question for `plan`, whose only constraint is that the rule be deterministic.
- **What the shares are used for.** WI-0002's settlement list is computed from these shares, so
  this answer is what makes its figures mean anything. WI-0002's notes now say so rather than
  pointing at an unanswered question.
