## Context

WI-0004, round 1, question 1 of 1: what deleting a person does to that person's expenses. This is
the only thing on this item I need from you — everything else refinement settled on its own, and
all of it is written down in `tracker/items/WI-0004/artifacts/refinement-qa.md` so you can see
what was decided in your name and object to any of it.

You asked for this item yourself. When we asked whether records could be corrected or removed, you
said: *"Hadn't really thought about it, but if I have to pick — being able to delete a mistake
matters more to me than editing one. Timing's up to you, doesn't need to hold up the who-owes-whom
feature."* The who-owes-whom feature is now delivered, so this item is next.

Deleting an expense is straightforward: the expense goes, and the next `settle` reflects it.
Deleting a **person** is not, and the reason is that a person is not a standalone record in this
tool. People are named inside expenses — as the payer, and as the sharers the cost was split
between. So if Ben is deleted while a 30.00 taxi he shared is still recorded, the tool has an
expense that refers to somebody who is no longer in the group, and there is no obviously right
thing for it to do about that.

Concretely, with the tool as it stands today:

    $ python3 -m expenses expense list
    2026-08-01  30.00  paid by Ana  shared by Ana,Ben  Taxi

    $ python3 -m expenses settle
    Ben pays Ana 15.00

Delete Ben, and that 15.00 either stays owed by a person the tool no longer knows about,
disappears along with the taxi, or is refused outright. Which of those happens is a decision about
your data — and deleting is irreversible, because you also told us you did not want an undo or a
record of what was deleted.

The draft of this item, written when the item was created, guessed at the third of those. I am not
willing to build on a guess about your data, which is why this is being asked rather than assumed.

## Question

When you delete a person who is named in expenses you have already recorded — as the payer, or as
one of the people who shared the cost — what should the tool do?

## Options considered

- **A — Refuse, and tell you which expenses are in the way.** `person delete Ben` changes nothing
  and prints something like `Ben is named in 2 expense(s); delete those first`, exiting non-zero.
  You delete the expenses yourself, then delete Ben. Consequence: nothing is ever destroyed that
  you did not name explicitly, and the who-owes-whom answer can never silently change because of
  a deletion you thought was small. The cost is that removing a person you added by mistake and
  then used in five expenses is six commands, not one. This is what the item's draft AC3 currently
  assumes, but nobody has put it to you.

- **B — Delete the person and every expense they appear in.** One command removes Ben, the taxi,
  and anything else he paid for or shared. Consequence: one command does the whole job. The cost
  is that it can destroy a great deal more than you meant — deleting Ben also deletes the taxi
  *Ana* paid for, and with it Ana's 15.00 claim against everyone else on that bill. There is no
  undo, so a mistyped name is not recoverable.

- **C — Delete the person and leave the expenses alone.** Ben vanishes from `person list`, the
  taxi still says `shared by Ana,Ben`. Consequence: it is the least surprising thing to type and
  the most surprising thing to live with — `settle` computes from the people it knows about, so
  Ben's 15.00 would quietly stop being owed to Ana and the numbers would no longer add up. I am
  recommending against this one rather than offering it neutrally: it is the only option that can
  make the tool print a wrong answer without telling you.

- **D — Refuse by default, delete everything with an extra flag.** A plain `person delete Ben`
  behaves like A; `person delete Ben --and-their-expenses` behaves like B. Consequence: you get
  the safe default and the one-command escape hatch, at the cost of one more flag to know about
  and roughly twice the behaviour to specify and test on this item.

- **Recommendation: A.** It is the only option where nothing is destroyed except what you named,
  and it fits what you have said elsewhere — *"Equal split, keep it simple"*, and no undo. If A
  turns out to be tedious in practice, D is a small item to add later on top of it; going the
  other way, from B to A, means having already lost data. But this is your call and not mine: it
  is your data, and none of it is recoverable once it is gone.

## Answer

[human] Go with A — refuse and tell me what's in the way. I'd rather do a couple extra commands
than have expenses vanish or numbers quietly go wrong because I mistyped a name.
