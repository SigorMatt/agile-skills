## Context

This is question 2 of 3 from intake on EP-001, part of the same batch as Q-001.

You asked to "at any point show who owes whom". That is WI-0002, and the phrase has two common
readings that produce different output and different code, so we cannot write a criterion someone
could decide without knowing which you meant.

Concretely, suppose Ana pays 30 for a dinner shared by Ana, Ben and Cara, and Ben pays 12 for a
taxi shared by Ben and Cara. Reading one prints each person's net position: Ana is up 20, Ben is
down 4, Cara is down 16. Reading two prints the payments that would settle everyone: Cara pays
Ana 16, Ben pays Ana 4. Both are honest answers to "who owes whom"; only the second tells the
group what to actually do.

## Question

When you ask who owes whom, do you want each person's net position (how much they are up or
down overall), or a list of specific payments that settles the whole group?

## Options considered

- **A — Net position per person.** Consequence: simplest to build and to check; the arithmetic is
  a single sum per person. The group still has to work out who hands money to whom.
- **B — A list of payments that settles the group.** Consequence: tells you exactly what to do.
  It needs a rule for choosing between equally valid settlements, so that the same data always
  prints the same list — that rule becomes an acceptance criterion.
- **C — Both, as two commands or two sections of one report.** Consequence: more to build and more
  to verify, but the net positions are a useful check on the settlement list.
- **Recommendation:** B, because "who owes whom" reads as a question about payments rather than
  about balances, and because it is the answer that saves you the arithmetic you said you wanted
  to avoid. If you would rather see the raw positions too, say so and we will take C.

## Answer

The stakeholder's reply, verbatim:

> The list of payments that settles it — that's what actually saves us the arguing after a trip.

That is **option B**, unambiguously: "who owes whom" means a list of specific payments that
settles the whole group, not each person's net position. They did not ask for C, so net
positions are not part of WI-0002 unless they later ask.

Option B carried a stated consequence, and it is part of what was chosen: a settlement list
needs a rule for choosing between equally valid settlements, so that the same data always prints
the same list. That rule becomes an acceptance criterion, and this answer adds it to WI-0002 as
AC4. What the rule *is* — which of several equally valid settlements to prefer, and in what
order to print it — is not decided here and is not the stakeholder's to decide; it is
refinement's, and then `plan`'s.
