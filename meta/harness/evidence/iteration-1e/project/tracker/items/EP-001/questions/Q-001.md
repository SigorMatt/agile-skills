## Context

This is question 1 of 3 from intake on EP-001 — the whole batch, asked together, from your
opening statement about tracking shared expenses in your friend group. Nobody was available to
ask in session, so they are written down instead.

You asked to "import expenses from my bank's CSV export instead of me typing them in". That is
WI-0003, and it is the one part of the idea nothing in the record describes: a bank CSV export
is whatever your particular bank produces, and there is no standard for it. Without knowing the
columns, WI-0003 has no acceptance criterion anyone with a terminal could decide, and no plan can
be written for it. This intake did not invent a format, because an invented one would be wrong in
a way that only shows up the first time you run the importer on a real file.

There is a second thing a bank export cannot tell us. A bank row says an amount was charged to
you; it does not say who shared it. So whatever the file looks like, the importer needs a rule
for that too — but which rule is possible depends entirely on what the file contains.

## Question

What does your bank's CSV export actually look like — ideally a real exported file (with the
amounts or names changed if you would rather), or failing that the header row and two or three
example rows, and which column holds the amount, the date and the description?

## Options considered

This is a missing fact rather than a choice between designs, so the options below are about what
we do while it is missing:

- **A — You supply a sample file or the column layout.** Consequence: WI-0003 can be refined and
  planned against something real. This is the only option that produces an importer that works on
  your data.
- **B — We invent a plausible layout (date, description, amount) and build against it.**
  Consequence: WI-0003 gets built and the acceptance criteria pass, against a file your bank does
  not produce. The failure surfaces the first time you use it, and the work is redone.
- **C — We drop WI-0003 from this epic and you keep typing expenses in.** Consequence: the rest
  of the tool is unaffected; you lose the part of the idea you asked for by name.
- **Recommendation:** A. B is the failure this question exists to prevent, and C throws away
  something you explicitly asked for. If a sample is awkward to produce right now, saying so is a
  useful answer in itself — we would then park WI-0003 and deliver the rest.

## Answer

The stakeholder's reply, verbatim:

> I'll send you a sample later.

That reply does not supply the layout, which is what this question asked for. What it does
settle is the choice this question put to them: it takes **option A** and refuses B and C. They
are not asking us to invent a layout, and — confirmed independently in their reply to Q-003,
*"I don't want to drop either one, the import's part of what I asked for too"* — they are not
dropping WI-0003 either. A sample is coming; it has not come yet.

So the decision taken under the deferral, by `answer-questions`, is the one this question's own
recommendation named as the response to exactly this reply: **park WI-0003 and deliver the rest
of the epic.** Concretely:

- WI-0003 is moved to `blocked` with `resume-to: draft`. It cannot be refined — its own notes
  already record that neither of its acceptance criteria can be made decidable without the
  layout — and refining it would mean inventing option B under another name.
- What unblocks WI-0003 is precisely one thing: a sample of the bank's CSV export, or its header
  row and two or three example rows naming which column holds the amount, the date and the
  description. When that arrives, WI-0003 returns to `draft` and refinement proceeds.
- EP-001 and its other two items are not held up. WI-0001 and WI-0002 do not depend on the CSV
  layout in any way.
- The second thing this question raised — that a bank row says an amount was charged but not who
  shared it — is untouched and stays untouched. It is a consequence of the layout and is recorded
  on WI-0003 as refinement's to settle once the sample exists.

Nothing here claims the layout is known. It is not, and no artifact says otherwise.
