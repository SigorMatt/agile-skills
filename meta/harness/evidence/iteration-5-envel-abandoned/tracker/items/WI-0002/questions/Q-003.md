## Context

WI-0002, refinement round 1, question 3 of 3 — the last of them, and the same conversation as
Q-001. Nothing else is open on this item after these three.

You will type `envel spend groceries 12.50` more often than you type anything else in this tool,
and nothing in the record says what happens on the screen when you do. Today's commands set the
precedent both ways: `envel new groceries` says nothing and exits, while `envel list` exists only
to print.

It matters for two reasons that are not about taste. The first is catching a mistake at the moment
you make it: `envel spend gorceries 12.50` is refused because that envelope does not exist, but
`envel spend groceries 1250` is not — it is a perfectly good spend of the wrong amount, and you
will only find it when the balance looks wrong later. The second is the date. This item lets you
type `--date 2026-08-31` for a shop you did last week, and if the tool prints nothing back, there
is no way to see which day a transaction actually landed on until the monthly summary exists.

## Question

When a recording command succeeds, what should the tool write to the screen — nothing, or
something; and if something, should it include the date the transaction was filed under?

## Options considered

- **A — nothing.** It exits 0 and says nothing, the way `envel new` does today. Consequence: the
  quietest possible tool, and the ordinary Unix convention — no news is good news. Cost: no
  confirmation of what was recorded, and the date a transaction landed on is invisible until
  WI-0003's summary is built.
- **B — the new balance of that envelope.** `groceries: 327.50 left`, or words to that effect.
  Consequence: the thing you actually want to know after recording a spend, without a second
  command. A mistyped amount is obvious immediately, because the balance jumps. Cost: still silent
  about the date, so a wrong `--date` goes unnoticed.
- **C — what was recorded, and the new balance** — the envelope, the amount, the date it was filed
  under, and what is left. Consequence: everything that could have gone wrong in the command is on
  the screen, including a mistyped date and a spend that landed in the wrong envelope. It also
  makes the date checkable at all, which no other command in this item can do. Cost: the most
  output, on the command you type most often; if you record ten things in a row it is ten lines
  rather than none.
- **Recommendation (ours, not yours):** **C**. The two mistakes this tool cannot otherwise catch
  are the wrong amount and the wrong date, and C shows both back to you at the moment you can still
  fix them cheaply. If it turns out to be too chatty, quietening it later costs one line of code
  and breaks nothing.

## Answer

[human] C — tell me what you recorded: the envelope, the amount, the date it went under, and
what's left. One line, not three. Catching a fat-fingered amount or a wrong date while I'm still
looking at the screen is worth the extra output.
