## Context

EP-001, intake round 1, question 1 of 5. These five arrived together and are one conversation
about the envelope-budgeting tool you asked for; they are split into separate files only so that
each answer has its own place in the record.

You said:

> A command-line tool for envelope budgeting: I set up named envelopes, put income into
> them, record spending against an envelope, and see what's left in each. I also want a
> simple monthly summary I can look at. Data must survive between runs. Python, no
> services.

Here is how that has been understood, and everything else built here rests on it:

You divide your money into named pots — "groceries", "rent", "petrol" — and you want to keep
that habit at a terminal rather than on paper. The tool lets you name your pots, put money into
them as income arrives, and record each thing you spend against the pot it came out of. At any
moment you can ask what is left in each pot, and you can ask for a summary of a month. Everything
you record stays on your own machine, in a file the tool manages, and is still there next time
you run it. It is written in Python and runs as a plain command you type; there is no server, no
account, no network, and nobody else uses your data.

That reading has been used to write one epic (`tracker/items/EP-001/item.md`) and three work
items (WI-0001 envelopes, WI-0002 income and spending, WI-0003 monthly summary).

## Question

Is that restatement of what you want correct, and if not, what is wrong with it?

## Options considered

This is a confirmation rather than a choice between options: it asks whether a paraphrase of
your own words is faithful, and only you can say. Two things in it were inferred rather than
stated, and are the most likely places for it to be wrong:

- **A —** "no services" was read as covering *any* server, daemon, hosted account or network
  access, not just a hosted web service. Consequence: nothing built here will ever make a network
  call, including for exchange rates or bank imports.
- **B —** "data must survive between runs" was read as *on this one machine, for you alone* — a
  local file, not something synced or shared. Consequence: no thought is given to two people, two
  machines, or two copies of the data.
- **Recommendation:** we think the restatement is faithful, and A and B are the readings we would
  keep. Say so and we proceed; correct any part of it and the epic is rewritten before anything
  is designed.

## Answer

[human] Yes, that's right. A and B are both correct — no network for anything, and it's my
machine and my data, just me using it.
