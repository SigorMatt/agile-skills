## Context

WI-0001, refinement round 1, question 2 of 2 — the last of them, and the same conversation as
Q-001. Nothing else is open on this item after these two.

Envelopes are identified by their name and by nothing else, so "is this the same envelope?" is a
question the tool has to answer on every single command, and it is the one that decides whether
your spending lands where you meant it to. If names are compared exactly as typed, then creating
`Groceries` on Monday and spending against `groceries` on Tuesday gives you two envelopes and a
balance that is wrong in both. If names are folded to lower case, you cannot have `Rent` and
`rent` mean different things — which nobody wants — but you also cannot have the tool print your
name back the way you capitalised it unless it stores that separately.

The second half of this is what a name may contain at all. `groceries` is easy. `eating out` has a
space in it and will need quoting at the shell. An empty name, or one made only of spaces, has to
be refused by something.

This decides the criterion for a rejected name, which is deliberately not written on WI-0001 yet,
and it decides whether AC5 (creating the same envelope twice is refused) fires on a difference of
case.

## Question

When are two envelope names the same envelope, and what may a name contain?

## Options considered

- **A — exact match, and any non-empty name goes.** `Groceries` and `groceries` are two different
  envelopes; spaces and punctuation are allowed; only an empty or all-whitespace name is refused.
  Consequence: the simplest rule to explain and the tool never second-guesses you; a capitalisation
  slip silently creates a second envelope, which is exactly the failure that makes budget totals
  wrong.
- **B — case-insensitive match, stored as you first typed it.** `Groceries` and `groceries` are the
  same envelope, and it is listed back with the capitalisation you used when you created it;
  spaces allowed; empty or all-whitespace refused. Consequence: the slip above becomes impossible
  and the display still looks like you wrote it; there is a small surprise the first time the tool
  tells you `groceries` already exists when you typed `Groceries`.
- **C — case-insensitive, and restrict names to letters, digits, hyphen and underscore.** As B, but
  `eating out` is refused and you would write `eating-out`. Consequence: no shell quoting, ever,
  and names are safe in any context; it forbids something you may well want, and the refusal will
  be annoying the day you hit it.
- **Recommendation:** we lean to B. The wrong-envelope failure in A is silent and corrupts the
  numbers the tool exists to get right, and C buys tidiness at the cost of telling you how to name
  your own pots.

## Answer

[human] B. `Groceries` and `groceries` are the same pot and I'd rather be told it already exists
than end up with two. I want spaces — "eating out" is one of mine — so don't restrict the
characters.
