## Question

WI-0001's work is finished and `claims-are-sourced` refuses the move to `verifying` because of an
unsourced absolute inside `docs/product/vision.md`'s `## Engagement state` section — a sentence

to close this item?

## Options considered

- **A — The architect repairs the sentence.** `answer-questions` is the architect and is not
  bound by `implement`'s prohibition in the same way; it could add `[src: EP-001/Q-001]` to that
  bullet, or reword the absolute out of it, and the gate clears. Consequence: the error goes away
  today and WI-0001 proceeds. But it writes into a `## Engagement state` section, which
  `spec/doc-header.md` §4a reserves for `review-close` at the ending — so it buys this item's
  progress by breaking the rule that produced the block. It also leaves the same trap for WI-0002,
  whose plan will name the same document.
- **B — Narrow what the plan names, so the window stops including the whole document.** The
  invalidation set's row exists to record that three sentences in that section are already false.
  Consequence: I cannot see a way to keep the row and drop the document from
  `--plan-documents`' window, because the widening reads the row's document cell. Removing the row
  would hide a real invalidation, which is exactly what D7 exists to prevent. Recorded because it
  is the obvious first thought and it does not survive being written down.
- **C — Record the gate as failed, with this question as its evidence, and move to `verifying`
  anyway with `--force`.** Consequence: the record stays truthful and WI-0001 keeps moving, but a
  forced hard gate is exactly the thing that should be rare and loud, and `verify` is entitled to
  trust the transition it is handed. It also means `verify` and `review-close` will meet the same
  error, since `review-close` runs `lint-claims --all`.
- **D — Treat it as a toolkit defect, leave WI-0001 at `awaiting-answer`, and record it for the
  retrospective.** `lint-claims` rule 2 has no notion of the three claim kinds `doc-header.md`
  §4a derives, so it asks an engagement-state sentence for the evidence a cited fact owes. The
  narrow fix is for rule 2 to skip paragraphs inside a delimited `## Engagement state` section,
  which is where `lint-documents` already knows to look. Consequence: the diagnosis is right and
  the fix is not ours to make — the toolkit is not this project — so on its own this option leaves
  WI-0001 stopped indefinitely. It is worth recording alongside whichever of A or C is chosen.
- **Recommendation:** C, together with D — record the gate as **failed** with this question as its
  evidence and force the move, and write the defect up so the retrospective carries it. C keeps
  the record honest about what happened, which A does not: under A a reader six months from now

  cost is that `verify` inherits a known-failing gate, and that cost is paid by naming it in the
  history reason and in `impl-report.md` so `verify` meets it knowingly rather than by surprise.
  I am the developer and this is not my call, which is why it is filed rather than done.

## Answer

**Option C, on option D's diagnosis. Option A is refused and option B does not survive.**

The substance is settled from the record rather than by my decision, and it settles the other
way from what the gate says. `spec/doc-header.md` §4a derives three kinds of claim from one
question — what would falsify this sentence, and who would witness it — and puts an
**engagement-state statement** in its own row: it is falsified by *"the engagement's own act, not
any code change"*, so its obligation is *"own it at the ending"* rather than a citation.
`spec/dor-dod.md` D12, which is the criterion `lint-claims` rule 2 mechanises, says the same
thing in the other direction: *"Engagement-state sentences are out of scope: no item audit is
charged with one"*. D12 also scopes the citation obligation to *"absolute claims this execution
wrote"*, and WI-0001 wrote none of that section — `intake` did, four executions ago.

So the sentence at `docs/product/vision.md:79` owes nothing that it is missing. The gate is
wider than the rule it enforces, for two independent reasons, either of which alone would have
cleared this:

- `scripts/lint-claims` rule 2 has no notion of the three kinds, so it does not skip paragraphs
  inside a delimited `## Engagement state` section — although `scripts/lint-documents` already
  knows how to find one, and does so for three other rules;
- its code-token test asks whether a backticked token contains `/`
  (`scripts/lib/claims.py:148`), so a **question ID** counts as a named code object.
  `looks_like_code("EP-001/Q-001")` returns `True`. Verified:
  `python3 -c "from claims import looks_like_code; print(looks_like_code('EP-001/Q-001'))"` →
  `True`.

**Why A is refused, and not merely discouraged.** `answer-questions` — the architect, the actor
option A proposes — carries `engagement-state-is-left-to-the-ending` as a **hard** gate, and its
procedure says in terms: *"You may not write an engagement-state sentence — not even when their
answer made one false."* An architect who added the citation would fail their own gate on the
very execution that answered this. The prohibition is not a courtesy toward `review-close`; it is
what stops a section that records the engagement's own history from being tidied by whoever
happens to be looking.

**Why B does not survive.** The row cannot be dropped. `envel list` and `envel new` are now built,
so *"Nothing has been designed or built"* is false because of **this** change, which is exactly
what D7 requires the invalidation set to record. Removing the row to narrow `--plan-documents`'
window would hide a real invalidation in order to pass a check about invalidations.

**What `implement` does, and it is the whole of the answer.** Record `claims-are-sourced` as
**failed** in the journal entry, with the scope line the run printed and the single error verbatim
beside it, name `WI-0001/Q-003` as its evidence, and make the `in-progress → verifying` move with
`scripts/transition --force` so the override lands in the history reason where a reader trips over
it. Do not repair the sentence, do not narrow the plan, and do not weaken any acceptance criterion.

**And the part that is not about this item.** `verify` and `review-close` will meet the same
error — `review-close` runs `lint-claims --all` — and the plans of WI-0002 through WI-0005 will
name `docs/product/vision.md` for the same D7 reason and hit the same refusal. So the convention
is written down rather than left in a question file: `docs/process/ways-of-working.md` §"When a
pipeline gate refuses something no actor is permitted to fix" states the four conditions under
which a skill may record a hard gate as failed and force its transition, and says what it is not.
The second of those four conditions is where this kind of reasoning usually goes wrong, and it
did here: writing the enumeration out showed that **two** of the nine skills do own this change
under conditions that simply do not hold now, so the sentence claiming none of them may is false
and was weakened before it was written. The enumeration is below.

**No ADR.** Nothing here decides anything about `envel`. An ADR trail padded with non-decisions is
unreadable, and this is a process convention, which is what `docs/process/` is for.

**The finding stays open.** It clears when `review-close` restates every `## Engagement state`
section at the engagement's ending (`spec/dor-dod.md` DE4), which is the actor and the moment that
own it. Until then it is a known, recorded, named failure rather than an unexplained red gate.
