## Context

Your idea says an expense is "paid by someone and shared by some or all". That pins down *who*
shares, but not *how much each of them shares*. Intake read the idea as stated and found nothing
that settles it, and this is not a detail — it decides what an expense record has to store, and
therefore what WI-0002 builds and what WI-0003 can compute.

The real cases are ordinary ones: a restaurant bill where one person had the expensive thing; a
holiday flat where two people take the double room; a taxi where one person is dropped off
halfway.

## Question

When several people share an expense, is the amount always divided equally between them, or do
you need to be able to say that some of them owe more than others?

## Options considered

- **A — Always equal.** An expense stores the payer, the amount and the list of sharers; each
  sharer owes amount ÷ number of sharers. Consequence: the simplest thing that works; WI-0002
  and WI-0003 get materially smaller. If you later need unequal shares, the stored expenses have
  to grow a new field and old data has to be migrated.
- **B — Equal by default, with an optional per-person amount.** You can say "£90, shared by Ana,
  Ben and Cass, but Cass's share is £40". Consequence: covers the restaurant case with one extra
  option; the tool has to check the parts add up to the total, and WI-0003's arithmetic has to
  handle both shapes. Adds work to WI-0002 and to WI-0003.
- **C — Equal by default, with optional weights or shares** ("Ana 1, Ben 1, Cass 2").
  Consequence: covers the holiday-flat case naturally and the restaurant case awkwardly;
  reintroduces the rounding problem of WI-0003/Q-002 in a harder form.
- **Recommendation:** A for now, because it is the only reading your words actually support, and
  because unequal shares can be added later as a separate item without changing the report's
  behaviour for equal ones. Choose B if you already know the restaurant case will come up in the
  first week — retrofitting it is more expensive than building it now.

## Answer

[human] Equal is fine for now — most of the time we're just splitting a bill evenly. Don't need per-person amounts.

**Read by `answer-questions` as option A — always equal.** The stakeholder took the
recommendation and declined the per-person amount of option B explicitly. Route: recorded
intent — their own words settle it, so no ADR was written for the choice itself.

What follows, stated so that `plan` and `implement` are bound by it:

- An expense stores the payer, the amount, a description and the list of sharers. It stores
  nothing about how the amount divides, because there is nothing to store: each sharer's share
  is the amount divided by the number of sharers.
- The command must not accept a per-person amount. Making it silently ignore one would leave a
  user believing they had recorded an uneven split; AC1 now says it is neither recorded nor
  accepted.
- "for now" is recorded as a scope boundary, not as a hedge to design around. Unequal shares are
  out of scope on both WI-0002 and EP-001, and the note says plainly that adding them later
  means a new item and a change to what an expense stores. `plan` should not pre-build a shape
  for shares that nothing asks for.
- The arithmetic of an equal division that does not come out evenly is not this item's job. It
  is fixed by ADR-0001 and checked by WI-0003 AC6; WI-0002's `## Notes` points there so the two
  halves of the same rule are findable from either end.
