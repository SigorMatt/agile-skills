## Context

Q-001 on this item settled what the report *is*: the payments to actually make, so everyone ends
up square — not a listing of every debt as it arose. AC1 requires those payments.

What is still open is whether anything else appears alongside them. When Q-001 was propagated,
`answer-questions` deliberately did not add a requirement you had not asked for, and recorded
this as a presentation choice for refinement. It comes back to you because two things turn on it:

- **AC2 asks a reader to reproduce the report's amounts by hand.** That is much easier if the
  screen also shows where each person stands overall, because the transfers are derived from
  exactly those numbers.
- **You explicitly declined the debt-by-debt listing.** That rules out one of the options below.
  It does not rule out the other, and I do not want to assume which way you meant it.

To be clear about the difference: a per-person balance is one line per *person* ("Cass owes
£15.00"). The thing you declined was one line per *pair* ("Cass owes Ana £10.00, Cass owes Ben
£5.00, Ben owes Ana £5.00"). They are not the same output.

## Question

When the report prints who pays whom, should it also print each person's overall balance?

## Options considered

- **A — the transfers, and nothing else.** `Cass pays Ana £15.00`. Consequence: exactly what you
  asked for and not a word more; the shortest thing to read. If a number ever looks wrong, there
  is nothing on the screen to check it against — you would have to redo the arithmetic from the
  expense list.
- **B — each person's balance, then the transfers.** `Ana is owed £15.00` / `Ben is square` /
  `Cass owes £15.00`, and then the payments. Consequence: you can see at a glance whether you are
  up or down, and the hand-check AC2 demands becomes easy because the balances are the working.
  Costs one line per person — four lines for a group of four.
- **C — the transfers, each annotated with the expenses behind it.** Consequence: looks like it
  explains itself. It cannot: the whole point of netting is that a transfer does not correspond
  to any particular expense, so the annotation would be misleading exactly when you most want to
  trust it. It is also close to the listing you declined.
- **Recommendation:** B. It is a strict addition to what you asked for rather than a substitute,
  it is what makes the report auditable by anyone in the group, and it is not the pairwise listing
  you turned down. Say A if you want the output kept to the minimum.

## Answer

[human] Yeah, show each person's balance too, not just the payments — makes it easier to check.

**Read by `answer-questions` as option B, in the stakeholder's own words**, with their reason
attached: "makes it easier to check". Route: recorded intent — `answered-by` is `human`, and no ADR
was written, because the decision is theirs rather than the architect's.

What this settles, and what it deliberately leaves alone:

- **The report has two parts, balances first, then the payments.** The order is not arbitrary: the
  payments are derived from the balances, so a reader doing AC2's hand-check reads them in that
  order, and their stated reason for wanting the balances is exactly that check.
- **A balance line is one line per person.** `Ana is owed 15.00`, `Ben is square`, `Cass owes
  15.00`. This is the distinction the question drew and the stakeholder acted on: it is not the
  per-*pair* listing they declined in Q-001, and that listing stays out of scope on this item.
- **Everyone registered gets a line, including people who are square.** A person missing from the
  report because they happen to owe nothing looks like a person the tool forgot, and this epic has
  no way to check a person's status any other way.
- **Amounts print to two decimal places with no currency symbol.** This is not a new decision — it
  follows from `docs/product/vision.md` ("Not multi-currency": every amount is in one currency and
  the tool does not name it) and from ADR-0001, which holds money in the minor unit and formats at
  the boundary. It is stated in AC7 because "an amount" is otherwise not something `verify` can
  compare against.
- **Option C was already ruled out by the record, not by preference.** Annotating each payment with
  the expenses behind it cannot be done honestly: netting means a payment need not correspond to
  any single expense, which WI-0003's own `## Out of scope` says.
