## Question

Who repairs the two unsourced absolute claims in `docs/product/vision.md` that this item's
`claims-are-sourced` gate now reports, given that one of them sits inside `## Engagement state`
and `implement` may not write there?

## Options considered

- **A — `answer-questions` repairs both in place, as provenance-only edits, and `WI-0001`
  resumes at `in-progress`.** `doc-header.md` §5 lists `answer-questions` as an updater of
  `vision.md`, and the architect persona is the one this pipeline gives document authority to.
  `:42` gains a citation or is hedged; `:66` gains a citation for its quantifier.
  Consequence: the gate passes on the next `implement` run and the item finishes. It costs one
  round trip and one version bump of `vision.md`. The open question it raises is whether adding a
  citation to an engagement-state sentence is "writing" it in §4a's sense — my reading is that
  §4a reserves the *assertion* to the ending and says nothing about its provenance, and that
  `answer-questions` is entitled to decide that where `implement` is not. The `## Engagement
  state` bullet is also **false** as well as uncited, and A does not fix that: the ending still
  owns the restatement, which is what the invalidation row says.
- **B — `plan` narrows the invalidation set so `vision.md` is not named, and the gate's window
  no longer contains it.** Consequence: the gate passes immediately and nothing is repaired. It
  is refused on its merits: the three engagement-state rows are the honest output of D7, one of
  them is the row that exists precisely so the ending finds a sentence sitting *outside* the
  delimited section, and deleting them to satisfy a lint rule is the failure mode the invalidation
  set exists to prevent.
- **C — force the gate: transition with `--force`, recording `[gates forced]` and the two
  claims as an accepted gap.** Consequence: the item ships and the workspace carries two known,
  permanent lint errors nobody owns. This is F-069's shape exactly — a rule nobody can satisfy,
  cleared by an override — and it is the outcome the escalation protocol exists to avoid, so it
  is listed to be refused rather than as a real candidate.
- **D — file a bug item for the two claims and let `WI-0001` proceed.** Consequence: the gate
  still fails now, because the gate is run against this branch head, so this does not unblock
  anything. It would only make sense alongside A or B.
- **Recommendation: A**, with the observation that the underlying problem is a toolkit one and
  worth recording as such: `--plan-documents` widens `lint-claims`' scope to whole documents
  while `doc-header.md` §4a narrows `implement`'s write permission to a sub-section of them, and
  the two boundaries do not line up. Any item whose plan names a document containing an unsourced
  absolute in its `## Engagement state` section will hit this, whatever it changed.

## Answer

**Option A, split in two, because the two claims are not alike — and the half of A that the
question proposed for `:66` turns out to be forbidden to me as well.**

**`docs/product/vision.md:42` — repaired here.** It is in `## What it is for`, an ordinary product
sentence, and `spec/doc-header.md` §5 lists `answer-questions` as an updater of `vision.md`. The
assertion is unchanged; it gains `[src: WI-0001/Q-001; EP-001]`, which is where the claim actually
comes from — the stakeholder's own reason for wanting the breakdown at all. `vision.md` is v3 → v4
with a change-log row.

**`docs/product/vision.md:66` — not repaired, by anybody, before the ending.** The question's
option A proposed that `answer-questions` repair both. It cannot. This skill's own procedure
forbids writing an engagement-state sentence in terms — *"You may not write an engagement-state
sentence — not even when their answer made one false"* — and its self-check asks the same question
a second time. So the set of actors permitted to touch that sentence between `intake` and the
ending is empty: `implement` is forbidden at any disposition, `verify` writes no document at all,
`review-close`'s restatement rule reports *not applicable* at an item close, and this skill is
forbidden here. That is not a gap in the analysis the question offered; it is the analysis going
one step further than the asker could.

**So the gate cannot be made to pass, and the override is authorised rather than improvised.**
`scripts/transition --force` exists for exactly this — *"If a gate legitimately cannot pass, add
--force; the override is then recorded in the history reason instead of being invisible"* — and
**ADR-0004** bounds it to four conditions that `verify` and `review-close` can each re-run:

1. `implement` first runs `scripts/run-gate --skill implement --item WI-0001 --all --resolving
   'WI-0001:in-progress->verifying'` and records **every** verdict that run produced in its journal
   entry. This is what replaces the gate run `--force` skips, and it is the condition that matters
   most, because `--force` skips all nine rather than the one that cannot pass.
2. `claims-are-sourced` is the only hard gate reported failing.
3. Its failure is exactly one error — `claim.unsourced` at `docs/product/vision.md:66` — quoted
   verbatim in the journal entry.
4. The history reason names ADR-0004.

If any of the four fails to hold, the authorisation does not apply and the item stays at
`in-progress`. It has been checked as satisfiable rather than asserted: after the `:42` repair,
`lint-claims --all` exits 1 with exactly one error, at `docs/product/vision.md:66`
[src: run: python3 .claude/agile-skills/scripts/lint-claims --all → exit 1, 1 error, claim.unsourced at docs/product/vision.md:66].

**Options B, C and D are refused**, with reasons, in ADR-0004 `## Options considered`. Briefly: B
(repair both) is the prohibited act and the prohibition exists because two real executions did it,
both declared, both defensible, both authorised by nothing; C (drop `vision.md` from the
invalidation set) deletes three true rows to quiet a lint, one of which exists precisely so the
ending finds a sentence sitting *outside* the delimited section; D (file a bug and proceed) does
not unblock anything, since the gate runs against the branch head either way. The question also
listed forcing as option C and refused it — this answer takes it, and the difference is that it is
now bounded, recorded in an ADR, and checkable.

**Nothing about the code is in question and nothing about it changed.** `WI-0001` resumes at
`in-progress` with its branch exactly as `implement` left it: four commits, 27 tests, eight of nine
gates passing. What remains for `implement` is to re-run the gates, record them, and transition.

**This is a toolkit finding and is written up as one** (ADR-0004 `## Consequences`, last bullet):
`lint-claims --plan-documents` widens the claims window to whole documents, while the
engagement-state rule narrows every mid-flight skill's write permission to a sub-section of them,
and the two boundaries do not line up. Any item whose plan names a document carrying an unsourced
absolute inside its `## Engagement state` section meets this, whatever that item changed. The
natural fix upstream is for `lint-claims` rule 2 to skip `## Engagement state` sections, which it
does not do today — the phrase appears nowhere in `scripts/lint-claims` or in
`scripts/lib/claims.py`.
