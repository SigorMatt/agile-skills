## Context

WI-0002's AC5 says an amount that is not a positive number is rejected. That leaves the shape of
a valid amount unstated, and "who owes whom" is arithmetic on these numbers, so how they are
stored decides whether the totals can be trusted. Two things are undecided: how many decimal
places an entered amount may have, and what the tool prints back.

There is a technical trap here that I will handle in the design either way — money held as
ordinary floating-point numbers accumulates error, so 0.1 + 0.2 is not 0.3 — but it only stays
handled if the input rule is decided rather than discovered. What I need from you is the human
part: what you will actually type.

Definition of Ready criteria this blocks: **R4** for AC5 — "not a positive number" is not
decidable without knowing what counts as a number here.

## Question

What may an amount look like when you type it — is `12.5`, `12.50` and `12` all fine, and is
anything with more than two decimal places (`12.345`) an error or is it rounded?

## Options considered

- **A — Up to two decimal places; more is an error.** `12`, `12.5` and `12.50` are all
  accepted and mean the same amount; `12.345` is rejected with a message and records nothing.
  Consequence: the stored data is exact, every printed figure has an obvious meaning, and you are
  told when you fat-finger an amount rather than having it silently changed.
- **B — Up to two decimal places; more is rounded to the nearest penny.** `12.345` becomes
  `12.35` and the tool says so. Consequence: never blocks you over a pasted figure, at the cost
  of the tool quietly altering a number about money.
- **C — Whole units only.** Only `12` is valid. Consequence: the arithmetic gets much simpler
  and the rounding question in WI-0003 nearly disappears. It is also useless for real bills,
  which end in `.47`.
- **Recommendation:** **A**. Rejecting is better than rounding for a tool that is meant to be
  trustworthy about money, and the case it rejects — more than two decimals — is nearly always a
  typo. Note this is about *input*: WI-0003 still has to decide what happens when a valid amount
  like `10.00` is split three ways, and that is WI-0003/Q-002.

## Answer

[human] Reject anything with more than two decimal places, I'd rather know than have it change
my number without telling me.

**[answer-questions]** Recorded as **option A — at most two decimal places; more is an error,
never a rounding.** "I'd rather know than have it change my number without telling me" rejects
option B explicitly. Option C (whole units only) is not in play; the answer presupposes decimals.

So `12`, `12.5` and `12.50` are all accepted and `12.5` and `12.50` are the same amount, while
`12.345` is rejected with a message and nothing is recorded. The existing AC5 already rejected
non-positive amounts; it now also carries the decimal rule, so one criterion covers what a valid
amount is rather than two criteria that could drift apart.

One consequence the question flagged and this answer makes binding: because input is never
rounded, the arithmetic must not round either where it can be avoided. An amount must not be held
as a binary float — `12.10` is not representable in one — or `WI-0003`'s "amounts net to zero, to
the last minor unit" criterion becomes a matter of luck. The store and the balance calculation
work in integer minor units, converting at the boundary. That is recorded in this item's Notes
where the implementer will read it.

This question's own closing remark still stands and is *not* answered here: rejecting `12.345` on
input says nothing about what happens when a valid `10.00` is divided by three. That is
`WI-0003/Q-002`, answered separately and recorded as
`docs/architecture/adr/ADR-0004-payer-absorbs-the-rounding-remainder.md`.
