## Context

"Alice paid 30, shared by Bob and Carol." Does Alice have a share of that expense?

Both readings are common and they give different answers to the question the tool exists to
answer. If Alice is included, Bob and Carol owe her 10 each. If she is not, they owe her 15 each.
Nothing in WI-0002's criteria, the epic, or your original idea settles it, and the difference is
invisible in the output — the numbers just come out wrong, and nobody can tell by looking.

I would rather have this in writing than discover it during verification, because a
"who owes whom" report that is quietly 50% wrong is the single worst thing this tool could do.

Definition of Ready criteria this blocks: **R4** and **R10** for AC1 and AC2 — "the people who
shared it" is currently ambiguous about whether the payer is one of them.

## Question

When you record an expense, is the payer automatically one of the people sharing it, or is the
list of sharers exactly the people who share — so that a payer who is not on the list paid for
other people entirely?

## Options considered

- **A — The sharers you list are exactly the sharers.** "Alice paid 30, shared by Bob and Carol"
  means Bob and Carol owe 15 each; to include Alice you list her too. Consequence: no hidden
  rule, and "I bought the taxi for you two, I walked" is expressible directly. The cost is
  having to type your own name in the common case, and forgetting to is a silent error.
- **B — The payer is always included in the sharers.** "Alice paid 30, shared by Bob and Carol"
  means all three share, so Bob and Carol owe 10 each. Consequence: matches the common case
  (someone pays for a group they are part of) with the least typing. The cost is that paying for
  other people without sharing becomes impossible to express, or needs a special flag.
- **C — Neither is implicit; you must always list everyone who shares, including yourself, and
  the tool warns if the payer is not among them.** Consequence: A's precision with a guard
  against A's silent error. Costs a warning nobody may read.
- **Recommendation:** **A**, with the "shared by everyone" shorthand from AC2 as the answer to
  the typing cost — in the common case you would write "shared by everyone" rather than listing
  names at all, so B's saving mostly disappears. Say the word and I will record **B** instead;
  what matters is that it is written down before anyone computes a balance.

## Answer

[human] If I paid and it's shared by all of us, include me automatically — most of the time I'm
one of the people splitting it too.

**[answer-questions]** Recorded as **option A, with option C's guard**, and with the everyone
case made the default so that the stakeholder's stated need is met without them typing anything.
The full decision, its alternatives and its reversibility are
`docs/architecture/adr/ADR-0003-sharers-are-exactly-who-you-name.md`.

The answer is conditional, and the condition matters. *"**If I paid and it's shared by all of
us**, include me automatically"* settles the everyone case and says nothing about an explicit
sharer list that omits the payer. Under A those are wholly different commands; under B they are
the same command. Reading the sentence as a blanket vote for B would be reading past its first
clause.

What is recorded:

1. Naming no sharers records the expense as shared by everyone currently in the group, the payer
   included — no flag, no names. This is the stakeholder's case, and it costs them fewer
   keystrokes than option B would have.
2. An explicit list means exactly those people. "I bought the taxi for you two, I walked" stays
   expressible; under B it is inexpressible without a special flag.
3. The sharer set is frozen at the moment of recording, so adding a friend to the group next week
   does not silently re-divide last week's dinner.
4. If an explicit list omits the payer, the expense is recorded, the exit status is zero, and a
   note goes to stderr saying the payer is not sharing and is owed the whole amount. This is
   option C's guard, and it is what makes A's silent-error risk visible.

**Why this was decided rather than escalated.** None of the four conditions in
`spec/question.md` §4 is met for the case the stakeholder spoke to — they answered it. For the
case they did not, the decision is reversible *now*, and the guard in point 4 bounds the damage
in the meantime. It is worth saying plainly that it stops being reversible once expenses exist:
the stored record cannot distinguish "Alice deliberately did not share" from "Alice forgot to
type her own name", so a later switch to option B could not be applied to history. That is why
the item's Notes ask anyone who has the stakeholder's attention before implementation to confirm
it, rather than leaving the caveat only in this file.
