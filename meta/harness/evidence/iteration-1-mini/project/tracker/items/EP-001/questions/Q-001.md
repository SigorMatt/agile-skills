## Context

Your idea says an expense is "paid by someone and shared by some or all". I read that as: the
people who shared it each owe an equal part of it. That is the simplest reading and it is what
the epic's worked example assumes (Alice pays 30 for a dinner shared by Alice, Bob and Carol →
Bob and Carol each owe Alice 10).

But real group spending often is not equal — one person had the steak, two people shared a
double room and one had a single, someone only came for half the trip. I am not going to guess
which world you are in, because it changes the shape of the stored data and of every command
that records an expense: equal shares need only a list of names, uneven shares need an amount or
a weight per person, and retro-fitting the second onto the first means rewriting the store and
the commands.

**A note on this question's `blocking` field.** This question blocks the epic in substance:
nothing downstream should be designed or built on a guess about it. It is nevertheless recorded
as `blocking: false`, because the toolkit makes `blocking: true` impossible on an epic. In
`.claude/agile-skills/pipeline.yaml` the epic status `open` is `terminal: true`, and the only
transitions into `awaiting-answer` or `blocked` are `from: any-non-terminal` — so there is no
legal transition that suspends an epic. Meanwhile `scripts/validate-workspace` reports
`question.blocking.not-suspended` if an epic carries an open blocking question. The two rules
contradict each other, and `intake`'s own escalation instruction ("set the epic to
`awaiting-answer`") cannot be carried out. Being addressed to `human` is what actually stops the
pipeline here (`spec/question.md` rule 4, and `pipeline.yaml` `orchestrator.steps` step 2), and
that does not depend on `blocking`. The work items this question bears on carry their own
blocking questions and are properly suspended.

## Question

When an expense is shared, is it always split **equally** among the people who shared it, or
must the tool also support **uneven** splits — and if uneven, in which form: exact amounts per
person, percentages, or integer shares (Alice 2 parts, Bob 1 part)?

## Options considered

- **A — Equal split only.** An expense records an amount, a payer, and a list of sharers; each
  sharer owes amount ÷ number of sharers. Consequence: the simplest thing that could work, and
  the fastest to a usable tool. Anything uneven has to be faked by entering two expenses, and
  supporting it properly later is a change to the stored format.
- **B — Equal by default, exact amounts when you ask for them.** The common case stays a list of
  names; an expense may instead carry an explicit amount per person, which must sum to the
  total. Consequence: covers the steak case exactly, costs an extra input form and a validation
  rule ("the parts must add up"), and the store carries a per-person amount from day one.
- **C — Equal by default, integer shares when you ask for them.** As B, but you give weights
  (Alice 2, Bob 1) and the tool divides. Consequence: better for "she stayed twice as long",
  worse for "his main was 8 more than mine", and it reopens the rounding question because
  weighted division rarely comes out exact.
- **Recommendation:** **A** for this epic, with B recorded as a follow-up epic if you want it.
  The reason is sequencing rather than taste: an equal-split tool is useful the day it works,
  and B is a strictly larger data model on top of it. If uneven splits are the actual reason you
  want this tool, say so now — that is much cheaper than discovering it after WI-0002 ships.

## Answer

[human] Equal split is all I need — that's what I meant.

**[answer-questions]** Recorded as **option A — equal splits only**. The stakeholder's wording
("that's what I meant") confirms the reading the epic already assumed, so nothing has to be
unwound; what changes is that it is now a decision rather than an assumption, and the option-B
data model is closed off. An expense therefore stores a list of sharer names and no per-person
amount, weight or percentage. This is deliberately the *smaller* data model: adding weights
later would mean rewriting the store and the recording command, and the stakeholder has said
they do not need it.
