## Context

Your idea covers recording expenses and asking who owes whom. It does not say what happens
**after** someone pays their debt. If Bob owes Alice 10 and hands her a note, nothing in the
tool as described lets the group record that, so the tool would keep reporting the same debt
forever and the group would go back to remembering things — which is the problem it exists to
solve.

I have put this in the epic's Out of scope for now so it is visible rather than assumed, but I
need your call, because "record a repayment" is roughly a fourth work item and it also decides
whether "who owes whom" is computed over expenses alone or over expenses and payments together.

**A note on this question's `blocking` field.** This question blocks the epic in substance:
nothing downstream should be designed or built on a guess about it. It is nevertheless recorded
as `blocking: false`, because the toolkit makes `blocking: true` impossible on an epic. In
`.claude/agile-skills/pipeline.yaml` the epic status `open` is `terminal: true`, and the only
transitions into `awaiting-answer` or `blocked` are `from: any-non-terminal` — so there is no
legal transition that suspends an epic. Meanwhile `scripts/validate-workspace` reports
`question.blocking.not-suspended` if an epic carries an open blocking question. The two rules
contradict each other, and `intake`'s own escalation instruction ("set the epic to
`awaiting-answer`") cannot be carried out. Being addressed to `human` is what actually stops the
pipeline here (`spec/question.md` rule 4, and `pipeline.yaml` `orchestrator.steps` step 2), and
that does not depend on `blocking`. The work items this question bears on carry their own
blocking questions and are properly suspended.

## Question

Should this tool let you record a repayment between two people ("Bob paid Alice 10") so that it
reduces what the balances say — in this epic, or not at all?

## Options considered

- **A — In scope for this epic.** A fourth work item adds a settle-up command, and the balance
  report nets expenses and repayments together. Consequence: the tool is genuinely complete —
  the group can use it across a whole trip and then close it out. Costs one more item's worth of
  work, and the balance calculation has to treat a repayment as a transfer rather than a shared
  expense.
- **B — Out of scope now; a later epic.** This epic ships expenses and balances only. To settle
  up, the group either keeps a note outside the tool or starts a fresh store. Consequence:
  fastest to something usable; the tool is honest about being incomplete. The balance code is
  written once and then changed when settling arrives.
- **C — Not needed at all.** The group only ever wants the running picture. Consequence: nothing
  to build, and the Out of scope entry becomes permanent rather than provisional.
- **Recommendation:** **B**, unless you already know your group settles up mid-trip. A repayment
  is easy to add to a balance model that was built knowing it was coming, and I will make sure
  WI-0003 records that it is coming — but it is not needed to answer "who owes whom" today.

## Answer

[human] Leave settling up out of this first version, let's get expenses and balances working
first. Also, I want to import expenses from my bank's CSV export instead of typing them in — can
we add that too?

**[answer-questions]** Two things arrived in this answer, and they are handled separately.

**1. Settling up — recorded as option B, out of scope for this epic, expected in a later one.**
"Leave settling up out of this first version" is a deferral, not a refusal, and the difference
matters to WI-0003: the recommendation attached to option B promised that WI-0003 would be built
knowing a repayment is coming. That promise is now written into WI-0003 rather than living only
here, because WI-0003's implementer will read the item, not this file. Concretely, a repayment
is a transfer between two people that changes their net positions and nothing else, so the
balance calculation must go through a net-position-per-person step that a transfer could later
be added to — not compute payments directly from expense rows.

**2. Importing from a bank's CSV export — a new request, not an answer to this question.** It is
recorded, and it is not being acted on yet. A bank statement row carries a date, a description
and an amount; it cannot say **who shared the expense**, and the sharers are the field the whole
product turns on. So "instead of typing them in" cannot be delivered as stated, and the range of
things it could mean instead — from "pre-fill the amount and date, you still say who shared it"
to "import my own spending and treat every row as shared by everyone" — differ enough that
guessing would be exactly the failure the question protocol exists to prevent. It is therefore
filed as a shaping question, `EP-001/Q-006`, rather than turned into a work item on a guess. The
epic's "Importing from a bank, a spreadsheet, or a chat export" exclusion has been narrowed to
drop the bank case, since the stakeholder has now asked for it directly, and the request is
recorded under a new "Requested, not yet scoped" heading so that it is neither silently refused
nor silently accepted.
