## Context

I have written five success measures on the epic from your idea, and they all test that the tool
*works*: data survives a restart, a hand-checkable example comes out right, the debts net to
zero, bad input does not produce a traceback, nothing is installed from the internet.

What none of them capture is the way a tool like this fails while passing every one of them: the
group stops using it. Usually that is because entering an expense takes too long at the end of a
meal, or because the output of "who owes whom" is not something you can read out loud to five
people in a pub. I cannot write a success measure for that without knowing which of those you
care about, and inventing a number ("under 10 seconds") would be a false requirement.

**A note on this question's `blocking` field.** This question blocks the epic in substance:
nothing downstream should be designed or built on a guess about it. It is nevertheless recorded
as `blocking: false`, because the toolkit makes `blocking: true` impossible on an epic. In
`.claude/agile-skills/pipeline.yaml` the epic status `open` is `terminal: true`, and the only
transitions into `awaiting-answer` or `blocked` are `from: any-non-terminal` — so there is no
legal transition that suspends an epic. Meanwhile `scripts/validate-workspace` reports
`question.blocking.not-suspended` if an epic carries an open blocking question. The two rules
contradict each other, and `intake`'s own escalation instruction ("set the epic to
`awaiting-answer`") cannot be carried out. Being addressed to `human` is what actually stops the
pipeline here (`spec/question.md` rule 4, and `pipeline.yaml` `orchestrator.steps` step 2), and
that does not depend on `blocking`. The work items this question bears on carry their own
blocking questions and are properly suspended.

## Question

What would make this a failure even if every command worked exactly as specified — and is there
an observable version of it I can add to the epic's success measures?

## Options considered

- **A — Speed of entry is what matters.** The measure becomes something like "recording an
  expense is a single command, no prompts, typed in one line". Consequence: pushes the design
  towards flags over interactive prompts, and towards short person names as identifiers.
- **B — Readability of the answer is what matters.** The measure becomes "the who-owes-whom
  output for a group of five with a dozen expenses fits on one screen and can be read aloud
  without interpretation". Consequence: pushes WI-0003 towards a short netted list rather than a
  matrix, and interacts directly with WI-0003/Q-001.
- **C — Trusting the number is what matters.** The measure becomes "any figure the tool prints
  can be traced back to the expenses that produced it, on demand". Consequence: adds a
  per-person breakdown to WI-0003 that nothing currently asks for.
- **D — Nothing beyond correctness; the five measures are enough.** Consequence: I record that
  you considered it and declined, and the epic ships against the measures as written.
- **Recommendation:** none, insufficient basis. This one depends on how your group actually
  behaves, and I have no way to observe that. B is the one I would guess at, which is exactly
  why I am asking rather than writing it in.

## Answer

[human] After a weekend away, one command tells us who pays whom and nobody argues about it —
that's what success looks like to me.

**[answer-questions]** The question asked for an observable version of "what would make this a
failure even if every command worked", and the answer supplies one sentence containing two
distinct requirements. Both have been turned into success measures on the epic rather than left
as a quotation, because a measure nobody can check is not a measure.

- *"one command tells us who pays whom"* — this is option **A** (speed of the answer), but about
  the report rather than about entry. It becomes: with people and expenses already recorded, a
  single invocation prints the payments that clear the group, with no second command and no
  arithmetic by hand. It rules out a design where you run a balance command and then work out the
  transfers yourself, and it confirms WI-0003/Q-001's answer independently.
- *"and nobody argues about it"* — this is option **B/C** (the answer has to be trustworthy). On
  its own it is not observable, so the observable form is the one WI-0003/Q-001 already asks for:
  the same output carries each person's net position alongside the payments, so anyone at the
  table can reconcile the settlement against what they remember paying. This is what makes the
  settlement checkable by a sceptic rather than something to be taken on faith — which is the
  concrete failure mode option A of WI-0003/Q-001 warned about ("Carol may be told to pay Alice
  for a taxi Bob paid for").

Option D (nothing beyond the five existing measures) is declined by the answer itself.
