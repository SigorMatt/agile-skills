## Context

WI-0001 is the roster: adding people and listing them. Its AC3 currently reads "adding a person
who is already in the group does not silently create a duplicate; the behaviour is stated and
exercised" — which is not a criterion, because it does not say what the behaviour *is*. I cannot
make it decidable without knowing what a person's identity is, and that is your call rather than
mine: it decides whether `Alice` and `alice` are one friend or two, and it is the kind of thing
that is nearly free to fix now and expensive after a store full of names exists.

Definition of Ready criteria this blocks: **R4** (every criterion decidable by observation) and
**R10** (every combination of behaviours either specified or explicitly left open) for AC3.

## Question

What identifies a person — is the name you type the identity, is it matched case-sensitively,
and what should happen when you add a name that is already in the group?

## Options considered

- **A — The name is the identity, matched exactly, and a repeat is an error.** `Alice` and
  `alice` are two different people. Adding `Alice` twice prints an error naming her and exits
  non-zero, changing nothing. Consequence: no surprises about who is who, and typos become
  visible immediately as an extra person in the listing rather than silently merging with
  someone else. The cost is that `alice` and `Alice` will eventually both appear and you will
  have to be careful.
- **B — The name is the identity, matched case-insensitively, and a repeat is an error.**
  `Alice` and `alice` are the same friend; the tool stores whichever spelling you typed first
  and reports the clash on the second. Consequence: forgiving about capitalisation at a terminal,
  which is where most of the typing happens. The cost is a rule to remember when two friends'
  names differ only by case — rare in a friend group, but it is a rule.
- **C — A repeat is silently accepted and does nothing.** Adding `Alice` twice leaves one Alice
  and exits 0. Consequence: scripts and repeated commands are safe to re-run. The cost is that a
  genuine mistake — you meant `Alex` — is invisible, and this is a tool about money.
- **Recommendation:** **B**. Case-insensitive matching costs one line and matches how people
  actually type names into a terminal; erroring on a repeat rather than swallowing it keeps
  mistakes visible, which matters more here than being re-runnable. If you have two friends
  whose names differ only in case, say so and I will record **A** instead.

## Answer

[human] None of us have names that differ only by case, so don't worry about that. If I try to
add someone who's already there, just tell me rather than quietly making a duplicate.

**[answer-questions]** Recorded as **option B — the name is the identity, matched
case-insensitively, and a repeat is an error.**

The second sentence decides the duplicate case outright: "just tell me rather than quietly making
a duplicate" rules out option C and requires an error that names the person and changes nothing.
The first sentence does not itself choose between A and B — it says the difference is
unobservable in this group. That removes the only cost the question attached to B ("a rule to
remember when two friends' names differ only by case"), so the recommendation stands rather than
being overturned, and B is recorded.

Two details the stakeholder was not asked about, decided here so that AC3 is decidable by someone
with a terminal rather than merely plausible. Both are reversible and both are recorded as
assumptions in `refinement-qa.md` rather than presented as the stakeholder's words:

- **Surrounding whitespace is stripped before matching**, so ` Alice ` and `Alice` are the same
  person. Without this the rule is silently defeated by a trailing space, which is the same
  silent-duplicate failure the stakeholder asked to be protected from.
- **The spelling kept and listed is the one entered first**, as the option-B text described. The
  alternative — last spelling wins — would let a later `add` mutate the roster while reporting an
  error, which contradicts "leaves the roster exactly as it was".
