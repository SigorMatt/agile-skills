## Context

**EP-001, the ending — one question, and it is the only one.**

You asked for this: *"A flashcard tool: add cards, review due cards daily, simple spaced
repetition. Progress persists."* Along the way you settled four things: it is a command-line tool
(*"I'll be sitting at a terminal doing this once a day, that's all it needs to be"*); every card
lives in one flat pool with no decks; the schedule is *"one day, then three, then a week, then a
month. Get it wrong and it goes back to the start"*; and a brand-new card you get right comes back
tomorrow, because *"that's the order I meant to actually see, starting from a new card."*

All three work items are finished and merged. Nothing is left running, nothing is blocked, and no
question is waiting on anyone. That is why you are being asked now: the work has stopped, and
whether it is **done** is your call rather than the pipeline's.

Here is what you would actually do with it today, run end to end on the merged code this morning:

```
$ recall add "die Katze" "the cat"
Added card 1.
$ recall add "Grüße" "greetings"
Added card 2.
$ recall review
die Katze
[Enter] to see the answer, q to stop
the cat
[y] right, [n] wrong, q to stop
y
Grüße
[Enter] to see the answer, q to stop
greetings
[y] right, [n] wrong, q to stop
n
Reviewed 2, right 1.
$ recall review
Nothing is due today.
```

Both cards are then due tomorrow — the one you got right is on the 1-day rung, the one you got
wrong is back at the start. Get the first one right again tomorrow and it goes out to three days,
then a week, then a month. All of it is in one file you can open and read (`~/.recall.json`, or
wherever `RECALL_FILE` points), and editing that file by hand is how you bring a card forward or
push it back.

## Question

The three work items under this epic, every one of them, and how each ended:

| item | what it was | ended |
|------|-------------|-------|
| **WI-0001** — Add a card and have it persist across runs | `recall add` writes a card's two sides to a JSON file on disk, and `recall list` prints the pile back in card-number order in a later run of the tool | **delivered** — closed `done`, merged |
| **WI-0002** — Review the cards that are due and record each answer | `recall review` shows every due card one at a time, question side first, takes `y`, `n` or `q` as whole typed lines, records each answer, and reports how many you reviewed and how many you got right. A card you have reviewed does not come back the same day | **delivered** — closed `done`, merged |
| **WI-0003** — Schedule the next review with simple spaced repetition | the 1 → 3 → 7 → 30-day ladder: a right answer moves a card one rung out, a wrong answer puts it back at one day, counted from the day you review rather than from the day it was due. A brand-new card is below the ladder, so its first right answer brings it back tomorrow — the reading you chose. The schedule is stored per card and survives restarts, older stores are upgraded automatically, and a scheduling value the tool cannot read stops it with a message instead of quietly dropping the card | **delivered** — closed `done`, merged |

That is every child item of this epic. **No bug was filed and left unfixed**, and there is no
fourth item sitting somewhere you were not told about. One defect was found — the schedule

Your five success measures for the epic, each checked on the merged code this morning, are in the
