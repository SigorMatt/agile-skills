## Question

Which scheduling scheme should "simple spaced repetition" mean?

## Options considered

- **A — a fixed interval ladder (Leitner).** Every card sits on a rung: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days,
  14 days, 30 days. Right moves it up one rung, wrong sends it back to rung 1. Consequence: a
  reader can work out any card's next due date by hand, which makes WI-0003 trivially verifiable.
  It ignores how hard a card is for you — a card you always get right and a card you barely get
  right advance identically.
- **B — SM-2, the Anki-style algorithm.** Each card carries an ease factor that goes up and down
  with your answers, and the interval is the previous interval times that ease. Consequence:
  adapts per card, which is genuinely better review. Costs floating-point state per card, needs
  a grade finer than right/wrong to drive the ease (see `Q-003`), and its due dates are no
  longer something you can predict or check by inspection.
- **C — doubling.** Right doubles the current interval, wrong resets it to 1 day. Consequence:
  simpler than A to describe, but the intervals run away fast — six right answers puts a card 32
  days out and the seventh puts it past two months, with nothing to cap it.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is the one that matches the word "simple", it is checkable by hand,
  and if you outgrow it, B replaces it behind the same stored cards. If you already know you
  want Anki-like behaviour, say B now rather than later — retrofitting ease factors onto stored
  cards is the expensive direction.

## Answer

[human] A card I get right comes back later each time — one day, then three, then a week, then a month. Get it wrong and it goes back to the start.
