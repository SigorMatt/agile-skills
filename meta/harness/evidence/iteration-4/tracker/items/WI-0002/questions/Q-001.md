settled the frame: it is a command-line tool, you record right or wrong, and there are no decks.

The session has four moments: the question side is on screen and you want to see the answer; you

## Question

During a review, what do you press to reveal the answer, to say you got it right, to say you got
it wrong, and to stop the session early?

## Options considered

- **A — Enter, then `y` / `n`, and `q` to stop.** Enter reveals the answer; `y` means you
  got it right, `n` means you did not; `q` at either moment ends the session and keeps what
  you have already recorded. Consequence: nothing to learn — Enter is what a person presses
  anyway, and y/n is the most common yes/no in a terminal. Two hands are not needed. Against it:
  `y` and `n` are adjacent to nothing in particular, so it is not the fastest possible for a
  long session, and Enter also means "I pressed something by accident".
- **B — Space to reveal, then `j` / `k`, `q` to stop.** Consequence: fastest for a long
  session, all four keys under the right hand, no reaching. It is the layout Anki users know.
  Against it: `j` and `k` mean nothing unless you already use vim or Anki, and getting them
  the wrong way round costs you a card's schedule with no undo (undoing an answer is out of
  scope on this item).
- **C — Enter to reveal, then `1` for wrong and `2` for right, `q` to stop.** Consequence:
  digits leave room to add grades later without changing the keys you already know. Against it:
  you have told me you do not want a grading scale (`EP-001/Q-003`), so this option is paying
  a daily cost for an option you have already declined.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is the one you can use on the first day without being told what the
  keys are, and this tool has one user who reviews once a day rather than a power user drilling
  through hundreds of cards. If you already review in Anki and want your fingers to keep working
  the same way, say B.

## Answer

[human] Enter to see the answer, y for right, n for wrong, q to stop. That's the one I can just
use without being told the keys.
