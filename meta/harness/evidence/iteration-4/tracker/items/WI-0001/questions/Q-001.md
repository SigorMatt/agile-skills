## Context

**WI-0001 — "add a card and have it persist" — round 1, question 1 of 2.** These two are one
ask, filed separately because each is its own decision; `Q-002` says that is all of them for now.

Your four answers on the epic settled the shape of the product: a command-line tool, one flat
pool of cards, a 1/3/7/30-day ladder, right-or-wrong at review. This item is the first piece of
it — writing a card down and having it still be there tomorrow — and to send it to a builder
every acceptance criterion has to be decidable by someone with a terminal and no context. Two of
them are not yet, and both are about what *you* type, which is why they are yours rather than
mine.

Everything else on this item I have settled myself and written into the record rather than
asking you: the tool is called `recall` (the project is already named that), a card gets a
number when it is added and that same number identifies it afterwards, listing when there are no
cards prints a plain "no cards yet" and exits 0, and card text is a single line of plain text
that survives byte-identical including accents and non-Latin scripts. Those are in

This question is about the moment of adding. It happens every time you write a card down, and it
decides the exact wording of four acceptance criteria — what is typed, what is printed, and what
happens when a side is left empty.

## Question

When you add a card, do you type both sides on the command line in one go, or does the tool ask
you for each side in turn?

## Options considered

- **A — both sides as arguments:** `recall add "die Katze" "the cat"`. Consequence: one line,
  and it works in a loop or pasted from a list, so adding twenty words at once is twenty lines
  you can prepare in advance. Quoting is on you — a card whose text contains a quote mark needs
  escaping, and an empty side is `recall add "" "the cat"`, which is refused with a message
  naming the empty side.
- **B — the tool prompts:** you type `recall add`, it asks "Question side:", you type it, it
  asks "Answer side:", you type it. Consequence: nothing to quote and nothing to remember, and
  it reads better if you are adding one card while thinking about it. But it cannot be scripted
  or pasted in bulk, and every acceptance criterion for this item then has to be checked by
  driving an interactive prompt rather than by running one command and reading its output.
- **C — both:** arguments when you give them, prompts when you do not. Consequence: you get the
  bulk path and the thinking path. Costs two behaviours to specify, two to build and two to
  verify, and it is the only one of the three where "what happens if you give exactly one
  argument" needs its own answer.
- **Recommendation: A.** It matches "I'll be sitting at a terminal" and it is the one that makes
  this item's criteria checkable by running a command and reading its output. C is a small,
  cheap addition later if typing quotes turns out to annoy you; B is the one that is expensive
  to walk back, because it makes every criterion interactive.

## Answer

[human] Both sides on the command line in one go — `recall add "front" "back"`. I'm sitting at a
terminal, I don't need it to ask me twice.
