## Question

The first time you answer a **brand-new** card correctly, should it come back **tomorrow** (one
day), or in **three days**?

## Options considered

- **A — a new card is already on the bottom rung, so the first right answer moves it up to
  three days.** The four intervals are the four places a card can rest, and a new card rests at
  the 1-day one from the moment you add it. Consequence: after successive right answers a card
  goes 3 days → 7 days → 30 days → 30 days, so a right answer always pushes a card at least
  three days out and the 1-day interval is only ever what a *wrong* answer gives you. A card
  reaches monthly after three right answers. The cost: the "one day" you named is never
  something a right answer produces.
- **B — a new card is below the ladder, so the first right answer puts it on the bottom rung,
  one day.** Consequence: the intervals you experience after successive right answers are
  exactly the sequence you wrote — 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 30 — and a card reaches monthly
  after four right answers rather than three. The cost: getting a brand-new card right and
  getting it wrong both bring it back tomorrow, so on that first review your answer changes
  nothing about when you next see it (it does still change what the file records, and every
  later review).
- **Recommendation: B.** It is the reading in which your own enumeration is the thing you
  actually experience, and it follows the same principle by which this ladder has four rungs
  instead of five: take what you wrote literally rather than tidying it. Choose **A** if you
  would rather a card you already know not come back tomorrow just because it is new — that is a
  real preference and it is the reason A exists.

## Answer

[human] B — tomorrow. When I said one day, then three, then a week, then a month, that's the
order I meant to actually see, starting from a new card.

## Consequences

enumeration is the sequence of waits they expect to experience, counted from a new card. So a card
that has never been answered sits **below** the bottom rung; its first right answer puts it on the
1-day rung and it is next due the day after that review, and successive right answers give 1, 3, 7,
30 in that order. A card reaches the top rung after four right answers rather than three. The cost
option B named is accepted, not discovered later: on a brand-new card's first review a right answer
and a wrong answer produce the same rung and the same due date, and only the stored `result` tells
them apart until the review after that.
