## Answer

**Option A. `answer-questions` applies both repairs, and has done so in this execution.**

The record settles it without a new decision. `spec/doc-header.md` §5 says in as many words that
*"`implement` and `verify` do **not** write to `docs/`. If either concludes that a document is
wrong, that is a question (`question.md`), and `answer-questions` makes the edit"*, and gives the
reason: otherwise the authoritative record is updated by the same execution that is trying to
satisfy it. Its `Updated by` table lists `plan` or `answer-questions` for `architecture/adr/*` and
never `implement`; `spec/question.md` §1 puts `implement` in the same row as `verify` and
`review-close`. That is a rule with a rationale, naming this skill as the remedy for exactly the
situation `implement` was in, so it governs.

The rationale is live here rather than notional. D7 and D12 are the two criteria WI-0002 was
rejected on, and the two sentences at issue *are* that failure. `review-close` declined to repair
them itself for the same reason, in its own words: *"a reviewer who writes the fix and then
approves it leaves nobody checking it"*. The symmetric point applies to the implementer.

`implement`'s `SKILL.md` §6a is not overruled, because it answers a different question. Its
three-row table classifies a stale claim — ours to repair, or the stakeholder's to arbitrate — and
both repairs here are its second row, an ordinary repair, which is why no question was owed to the
human and none was filed. What §6a does not do is assign the typist, and read that way the two
documents do not conflict at all.

**The repairs themselves are `review-close`'s, unchanged.** Nothing in its verdict was
re-litigated; I applied it. Two things were forced by the toolkit along the way and are worth
naming:

- `spec/doc-header.md` §4b requires every `## Corrections` entry to have a matching change-log row
  and version bump, and `validate-workspace` enforces the count. So the three ADR-0005 repairs are
  recorded as **two** entries — one `erratum` for the false clause, one `provenance` covering the
  citations — rather than one per sentence, with `ADR-0005` going to `version: 3`.
- Editing the two ADRs pulled them into `lint-claims --changed-since main`, which had been running
  over an empty window (`review.md` Findings 3). It immediately reported four pre-existing
  `claim.unsourced` errors — three in `ADR-0003` `## Context` and one in `ADR-0005` `## Context` —
  which would have blocked `implement`'s `claims-are-sourced` gate on resume. `doc-header.md` §4a
  says the next execution that edits a document is the one that must source what it writes, and
  F-067 is exactly this shape, so they are repaired as `provenance` corrections in the same pass.
  `lint-claims --changed-since main` now exits 0.

**`implement` resumes at `in-progress`** with the third repair already committed (`e4dd5c6`) and
nothing left to write under `docs/`. What remains to it is its own job: the gates on the branch
head, `impl-report.md`, and the move to `verifying`.
