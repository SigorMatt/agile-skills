## Context

This is question 1 of 3 that intake is asking about EP-001, "Pretty-align markdown tables from a
stdin filter", in one round; they are meant to be read together and answered in one sitting.

We have turned your sentence — *"A filter that reads markdown on stdin and pretty-aligns its
tables: pads columns, honours alignment markers, leaves non-table content untouched"* — into an
epic and two work items (WI-0001, the padding and the passthrough; WI-0002, the alignment
markers). Every other question we are asking comes from our list of things we need in order to
build it. That list has a blind spot: it can only contain what we already thought of.

This question exists to make room for what is on your mind and not on our list. It does not
block anything — the pipeline will not wait on it — but it is the cheapest moment in the whole
engagement for an answer to change what gets built, so it is worth more now than later.

## Question

What else matters to you about this tool that we have not asked you about?

Anything counts: a behaviour you assumed we would include, a limit you have in mind, something
you have been bitten by in another tool, a thing you would find annoying, a preference about the
output that you would not have thought worth stating. If nothing comes to mind, "nothing to add"
is a real answer and we will record it as one.

## Options considered

Not a choice between options. This is an open elicitation question (`spec/question.md` §2), and
offering options would defeat its purpose by re-imposing our agenda on it.

## Answer

[human] Three things. Anything that is not a table comes out exactly as it went in, byte for
byte — that is the part I care about most, and I will stop using it the first time it edits a
paragraph. If a table is broken — a row with the wrong number of cells, a missing separator
line — leave it alone rather than guess what I meant; I would much rather it did nothing than
mangled something. And no trailing whitespace at the end of any line it writes: columns are as
wide as the widest cell in them, with no maximum, but nothing hangs off the right-hand edge.

## Consequences

  7 are this answer: a malformed block is copied whole rather than partially reformatted, a line

  and a column's width is its widest cell with no maximum.

## Cross-answer check

Checked against: EP-001/Q-002; EP-001/Q-003.

- `EP-001/Q-002` — compatible: it is about what the tool runs as (a plain script, no build step);
  this answer is about what the tool does to text. Neither constrains the other, and nothing in a
  standard-library Python script makes byte-exact passthrough harder or easier.
- `EP-001/Q-003` — compatible, and mutually reinforcing: Q-003 says *"where it cannot tell, it
  should pass the text through and do nothing"* and this one says *"I would much rather it did
  nothing than mangled something"*. They are the same instruction reached from two directions, and
  ADR-0002 implements them as one rule. One clause needed reading rather than reconciling: *"no
  trailing whitespace at the end of any line it writes"* sits two sentences after *"anything that
  is not a table comes out exactly as it went in, byte for byte"*, and read as covering copied
  lines it would contradict the sentence above it. The colon in the original scopes it to table
  columns, so ADR-0002 decision 6 reads "writes" as "composes" and leaves copied lines alone. That
  is a reading of one answer, not a contest between two, so it is recorded as a decision in the
  ADR with its reversibility rather than put back to the stakeholder.
