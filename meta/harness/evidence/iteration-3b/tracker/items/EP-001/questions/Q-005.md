## Question

Which of your two statements governs a table cell that contains a line break or a `<br>` — the
earlier one (*"every row, every column, no exceptions"*), or the later one (*"markers are for
normal cells, not for those"*)?

## Options considered

The options are your two sentences. We are not offering a third of our own invention.

- **A — The earlier one wins, unchanged.** *"The alignment marker decides everything … every row,
  every column, no exceptions."* Consequence: a cell containing `<br>` is placed by its column's
  marker exactly like any other cell, which is what the tool does today. `ADR-0005` decision 3
  stands as written, nothing is built, and `WI-0003` — the draft item we opened to record the work
  your sign-off reply implied — is dropped. Choose this if we have read too much into a passing
  remark.

- **B — The later one wins, and narrows the earlier one.** *"Markers are for normal cells, not for
  those."* Consequence: cells containing a line break or a `<br>` become an exception to the rule
  you set in `WI-0002/Q-001`; `ADR-0005` decision 3 is superseded by a new ADR that records the
  exception with your authorisation on it; `WI-0003` is refined, built and verified, and the
  engagement comes back to you for a fresh sign-off when it is done. This is what *"fix the
  multiline cells and we are done"* asks for.

- **Recommendation:** none — this is yours to settle. Both sentences are yours and we have no
  standing to prefer one; a recommendation here would be us deciding it with extra steps.

If you choose **B**, do not feel you have to spell out the details now — we will come back with a
short round of questions on exactly what counts (`<br>`, `<br/>`, `<br />`, a trailing backslash,
anything else you write), and on whether such a cell should still be padded out to its column's
width or left as-is. Answering those now is welcome and will save a round trip, but the question
above is the only one that is blocking.

## Answer

[human] You're right, and I over-spoke the first time — the later one wins: a cell with a line
break or a `<br>` in it sits top-left whatever the column marker says, and markers govern
everything else. That is your option B, and yes, treat this as me superseding what I said before.
