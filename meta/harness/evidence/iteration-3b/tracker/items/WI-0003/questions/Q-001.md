## Context

This is question 1 of 2 that refinement is asking about **WI-0003**, *"Leave a cell containing a
line break at the left of its column"*, in this round — the item that exists because of your
sign-off reply. Two questions, one conversation. Everything else this item needed turned out to be
answered already, either by you or by a decision recorded while WI-0001 and WI-0002 were built, and
the full list of what was settled and where is in
`tracker/items/WI-0003/artifacts/refinement-qa.md`.

You told us:

> A cell with a line break or a `<br>` in it should just sit top-left, plain, whatever the column
> marker says; markers are for normal cells, not for those.

[src: EP-001/Q-004]

and, when we asked which of your two statements about markers wins:

> the later one wins: a cell with a line break or a `<br>` in it sits top-left whatever the column
> marker says, and markers govern everything else.

[src: EP-001/Q-005]

That settles *what happens* to such a cell. It does not settle *which cells they are*, and we
cannot work it out from anything on record. A markdown pipe table's row is one physical line, so a
cell can never contain a real newline character — whatever "a line break in a cell" is, it is
something you type that stands for one, and the candidates differ. You named `<br>`. There are at
least four more things people write for the same thing, and a filter that recognises the wrong set
either leaves cells placed by the marker that you wanted left alone, or exempts cells you meant to
be centred.

This is not a question about how the tool is built. It is a question about what you actually write
in your own documents, which is the one thing nobody here can look up.

## Question

Which of these, written inside a table cell, should make that cell sit at the left of its column
instead of being placed by the column's alignment marker?

For reference, these are the forms in question:

```
| Task    | Notes                        |
|:-------:|:----------------------------:|
| deploy  | build<br>test<br>ship        |   <- a plain <br>
| review  | read<br/>comment             |   <- the self-closing spelling
| ship    | tag<br />push                |   <- the self-closing spelling with a space
| audit   | check<BR>sign                |   <- upper case
| release | freeze\                      |   <- a backslash at the end of the cell's text
```

## Options considered

- **A — Only what you named: `<br>`, in any letter case, and its self-closing spellings.** That
  is `<br>`, `<br/>`, `<br />`, `<BR>`, and the same with any amount of space before the slash.
  Consequence: the four `<br>` rows above are exempt; the trailing backslash is not, and a cell
  ending in `\` keeps being placed by its column's marker. This is the narrowest reading of your
  sentence that still covers the way people actually type an HTML break.

- **B — A, plus a trailing backslash at the end of the cell's text.** Consequence: all five rows
  above are exempt. The backslash is markdown's own line-break syntax rather than HTML's, so if
  you write it, it means the same thing to you as `<br>` does. The cost is that a cell that ends
  in a backslash for some *other* reason — a Windows path, an escaped character you meant
  literally — would also stop obeying its marker, silently.

- **C — Only the exact string `<br>`, lower case, nothing else.** Consequence: only the first row
  above is exempt; `<br/>`, `<br />` and `<BR>` are placed by the marker like ordinary text.
  Choose this if you only ever write the bare lower-case form and would rather the tool did
  nothing at all with anything else — it is the most conservative and the most literal reading of
  what you said.

- **D — Something else, or a wider rule.** If what you have in mind is "any HTML tag at all", or
  "any cell longer than N characters", or something we have not thought of, say so in your own
  words and we will write it down as you say it rather than fitting it to one of the above.

- **Recommendation (ours, not yours to inherit):** **A**. Your words were *"a line break or a
  `<br>`"*, and A is the set of things that are unambiguously a `<br>` however they are typed —
  no judgement call, no silent surprises. We put B second and would take it happily if you write
  the backslash form; we mention its one cost above because it is the kind of thing that is
  annoying to discover later. We say this last and mark it as ours because the choice is about
  your documents, not about our code.

## Answer

[human] Any way of typing a `<br>` counts — I don't type it the same way twice, so `<br>`, `<br/>`,
`<br />` and the upper-case one should all behave identically. A backslash on the end of a cell is
not something I write to mean a line break, so leave those cells alone and let the marker place them
as usual. That's your A.
