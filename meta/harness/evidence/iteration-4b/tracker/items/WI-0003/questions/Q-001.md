asks whether a sitting should tell you when you will next see a card. That is all of them for

You told us how the ladder works, and we built the rest of the record on it:

> "Just right or wrong, no rating scale. If I get it right it comes back later each time — a
> day, then three, then a week, then a month or so. If I get it wrong it goes back to the

That gave us four intervals — 1, 3, 7 and 30 days — and they are now written into `ADR-0002`,
into `docs/product/vision.md` and into this item's criteria. One thing your sentence does not
say is what happens **after** the fourth one. You stopped at "a month or so", and a card that

conversation. It is one constant either way and entirely reversible, but it is the difference
between a card you have known for a year turning up twelve times a year and turning up once or
twice — and that is your evening, not ours.

tomorrow whatever you answered, as a placeholder. This item is where the real arithmetic lands,
so answering now costs nothing to change.

## Question

Once a card has reached the longest gap — a month — and you keep getting it right, should it
keep coming back every month for ever, or should the gap carry on growing?

## Options considered

- **A — it stops at a month.** The gaps go 1, 3, 7, 30 days and then stay at 30. A card you have
  known for a year still comes round every month. Consequence: the whole rule is four numbers you
  can hold in your head, and you can work out any card's next date on your fingers. Nothing ever
  disappears from your deck for long. The cost is that a deck you have largely learned settles
  into a permanent monthly floor of cards — with 300 cards you know cold, that is around ten a
  day for ever.
- **B — it keeps growing, by doubling.** After 30 days the next gaps are 60, 120, 240, 480 days
  and so on, with no ceiling. Consequence: cards you genuinely know get out of your way for good,
  and the daily pile shrinks towards only the cards you are actually still learning. The cost is
  that a card can go more than a year without being seen, and if you have in fact lost it in the
  meantime you will not find out until then — and the doubling is a rule you never named, so it
  is us inventing arithmetic on your behalf.
- **C — it keeps growing but stops somewhere you name.** Same as B, but the gap holds once it
  reaches a ceiling you pick — a year, say: 30, 60, 120, 240, 365, 365 … Consequence: the daily
  pile drops a lot without any card ever vanishing from the rotation, and you decide the longest
  gap rather than us. The cost is two more numbers to remember when you work a date out by hand,
  and we would need the ceiling from you.
- **Recommendation:** ours is **A**, and it is a preference rather than a finding. It is the
  only one of the three built entirely out of numbers you gave us, "simple" was your word for
  the whole thing, and it is the option where you can still predict every date without a
  calculator. We would change our minds if your deck is going to get large — B and C are the
  options that keep a big deck's daily load falling — and how large your deck gets is a thing
  only you know. Pick B or C freely; none of the three is more work to build than the others.

## Answer

[human] Stops at a month — option A. The whole point was simple, and I don't want to be doing
math to figure out when a card's coming back. If my deck gets huge later I might change my mind,
but not now.
