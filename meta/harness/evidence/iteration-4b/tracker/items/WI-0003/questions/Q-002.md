## Context

This is refinement for WI-0003, the scheduling rule, round 1, question 2 of 2. The other one
asks what happens above the longest gap. That is all of them for this item.

Right now the scheduling this item builds would be completely invisible to you. A sitting prints
the question, waits, prints the answer, asks `y` or `n`, and moves straight on to the next card.
`recall list` prints `question | answer` and nothing else. So once this item ships, the tool will
be quietly moving your cards along the ladder and the only way you could ever see it happening is
to open the deck file in an editor.

This matters more than a cosmetic choice, for two reasons. The first is that the whole point of
the tool is the scheduling — "it decides when I next see this" is the thing you are buying, and
at the moment nothing ever confirms to you that it did anything. The second is that this item's
fourth criterion, written at intake, says the new date must be applied at the moment you answer
"so it is visible without any further run or command", and nobody has ever decided what
*visible* means. We are not going to decide it for you, because it is the one part of this item
that changes what you actually see on screen.

Two things bound the answer, and we are not proposing to reopen either. You said *"nothing
fancier than that"* (`EP-001/Q-001`), and the epic and the vision both exclude statistics,
streaks and dashboards. So the widest thing on the table below is one extra line per card.

## Question

When you answer a card in a sitting, should the tool tell you when you will next see that card?

## Options considered

- **A — say nothing.** The sitting stays exactly as it is: question, answer, `y`/`n`, next card.
  Consequence: the sitting stays as short and quiet as it is now, and there is nothing extra to
  read on a twenty-five card pile. The cost is that the scheduling is invisible — you would have
  no way of telling a working ladder from a broken one without opening
  `~/.local/share/recall/deck.json`, and getting a card wrong would feel exactly like getting it
  right.
- **B — one line after each answer.** After you answer, the sitting prints something like
  `next in 7 days (2026-09-06)`. Consequence: you see the ladder working, you get immediate
  feedback that a wrong answer costs you the progress you had on that card, and the arithmetic in
  this item becomes something you can check yourself instead of taking on trust. The cost is one
  more line of output per card — on a twenty-five card sitting that is twenty-five extra lines
  scrolling past, though it adds nothing to how long the sitting takes.
- **C — nothing during the sitting, and a way to look it up afterwards.** The sitting stays
  silent as in A, and `recall list` gains the next-review date beside each card, so you can see
  the state of the whole deck when you want to. Consequence: the sitting stays terse and you can
  still audit the schedule; and it answers "what is coming up this week" in a way B never does.
  The cost is that it is more to build than B, it changes what `recall list` prints (which is
  behaviour already delivered and signed off), and the feedback arrives when you are no longer
  thinking about the card.
- **Recommendation:** ours is **B**, and it is a preference, not a finding. It puts the one fact
  you cannot otherwise see in front of you at the moment it is decided, and it costs a single
  line. We are conscious that it is also the option that makes a sitting noisier, and you were
  explicit that a sitting should be quick and that you did not want anything fancy — if the
  quietest possible sitting is what you want, **A** is a perfectly good answer and we will record
  the fact that the schedule is only readable in the deck file. **C** is a real answer too and we
  would build it; it is the one that costs the most.

## Answer

[human] Option B — show the next date after each answer. I want to actually see it's working,
and one line isn't going to slow me down.
