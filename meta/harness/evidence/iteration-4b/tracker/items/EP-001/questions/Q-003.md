## Context

This is intake for EP-001, the flashcard tool, and this is question 3 of 3 filed in this round.
That is all of them for now.

You said "simple spaced repetition". Everyone means something slightly different by that, and
the difference is not cosmetic: it decides how many answers you get to choose between after each
card (two? four? six?), what WI-0003's acceptance criteria say, and whether a reader can work
out by hand when a card is next due. We are not asking you to design an algorithm — we are
asking which of these behaviours you pictured.

This is blocking, because WI-0002 cannot settle how you grade a card without it and WI-0003 is
entirely about it.

## Question

When you answer a card, what should decide when you next see it?

## Options considered

- **A — A doubling ladder.** Two answers: got it, or missed it. Got it moves the card to the
  next rung — 1 day, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 — and missed it drops it back to 1 day. Consequence: the
  simplest thing that is genuinely spaced repetition. A person can predict every date in their
  head, which makes WI-0003's "work it out by hand and the tool agrees" criterion trivially
  checkable. It is coarse: a card you nearly knew and a card you had no idea about are treated
  identically.
- **B — Leitner boxes.** Cards live in five boxes reviewed at fixed frequencies (box 1 daily,
  box 2 every 3 days, and so on). Got it promotes a card one box; missed it sends it back to box
  1. Consequence: the same two answers as A but the schedule is a property of the box rather
  than of the card, which is easier to explain and to show ("you have 12 cards in box 1"). Fixed
  ceiling: a card you have known for a year still comes back every 32 days or whatever box 5 is.
- **C — SM-2, the Anki-style algorithm.** You rate recall on a scale (typically 0-5), and each
  card carries an ease factor that the ratings nudge up or down; the next interval is the last
  one multiplied by that ease. Consequence: what people who use flashcard software daily are
  used to, and it adapts per card. It is materially more work — the ease factor is state that
  WI-0001's storage has to carry, the arithmetic needs its own tests, and "work out the next
  date by hand" stops being something you would do casually.
- **Recommendation:** ours is **A**, on the strength of the word "simple" in what you said, and
  because it keeps WI-0003 checkable by inspection. We would pick C only if you already use
  Anki-style grading and would find anything coarser annoying — which is exactly the sort of
  thing we cannot know.

## Answer

[human] Just right or wrong, no rating scale. If I get it right it comes back later each
time — a day, then three, then a week, then a month or so. If I get it wrong it goes back to
the start.

## Cross-answer check

Checked against: `EP-001/Q-001`; `EP-001/Q-002`.

- `EP-001/Q-001` — **compatible**. Their *"review session that drags on more than a couple
  minutes"* constrains how many cards a sitting holds; this answer constrains when a given card
  returns. A ladder that lengthens intervals reduces the daily count, so the two pull the same
  way. The one place they could have collided — a rule that brought cards back faster over time —
  is not what was chosen.
- `EP-001/Q-002` — **compatible**. A two-way right/wrong grade is trivially expressible at a
  prompt, which is what the command-line choice gives us.
