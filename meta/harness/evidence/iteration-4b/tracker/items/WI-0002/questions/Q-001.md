## Context

This is refinement for WI-0002, the daily review sitting, round 1, question 1 of 2. The other
one asks about brand-new cards. That is all of them for this item.

You named two things that would make this tool a failure. One of them lands squarely here:

> "don't lose my progress — that's the one thing that would make this a failure, along with a
> review session that drags on more than a couple minutes." (`EP-001/Q-001`)

We have not turned the second half of that into an acceptance criterion, and we are not going to
without you, because doing so means inventing a number you never gave. Right now nothing in the
tool bounds how long a sitting is. The sitting shows the cards that are due, and how many that
is on any given day is not under your control — that is the whole idea of spaced repetition. Two
days of ordinary use are short. The day you come back from a week away is not: everything that
came due while you were gone is waiting, and on a deck of a few hundred cards that is easily an
hour rather than a couple of minutes.

One related thing we decided rather than asked, because it bears on this and you should see it
while you answer: **a card whose review date has already passed is still shown.** Missing a day
puts a card in tomorrow's pile rather than dropping it from the schedule — the alternative would
lose progress, which is the other half of the sentence above. If that is wrong, say so here.

This is blocking: WI-0002 cannot be made ready without it, because the answer decides whether the
item has a criterion about sitting length at all, and what that criterion says.

## Question

Should a review sitting put a limit on how many cards it shows you in one go — and if so, what
is the limit?

## Options considered

- **A — No limit.** A sitting shows every card that is due or overdue, however many that is.
  Consequence: nothing is ever held back from you and there is no hidden backlog — what is due
  is what you see. The cost is that the sitting is exactly as long as the pile, so after time
  away it will run well past a couple of minutes and the only way to shorten it is to stop
  part-way yourself (which is safe — the answers you already gave are kept). Your
  couple-of-minutes sentence stays in the record as a constraint we designed towards, but it
  does not become a criterion, because nothing in the tool enforces it.
- **B — A limit on the number of cards, chosen by you.** A sitting shows at most N cards; the
  rest stay due and turn up in the next sitting. Consequence: a sitting has a predictable
  length, and "a sitting never presents more than N cards" is something a person with a terminal
  can check, so your sentence becomes a real criterion. The cost is that a backlog drains over
  several days instead of in one go, and on a heavy day the tool decides for you what waits. We
  would need the number from you — twenty cards is roughly a couple of minutes at a few seconds
  a card, but that is our arithmetic, not your preference.
- **C — A limit on the clock.** The sitting ends after a stated number of minutes, finishing the
  card in your hand. Consequence: it matches your sentence most directly of the three. The cost
  is that a criterion about elapsed time is awkward to check reliably — a verifier has to make
  the tool run long enough to hit the limit — and how many cards you get through varies with how
  fast you read, so two sittings are never comparable.
- **Recommendation:** ours is **B**, with a number you pick. It is the only one of the three
  that turns the sentence you wrote into something anyone can decide by running the tool, and a
  backlog that drains over a few days is still visible to you rather than lost. But we are aware
  that B is us deciding what you do not get to see today, and if you would rather always be shown
  everything that is due, **A** is a perfectly good answer — we will record the couple-of-minutes
  constraint as a note rather than pretend it is a criterion.

## Answer

[human] No limit — A. Show me everything that's due. If it's a big pile after a week away I'll just stop partway, that's fine by me.
