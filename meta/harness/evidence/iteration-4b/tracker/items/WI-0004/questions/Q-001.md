## Context

This is refinement for WI-0004, deleting a card, round 1, question 1 of 2. The other one asks
whether deleting stops to check with you first. That is all of them for this item.

This item exists because of a sentence in your answer to `EP-001/Q-001`: *"I want to be able to
delete a card I don't need anymore; editing can wait."* It was filed as a draft with four
acceptance criteria, and every one of them begins "after deleting a card" without saying how you
say **which** card. That is the one thing the item was left open on, deliberately — the note in
it reads *"How a card is named when deleting it … it is the sort of thing the stakeholder has a
view on, so it is theirs to be asked"* — and it is the reason we cannot write a criterion anyone
could check. `recall delete` has to take something you type, and what that something is decides
what you do every time you use it.

There is a wrinkle in the way, and it is one of your own rules rather than ours. When WI-0001
was refined it was settled that adding the same question twice is allowed and gives you two
cards — the tool does not deduplicate and does not refuse (`WI-0001` AC9, and
`docs/process/using-recall.md`: *"Two cards with the same prompt and different senses is a
normal thing to want in a vocabulary deck"*). So "the card whose question is X" is not always
one card, and each option below has to say what it does about that.

What `recall list` prints today is one line per card, `question | answer`, in the order you
added them, and no numbers or codes. Two of the three options below change that line. You were
asked about changing it once before, in `WI-0003/Q-002` — option C there would have put the
next-review date into `recall list` — and you chose the option that left it alone. We are not
reading that as a standing rule about `recall list`; it was a choice about dates. But it is why
we are naming the change explicitly here rather than slipping it in.

## Question

When you delete a card, how do you tell `recall` which card you mean?

## Options considered

- **A — by its number in the listing.** `recall list` gains a number at the front of each line
  (`3. capital of France | Paris`), and you type `recall delete 3`. Consequence: it always
  identifies exactly one card, including when two cards share a question side, and there is
  nothing to type twice — you read the number off the screen. The costs are two. The numbers
  shift: delete card 3 and what was card 4 becomes card 3, so a number you noted a minute ago
  may now point at a different card. And it changes what `recall list` prints, which is
  behaviour already built and signed off.
- **B — by typing the question side.** `recall delete --question "capital of France"` removes
  the card with that question. If two cards share that question, the delete is **refused**,
  it tells you two matched, and nothing is removed. Consequence: nothing about `recall list`
  changes, you name the card in the words you already have in front of you, and there is no
  number to go stale. The cost is the duplicate case: if you do have two cards with the same
  question and you want one of them gone, B gives you no way to say which — you would have to
  open `~/.local/share/recall/deck.json` in an editor and delete it by hand, which is exactly
  the sort of thing this tool is supposed to save you from.
- **C — by a short code the tool gives each card.** Every card gets a four-character code that
  never changes, stored in the deck file; `recall list` prints it at the front of the line
  (`a3f1  capital of France | Paris`) and you type `recall delete a3f1`. Consequence: it names
  exactly one card, it keeps working with duplicate questions, and unlike A it stays correct
  after other deletions. The costs: it is the most to build of the three; it adds a field to the
  deck file, which is a file you can open and read yourself and which currently holds nothing you
  did not put there; and it also changes what `recall list` prints.
- **Recommendation:** ours is **B**, and it is a preference rather than a finding. It is the only
  one of the three that leaves the tool exactly as it is apart from one new command, and typing
  the question is the closest thing to how you would say it out loud — which reads to us like
  *"nothing fancier than that"*. We want to be straight about the hole in it, though: if your
  vocabulary deck does end up with the same prompt on two cards, B is the only option of the
  three that leaves you unable to remove either one. If that sounds like your deck, **A** and
  **C** are both good answers and neither is much more work.

## Answer

[human] B — let me just type the question, that's the most natural way for me to say which card
I mean. If I ever end up with two cards that share a question, I'll deal with that separately;
it's not worth building for.
