## Context

An independent regression pass over `main` at `6d1e437b4293571296809b322c47fb0dc83d1ad6` — run
*after* EP-001 was closed — found three defects in behaviour WI-0001 delivered, and filed them as
`BUG-0001`, `BUG-0002` and `BUG-0003` at status `ready`.

`verify`'s escalation rule says exactly where such a bug goes: "a new `bug` item at status
`ready` **under the same epic**, with `found-in` naming that item". That is what was done, and
`scripts/new-item` requires `--epic` to name an existing epic, so there was no other place to put
them.

The result is a workspace the validator rejects:

```
$ .claude/agile-skills/scripts/validate-workspace
tracker/items/EP-001/item.md: ERROR [epic.closed-with-open-children] the epic is done but
    BUG-0001, BUG-0002, BUG-0003 are not
    hint: Definition of Done DE1
```

The record does not settle how to clear it, and every route this skill could take on its own is
blocked by a rule:

- Re-opening EP-001 is not a legal transition. `spec/ids-and-statuses.md` §4 lists `open → done`
  for epics and nothing back; `spec/journal-and-history.md` §1 states outright that "a row whose
  `to` is `done` MUST be the last row", so `history.md` cannot even record the reopening.
- Filing the bugs under a different epic would need an epic that does not exist, and epics are
  `intake`'s to create, not `verify`'s.
- Leaving the error unfixed makes `workspace-valid` — a hard gate of this skill — fail for every
  execution in this project until it is resolved.

`verify` may not ask the human and may not guess, so this is filed rather than decided. It is
**non-blocking**: the three bug items are complete and independently workable at `ready`
whichever way this is answered, and none of their content changes.

## Question

When a defect in delivered behaviour is found after its epic has been closed, what carries it —
and what makes `validate-workspace` clean again, given that an epic cannot legally leave `done`?

## Options considered

- **A — allow `done → open` for an epic when a child is filed against it.** Adds a transition
  row to `spec/ids-and-statuses.md` §4 with `verify` as an actor, and requires relaxing
  `journal-and-history.md` §1's "a row whose `to` is `done` MUST be the last row" for epics.
  Consequence: EP-001's closure record stops being final, and `review-close` would have to close
  the epic a second time. It also makes the epic's `outcome: delivered` temporarily untrue,
  which is the thing DE3 exists to keep honest.
- **B — bugs found after an epic closes go under a new follow-up epic** (say EP-002,
  "linecount defects found after EP-001 closed"), keeping `found-in` pointing at WI-0001 so the
  provenance survives. Consequence: `intake` has to create that epic before `verify` can file
  anything, so a QA pass can be stopped by a missing epic — but EP-001's closure record stays
  intact and DE1 keeps meaning what it says.
- **C — relax `epic.closed-with-open-children` so children of type `bug` created after the
  epic's closing timestamp do not count.** One rule in
  `.claude/agile-skills/scripts/validate-workspace` (`check_epics`). Consequence: the board shows
  open bugs under a done epic, which is arguably the truth — an epic is a goal that was met, and
  a later defect does not un-meet it — but DE1's "every child item is `done`" then has a silent
  exception that a reader of the spec would not expect.
- **Recommendation:** none, insufficient basis. The choice is between three methodology
  documents, not between three implementations, and it changes what "done" means for every epic
  in every project using this pipeline. That is above what `verify` may decide from this
  project's record.

## Answer

**Option A. The bugs stay under EP-001, and EP-001 returns to `open`.** The record now settles
this, so no new decision was taken here — it was read out of three files, each checked directly
rather than taken on trust:

- `spec/ids-and-statuses.md` **§3.4, "A closed epic can be reopened"**: "a `done` epic MAY return
  to `open`, **by any skill**, when a child item is filed against it after it closed. The
  transition is recorded like any other, and the reason names the item that caused it. When that
  item closes, `review-close` applies the epic Definition of Done again and may close the epic
  again."
- `pipeline.yaml`, transition list, line 176: `from: done → to: open`, `actor: any`,
  `resume_to: forbidden`, condition "Epic only. A defect was filed against the epic's delivered
  behaviour after it closed, so the goal is no longer met. The reopening row records which item
  reopened it."
- `scripts/validate-workspace`, `check_history`: the `history.after-done` error is raised unless
  `expected_type == "epic" and to_status == "open"` — exactly one row may follow `done`, and only
  this one. `spec/journal-and-history.md` §1 has been amended to match: "A row whose `to` is
  `done` MUST be the last row **for a `work-item` or a `bug`** … an epic may be reopened."

§3.4 also answers, in the methodology's own words, the two objections this question raised
against option A:

- *"EP-001's closure record stops being final."* It does not: both rows stay in `history.md`, and
  the closure remains true of the moment it was made. What changed is the world, not the record —
  three defects were found in the behaviour the epic delivered, so its goal is no longer met.
  §3.4's reasoning is that the alternative "produces a lie … A methodology that makes 'do not
  record the defect' the path of least resistance has chosen the wrong invariant."
- *"`review-close` would have to close the epic a second time."* That is the intended flow, stated
  in §3.4: when the last reopening item closes, `review-close` applies the epic Definition of Done
  again. DE3 will therefore be re-evaluated against the success measures on the merged trunk, not
  inherited from the first closure.

Options B and C are rejected for the reasons §3.4 gives. B (a follow-up epic) breaks the link
between a defect and the goal it violates, and makes a QA pass depend on `intake` creating an epic
first. C (relaxing the validator) leaves DE1 with a silent exception that a reader of the spec
would not expect, and hides open bugs under a `done` epic on the board.

One thing §3.4 does not decide is what happens to the epic's recorded `outcome` while it is
reopened. I first decided to leave `outcome: delivered` in place, on the grounds that the epic
*was* delivered and the reopening row already marks the claim provisional. **The validator
refused it**, and the validator is right:

```
tracker/items/EP-001/item.md: ERROR [item.outcome.premature] outcome is set to 'delivered'
    but the item is not done
```

So the field is **cleared** while EP-001 is open, and `review-close` sets it again when it closes
the epic the second time. The rule the validator enforces is the better one: an `outcome` is a
statement about a closed item, and an open item that still carries one would tell every reader —
and the board — that a goal with three open defects under it had been delivered. What preserves
the history is `history.md`, which keeps the `open → done` row and the `done → open` row that
follows it, not a stale field in the frontmatter.

This correction is recorded rather than tidied away: the first attempt is in this project's
journal, in the entry that made it and in the one that corrected it.

**Not escalated to the human.** None of the four conditions in `spec/question.md` §4 applies once
§3.4 exists: the intent is recorded in the methodology's own spec, the reopening is reversible
(the epic closes again when the bugs are done), it contradicts no ADR or product document — it is
now *required* by the spec — and it is not a coin flip. `verify` was right to file rather than
guess: when the question was written, the record genuinely did not settle it, and the answer
changed a methodology document rather than this project's code.

## Consequences

- `tracker/items/EP-001/item.md` — `status: done` → `status: open`, written by
  `scripts/transition`; and `outcome: delivered` **removed**, after `validate-workspace` rejected
  it with `item.outcome.premature` (see the answer above). `review-close` restores it when the
  epic closes again.
- `tracker/items/EP-001/history.md` — one row appended by `scripts/transition`:
  `done → open`, actor `answer-questions`, reason naming BUG-0001, BUG-0002 and BUG-0003 as the
  items that reopened it. This is the row `spec/journal-and-history.md` §1 now permits, and the
  only one that may follow `done`.
- `tracker/items/EP-001/journal.md` — the `answer-questions` entry for this execution, with the
  route the answer came from and the gate evidence.
- `tracker/items/EP-001/questions/Q-001.md` — this file: `status: answered`, `answered-by:
  answer-questions`, with the answer and its basis.
- `tracker/board.md` — regenerated: EP-001 shows as `open`, and the question leaves the open list.
