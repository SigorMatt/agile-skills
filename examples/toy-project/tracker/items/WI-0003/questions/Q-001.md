Where the record stops short: **I may not make this edit.** `spec/doc-header.md` §5 allocates

## Question

Should `answer-questions` update `docs/product/vision.md` to record `--sort` as delivered — so
that D7 and DE4 can pass — or should the item and the epic close with the vision left saying the
flag is not delivered?

## Options considered

- **A — `answer-questions` updates `vision.md` to v3**, changing the `--sort` bullet's
  parenthetical from "(being added, WI-0003; not delivered at the time of writing)" to record it
  as delivered, with a change-log row per `spec/doc-header.md` §3. Nothing else in the document
  needs to change: the bullet's description of what the flag does was written from the refined
  criteria and matches what shipped. Consequence: D7 and DE4 pass on their own terms; the edit is
  made by a skill the doc-header table names as an updater; `review-close` resumes at `in-review`
  and closes the item and the epic. Cost: one `answer-questions` cycle.
- **B — `review-close` makes the edit itself** as part of closing. Consequence: no extra cycle,
  but the document I certify under D7 and DE4 is edited by the same execution that certifies it,
  which is the circularity `spec/doc-header.md` §5 allocates away, and `vision.md` is not in this
  skill's contract outputs. It would also be the second time in this epic that a rule about who
  writes what was bent for convenience in the same document.
- **C — close the item and the epic with D7 and DE4 recorded as unmet**, leaving the vision
  saying `--sort` is not delivered. Consequence: honest at the moment of closing, and permitted —
  `spec/dor-dod.md` §4 allows closing an epic with an unmet measure as long as it is stated. But
  it leaves the product document describing a shipped feature as pending, and nobody re-reads a
  closed epic's review record. This is precisely the shape of the gap the human objected to last
  time.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is the only option that both fixes the document and keeps the edit
  with a skill entitled to make it. The answer is fully determined by the record — the branch,
  `impl-report.md` and `verify-report.md` all show the flag was built and verified — so this does
  not need escalating to the human under any of `spec/question.md` §4's four conditions: no intent
  is missing, nothing is irreversible, no ADR or product doc is contradicted (the update makes the
  doc *agree* with what shipped), and the record is not silent.

item resumes at `in-review` so `review-close` can merge and close it. No new decision was needed
and no ADR was written: this is a **fact** the record already establishes, not a choice.

So the flag exists and does what the vision's bullet already says it does. That bullet's

`answer-questions` propagates an answer, it does not take the opportunity to rewrite a product
document.

**On the two options not taken.** Option B (the reviewer edits it) was correctly rejected by the
asker: `spec/doc-header.md` §5 keeps `review-close` out of `product/vision.md` precisely so that a
reviewer cannot edit a document and then certify D7 and DE4 against their own edit. Option C
(close with D7 and DE4 unmet) is permitted by `spec/dor-dod.md` §4 but would leave the product
document describing a shipped feature as pending, which is the gap the human objected to when
EP-001 last closed.

**One ordering caveat, stated rather than hidden.** This edit is written *before* the merge —
`review-close` resumes at `in-review` and performs the merge as its next step. The word "delivered"
is therefore true within the same execution that follows this one, not at the instant it was
written. If `review-close` were to reject the change instead of merging it, this consequence must
be reverted along with the item's status; nothing else in v3 would need to change. That risk is
small and named: the reviewer had already completed every other Definition of Done criterion and
recorded D7 as the only one outstanding.

**Not escalated to the human**, and none of `spec/question.md` §4's four conditions applies: no
intent is missing (he asked for this vision update in as many words at intake — "I'd rather the
vision described what the tool actually does"), nothing here is irreversible (a documentation
version bump), no ADR or product doc is contradicted — the edit makes the document *agree* with
what shipped — and the record is not silent, as the three citations above show.
