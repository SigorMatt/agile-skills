## Context

Implementing plan step 5, I built the fixture AC10 describes and could not: **the folder it names
cannot exist.**

AC10 reads:

> the count column is as wide as the widest number actually printed, the total included, so the
> total row lines up with the file rows even when it is the wider number. For a folder of 27
> files summing to 1204 lines whose two largest hold 9 and 7 lines, `--top 2` prints
> `···9··big.txt`, `···7··next.txt`, `1204··total (all 27 files)`, where `·` stands for one space
> character

If the two largest files hold 9 and 7 lines, then no file holds more than 9, so 27 files sum to
at most `27 × 9 = 243`. They cannot sum to 1204. The two halves of the example — the file counts
and the total — contradict each other.

The **rule** in the first sentence is unambiguous and is what I implemented: `format_report`
computes `width` over every count printed *and* the total, so the total row and the file rows
share one column whichever is wider. Refinement Q6 records the human agreeing to exactly that
sentence ("size the column to the widest number actually printed, total included, so everything
lines up"), and the numbers came in as an illustration of it.

Note that the criterion's expected **output** is perfectly producible — it is only the folder that
is not. `format_report([(9, "big.txt"), (7, "next.txt")], 1204, "total (all 27 files)")` returns
those three lines exactly, and `TopFormatTest.test_ac10_width_covers_an_explicit_total` asserts
them byte for byte. What cannot be built is a directory that would make the tool print them.

I have not edited AC10 — that is not mine to do — and I am not blocked, which is why this
question is filed `blocking: false`. What I have done is split the end-to-end evidence in two,
and both tests pass:

- `TopTest.test_ac10_column_width_includes_the_total` — 27 files summing to 1204 (26 of 46 lines
  and one of 8), `--top 2` → `  46  f00.txt` / `  46  f01.txt` / `1204  total (all 27 files)`:
  the total is the wider number and the rows are padded to it, which is the point of the rule.
- `TopTest.test_ac10_two_largest_are_nine_and_seven` — 27 files whose two largest hold 9 and 7
  (the rest 1 line each), `--top 2` → ` 9  big.txt` / ` 7  next.txt` / `41  total (all 27 files)`:
  the criterion's stated file counts, with the total they actually produce.

`verify` will build this fixture next and hit the same wall, which is the reason to settle it now
rather than to leave a note in the implementation report.

## Question

AC10's worked example describes a folder that cannot exist (27 files whose largest holds 9 lines
cannot sum to 1204). How should the criterion be corrected, so that `verify` has a fixture it can
actually build and the human's rule is preserved?

## Options considered

- **A — Correct the example's numbers, keeping the rule.** Replace the illustration with a
  realizable one, e.g. "for a folder of 27 files summing to 1204 lines whose two largest hold 46
  lines each, `--top 2` prints `··46··f00.txt`, `··46··f01.txt`, `1204··total (all 27 files)`".
  Consequence: AC10 becomes checkable end to end exactly as written; the rule, the label and the
  alignment are untouched; the existing test
  `TopTest.test_ac10_column_width_includes_the_total` already is that fixture. The human's own
  1204 and 27 survive; only the "9 and 7" go.
- **B — Keep the numbers, and state that the example is checked at the renderer.** Leave AC10's
  text as it is and add a sentence saying the three expected lines are demonstrated by calling
  `format_report` with that total and label, since no directory produces them. Consequence: the
  criterion stays in the human's words, but its end-to-end half is permanently uncheckable, and
  every future reader has to rediscover why.
- **C — Split AC10 into two criteria**: the alignment rule with a realizable folder, and the
  renderer's exact output for the stated three lines. Consequence: the most precise, and the most
  bureaucratic — it turns one criterion the human recognises into two he never saw, for a defect
  that is arithmetic in an illustration.
- **Recommendation: A.** It is the smallest edit that makes the criterion decidable by observation
  (DoR R4), it preserves the human's rule, his `1204` and his `27`, and the test that demonstrates
  it already exists and passes. If A is chosen, the two `test_ac10_*` tests can both stay — the
  second one becomes extra coverage rather than a workaround, and the note in its docstring
  pointing at this question should be updated to point at the corrected criterion.

## Answer

**Option A: the example's numbers are corrected, the rule is untouched.** AC10 now reads, for its
second sentence:

> For a folder of 27 files summing to 1204 lines — `f00.txt` … `f25.txt` of 46 lines each and
> `small.txt` of 8 — `--top 2` prints `··46··f00.txt`, `··46··f01.txt`,
> `1204··total (all 27 files)`, where `·` stands for one space character.

The basis is recorded intent, not my preference. Refinement Q6 put the rule to the human in these
words — "size the column to the widest number actually printed, total included, so everything
lines up" — and he confirmed it verbatim. That sentence is what AC10 exists to state, and it is
unchanged. What changed is an illustration that contradicted itself: the analyst wrote the file
counts and the total independently, and no arithmetic check was made when the criterion was
drafted at refinement.

Two things this answer deliberately does **not** do:

- It does not weaken the criterion. The corrected folder still shows a total wider than every row
  printed (four digits against two), which is the only property the rule is about, and `--top 2`
  still selects by the order AC2 fixes — the two 46-line files that come first by filename.
- It does not reshape the criterion around what was built. The delivered code was written against
  the rule, not against the example, and the *original* expected output is still produced exactly
  — `format_report([(9, "big.txt"), (7, "next.txt")], 1204, "total (all 27 files)")` returns those
  three lines, asserted in `TopFormatTest.test_ac10_width_covers_an_explicit_total`. The
  correction changes which **folder** demonstrates the rule, not what the tool prints.

Option B was rejected because it leaves a criterion that a verifier cannot check end to end and
that every later reader has to re-derive as impossible. Option C was rejected as disproportionate:
it turns one criterion the human recognises into two he never saw, over an arithmetic slip in an
illustration.

**Not escalated to the human.** None of the four conditions in `spec/question.md` §4 applies: the
intent is recorded verbatim (Q6), the change is reversible in one paragraph, it contradicts no ADR
or product document, and it is not a coin flip — the rule already decides the behaviour, and only
the illustration was in question. Escalating would have spent his attention on correcting his own
example's arithmetic.

`implement` was right to file this rather than to pick a fixture silently, and right to file it
non-blocking: nothing about the delivered behaviour turned on the answer.
